## Overview

This podcast episode from patent attorney Dr. Adam Diament walks inventors through what happens after filing a patent application with the USPTO. After filing electronically, an inventor immediately receives an application number and confirmation code, then an official filing receipt a few weeks later. The actual examination process typically takes an average of 16 months, though responses can come as quickly as 3 months or as long as 30+ months depending on the technology field. After 18 months, the application becomes public regardless of whether a patent has been issued.

In the ideal scenario, a patent examiner reviews the application and issues a Notice of Allowance, after which the inventor pays an issue fee and the patent publishes. However, roughly 90% of applications receive at least one rejection — called an Office Action — for reasons such as obviousness, lack of novelty, insufficient description, or improper claim structure. Inventors have 3 months (or 6 months with a surcharge) to respond by arguing against the rejection or amending claims.

If the examiner maintains a rejection with a Final Office Action, the process is far from truly "final." Inventors can file a Request for Continued Examination (RCE) for an additional fee, or appeal to the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB), where three patent judges review the case. Further appeals can go to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit or even the U.S. Supreme Court (extremely rare). The episode also explains the difference between patent agents and patent attorneys, and notes that patents require maintenance fee payments at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after grant.

## Key Takeaways

- After filing, you get an application number immediately; the USPTO assigns a patent examiner who typically responds within 16 months on average
- About 90% of patent applications receive at least one rejection (Office Action); inventors have 3–6 months to respond
- "Final Office Action" is misleading — you can still file an RCE, appeal to PTAB, or appeal to federal courts
- Patent agents and patent attorneys both passed the patent bar exam; only attorneys can also handle contracts, licensing, and litigation
- Patents require maintenance fees at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years from the grant date to stay in force
- Expedited examination (Track One) is available for additional fees, aiming for a 12-month response
- The USPTO's examination procedure is governed by the 3,000+ page Manual of Patent Examining Procedure (MPEP)

## Best For

Inventors who have just filed or are preparing to file a patent application and want a clear, practical roadmap of the examination process from filing through potential appeals.
