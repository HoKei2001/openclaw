## Overview

This official USPTO information session is designed to help prospective applicants understand the patent examiner role in depth. Led by Technology Center Group Director John Fristoe and HR Division Chief Michelle Martin, the webinar covers the USPTO mission, what patent examiners do day-to-day, the patent review process, career progression, compensation and benefits, and how to apply.

Patent examiners are engineers and scientists who work directly with inventors to evaluate patent applications, conduct prior art searches, and make legal determinations about whether an invention deserves patent protection. The role sits at a distinctive intersection: examiners must help inventors get the correct scope of protection (not too broad, not too narrow) while simultaneously protecting the public's interest in ensuring that overbroad claims do not restrict legitimate innovation. Fristoe describes this using a property metaphor — the claims define the fence around the intellectual property, and an examiner's job is to help put the fence in the right place. After initial examination, the applicant can amend claims and argue rejections, creating a collaborative back-and-forth process.

Career progression is non-competitive from GS-7 through GS-13 — examiners are promoted based on meeting production and quality standards rather than waiting for a vacancy to open. The training program starts with two months of formal coursework, followed by a structured mentorship under experienced examiners with gradually increasing independence. Benefits include competitive salaries at a special pay rate above the standard GS scale, flexible work hours, law school and graduate school tuition reimbursement, a 401(k) retirement plan, and access to campus facilities in Alexandria, Virginia. A notable trivia fact: USPTO employees are the only people in the United States prohibited from filing patent applications, since their access to unpublished applications and technology trends would give them an unfair advantage.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent examiners are engineers and scientists who evaluate patent applications, conduct prior art searches, and determine whether inventions meet the legal requirements for patent protection.
- The examiner's role balances helping inventors get appropriate protection with protecting the public from overly broad patents that restrict legitimate innovation.
- Career advancement from GS-7 to GS-13 is non-competitive — based entirely on meeting individual production and quality standards, not on waiting for open positions.
- Initial training includes two months of formal instruction followed by mentorship under experienced examiners with progressively greater independence.
- Compensation is above the standard GS scale at a special technical pay rate; additional benefits include tuition reimbursement, flexible hours, retirement plan, and campus amenities.
- USPTO employees are the only people in the US who cannot file patent applications, due to their privileged access to unpublished applications and technology direction.
- Applications are submitted through USAJOBS, with multiple cut-off dates; qualified candidates receive HireVue first-round interview invitations after the cut-off date passes.

## Best For

Engineers, scientists, and STEM graduates who are considering a career as a USPTO patent examiner and want a comprehensive overview of the role, the day-to-day work, career progression, and the application process.
