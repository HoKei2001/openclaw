# How to Pick The Right Patent Attorney

## Overview

This Patents Demystified video features an interview with a patent attorney who specializes in working with startups and early-stage companies. The discussion provides practical guidance on what to look for when choosing a patent attorney, framed around two key criteria: technical background match and client-type fit. The attorney has a background in biochemistry, molecular biology, and electrical engineering, and focuses primarily on computer hardware, software, and robotics patents.

A central insight from the interview is that building a patent portfolio for a startup is fundamentally different from managing patent assets for a Fortune 500 company. Large corporations operate at scale with thousands of patents, where any individual filing is relatively low-stakes. For startups and individual inventors, patent number one or five is critical — it may define the company's negotiating position with investors or partners, and the stakes are high. This means the attorney needs not only the right technical background but also an understanding of startup budget constraints, business cycles, and how to leverage a small patent portfolio strategically.

The interview also addresses value creation: a good patent attorney for a startup should help clients understand how to use patent assets as business tools — for projecting value, attracting investment, or creating negotiating chips — not just execute administrative filing tasks. The attorney expresses that if a client's patent investment does not generate a 10x to 100x return in business value, the attorney has not fully done their job.

## Key Takeaways

- Choose a patent attorney whose technical background aligns with your technology field.
- The attorney's experience with your type of client (startup vs. large corporation) matters as much as their technical expertise.
- Building a patent portfolio for a startup requires different strategy than managing large-scale corporate patent assets.
- A good patent attorney for startups explains how patents serve business objectives, not just how to file them.
- Patents must create business value — they should function as negotiating chips, investment signals, or competitive barriers.
- To be a patent attorney, candidates must pass a Patent Bar exam requiring a technical/engineering background.
- Attorneys with interdisciplinary technical backgrounds (e.g., biology + electrical engineering) can handle a broader range of technologies.

## Best For

This video is most useful for startup founders and early-stage entrepreneurs who are selecting their first patent attorney and want to understand what expertise and client-orientation to look for.
