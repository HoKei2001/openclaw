## Overview

This podcast episode covers the details of paying US patent maintenance fees — the periodic fees required to keep an issued patent in force. The presenter explains the fee schedule, timing, and key details that patent holders need to understand to maintain their patent rights over its full 20-year term.

## Key Takeaways

- A US utility patent generally lasts 20 years from the filing date, but maintenance fees must be paid to keep it in force throughout that term.
- Maintenance fees are due at three intervals after the grant date: 3.5 years, 7.5 years, and 11.5 years.
- Failing to pay maintenance fees on time can result in the patent lapsing, which ends the patent holder's exclusive rights.
- The 20-year patent term runs from the filing date, while maintenance fee due dates are calculated from the grant date — these are distinct reference points.

## Topics Covered

- US patent maintenance fee schedule (3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years from grant)
- Distinction between 20-year patent term (from filing) and maintenance fee timing (from grant)
- Consequences of failing to pay maintenance fees
- Overview of the patent maintenance obligations for patent holders

## Best For

Inventors and patent holders who have recently received a patent grant and need to understand their ongoing financial obligations to keep the patent in force, as well as entrepreneurs planning long-term IP budget management.
