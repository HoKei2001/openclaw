## Overview

In this Q&A-style video, The Patent Professor answers a viewer's question about software patent eligibility and timelines. The professor addresses the common misconception that patents are only for physical products, noting that many inventors only realize software can be patented after a competitor has already launched a similar product — at which point it may be too late. Software is absolutely patentable, but it must be handled in a specific way to satisfy USPTO requirements.

The honest message on timelines is sobering: software patent applications are processed by one of the slowest technology art units at the USPTO, and inventors should expect approximately three years from filing to grant. Despite the long wait, the video emphasizes a critical upside — once you file a patent application, you are protected and no longer need to keep the invention confidential. That means inventors can immediately begin marketing, pursuing sales, approaching investors, and meeting with venture capitalists after filing, without any risk of losing patent rights through public disclosure.

The professor also mentions providing a free software patents guide (written in plain English for non-attorneys) through their office and highlights the importance of timing — understanding that filing early is essential for U.S. patent rights.

## Key Takeaways

- Software is absolutely patentable, but must be structured correctly in the patent application
- Software patent applications are among the slowest to be examined at the USPTO — expect approximately 3 years to grant
- Once you file, you no longer need to keep the invention confidential and can freely market, sell, and seek investment
- Many inventors mistakenly believe patents are only for physical products — a costly misunderstanding
- Filing early is critical; competitors filing first in the U.S. first-to-file system can block your rights
- A well-written software patent guide (in plain English) can help inventors understand the process before working with an attorney
- "Patent pending" status begins the moment you file and offers meaningful protection during examination
