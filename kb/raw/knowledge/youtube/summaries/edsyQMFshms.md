## Overview

Patent professional Edel Patents presents five practical tips for conducting more effective prior art searches in Google Patents, using a mechanical pencil with a lead-protection spring mechanism as the working example. The central insight is that choosing search terms is the most critical — and most error-prone — part of any patent search, and that the wrong terms will systematically exclude the best references from results.

The five tips focus on: (1) brainstorming search terms methodically by asking what makes the invention different, what its simplest version is, and which details are most important; (2) always asking whether adding a term will exclude the best reference, not just irrelevant results; (3) using minus-sign exclusion to remove batches of irrelevant documents while being careful not to accidentally exclude relevant art; (4) scanning efficiently by using Google's context-highlighted search terms to quickly evaluate relevance without reading full documents; and (5) leveraging the "similar results" button when a good reference is found, to let Google find related documents automatically. The video also demonstrates how to create a custom broadened search after exhausting a narrow search area by using minus signs to exclude already-searched territory.

A memorable framing repeated throughout: "Don't throw out the baby with the bathwater" — the search goal is to find the most relevant reference, and every constraint added to a search risks excluding exactly that reference.

## Key Takeaways

- The most descriptive terms are often the worst search terms — they may exclude the best reference
- For every search term, ask: "Do the documents I'm looking for have this term?" before adding it
- Use minus-sign exclusion to remove irrelevant result batches, but choose the exclusion term carefully
- Efficient scanning: check title first, then use Google's bold-highlighted context snippets around keywords
- Use images to quickly eliminate irrelevant references without reading
- When a good reference is found, use the "similar results" button to find related documents automatically
- After exhausting a narrow search, broaden by adding a minus sign before the narrow term to explore new territory
- Brainstorm terms by asking: what makes it different? What's the simplest version? Which details matter most?
