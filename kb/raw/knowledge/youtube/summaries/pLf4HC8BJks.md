## Overview

This inventRightTV presentation by inventor and licensing expert Stephen Key introduces the concept of a "transaction ready" provisional patent application — one that serves not just as a legal placeholder but as a persuasive tool for overcoming objections from investors, licensees, and patent examiners. The core argument is that most provisional patent applications lack the depth needed to be commercially valuable, and inventors must take specific steps to change that.

A transaction ready provisional overcomes three future arguments. First, when presenting to a potential licensee or investor, it answers the question "why should we pay you?" by clearly demonstrating novelty and a defensible point of difference. Second, it increases the chances of a patent being issued when the non-provisional application is filed, because the examiner's job is to find reasons to reject — and a thorough application anticipates those rejections. Third, it helps deter copycats and infringers by establishing a broad, well-documented scope of protection.

The specific content elements that make a provisional transaction ready are: a clear description of the problem that anyone can understand; a clear description of the solution and the inventor's specific point of difference compared to existing products (determined through Google image and shopping searches and prior patent review); manufacturing and materials information — this is the most commonly missing element and one that investors and licensees care deeply about; workarounds and variations, which means stealing the idea from yourself by imagining how a competitor might design around your invention and including those alternatives; and detailed line drawings with labeled components. Stephen emphasizes that by including all these elements, an inventor creates a document that reduces risk, builds confidence, and tells a compelling story that people can act on.

## Key Takeaways

- A "transaction ready" provisional patent application functions both as legal protection and as a persuasive selling tool for investors and licensees.
- Conduct Google image and shopping searches before writing to identify your point of difference compared to existing marketplace products.
- Clearly describe the problem your invention solves in plain language that any reader can understand.
- Include manufacturing processes and materials information — this is what most patents lack and what gives the application real commercial value.
- Describe workarounds and variations ("steal it from yourself") to protect not just the invention but the broader innovation space.
- Use broad, functional language for features (e.g., "any mechanical closing device" rather than "velcro") to maximize protection scope.
- Line drawings with labeled components are essential — they communicate what words alone cannot and cover variations that may not be fully described in text.

## Best For

Inventors who plan to license their product and want to write a provisional patent application that will impress potential licensees and investors while also providing genuinely broad patent protection.
