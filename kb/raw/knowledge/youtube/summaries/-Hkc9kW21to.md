## Overview

This video from Bold Patents Law Firm (presented by J.D. Houvener) covers the six core principles of patent claim drafting, spanning USPTO rules, formatting requirements, and the four main claim types: apparatus, process, composition of matter, and design patent claims. The video is structured with timestamps and is supplemented by a detailed blog article. It provides a thorough reference for anyone wanting to understand how patent claims are structured and what makes them legally effective.

## Key Takeaways

- Claims are located at the end of the patent specification and each must be a single sentence; claims start with "I claim" or "We claim."
- The preamble sets up the claim using "comprising" (open, allowing additional elements) or "including" (more closed/limiting).
- Antecedent basis is critical: every term introduced for the first time must be properly referenced; using "the" before introducing a term is an error the examiner will reject.
- Apparatus claims describe physical devices or structures; they must have support in the written specification for every element claimed.
- Process claims use verbs (creating, storing, crafting) and the sequence of steps can be locked in or left flexible — locking in order narrows scope and makes infringement harder to prove.
- Composition of matter claims (e.g., pharmaceuticals, alloys) focus on proportions and molecular composition rather than structure or process order.
- Design patent claims are unique: there is only one claim, and it consists of standard language pointing to the ornamental drawings — the drawing itself is the claim.
- Multiple independent claim sets can coexist (e.g., an apparatus claim and a method of construction claim for the same invention).
