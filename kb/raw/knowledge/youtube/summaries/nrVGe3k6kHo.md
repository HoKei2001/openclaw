## Overview

In this inventRightTV interview, host Stephen Key and patent agent Kevin Prince discuss two powerful strategies for inventors: using the USPTO's Track One (fast-track) program to accelerate patent examination, and combining utility and design patents for stronger IP protection. Kevin Prince, who has been a patent agent for 24 years with Quick Patents, explains that Track One allows approximately 15,000 applications per year to receive examination within 12 months instead of the typical 18–36 month backlog, for an additional fee of about $1,400–$1,500 for small/micro entities. A recent application Kevin filed for Stephen's packaging innovation received examination in only 5 months.

The discussion shifts to what Kevin calls the "one-two punch" IP strategy: filing both a utility patent and a design patent on the same invention. Kevin notes that design patents have a 95% allowance rate (compared to roughly 95% rejection rate for utility patents on first office action), are less expensive, process faster (typically 12–18 months, or as fast as 6 weeks via the "rocket docket" program for an extra ~$1,000), and can be used to say "multiple patents pending." Design patent drawings use broken lines strategically to claim only the distinctive visual elements, such as shape, without claiming optional features like logos. Kevin also shares that design patents are particularly effective against Amazon copycats because the visual similarity is obvious to the platform without needing to analyze claims.

For utility patents, Kevin advises inventors to focus on the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) when providing information — the simplest version of the product without which the core invention wouldn't be recognizable. He emphasizes that patents don't license you to make something — they stop others from making it — so claiming the MVP creates the broadest barrier for competitors.

## Key Takeaways

- Track One (USPTO fast-track) costs ~$1,400–$1,500 extra and aims to deliver examination within 12 months (sometimes 5 months)
- Design patents have a ~95% allowance rate vs. ~95% rejection rate for utility patents on first office action
- The "one-two punch" strategy: file both a utility and design patent to maximize protection
- Design patent "rocket docket" can result in allowance in as little as 6 weeks for ~$1,000 additional fee
- For utility patents, describe the MVP (minimum viable product) — the simplest core version — to get the broadest claim
- Design patent broken-line drawings allow disclaiming optional elements (like logos or variable lengths) to broaden protection
- Design patents are effective enforcement tools against online marketplace copycats

## Best For

Inventors and product licensors who want to protect their innovations both quickly and comprehensively using a combination of utility and design patent strategies.
