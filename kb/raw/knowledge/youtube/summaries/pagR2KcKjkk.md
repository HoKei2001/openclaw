# 3 Biggest Mistakes to Avoid When Filing a Patent

## Overview

UIPatent Inc. presents a concise overview of the three most consequential mistakes inventors make when filing a patent application. The video is structured for maximum clarity, devoting focused attention to each mistake and its solution. The framing is practical and action-oriented: each mistake is a discrete decision point where the right action prevents costly outcomes.

The three mistakes are: (1) publicly disclosing an invention before filing — sharing at a conference, posting online, or discussing with investors without patent protection allows others to file first and can permanently destroy the inventor's ability to patent; the solution is to file a provisional patent before sharing any details; (2) filing a vague or weak patent application — broad or unclear claims make it difficult for examiners to approve the application, and lack of detail leads to rejection; the solution is a well-structured application with strong, specific claims; and (3) skipping the prior art search — failing to search before filing leads to applications rejected for lacking originality, as well as potential financial losses from infringement of existing patents; the solution is a thorough prior art search before applying.

## Key Takeaways

- Filing a provisional patent application before sharing any details protects against disclosure-based loss of patent rights
- Disclosing an invention at a conference, online, or to investors without filing allows others to file first
- Patent applications with vague or broad claims are difficult for examiners to approve and are commonly rejected
- A well-structured application with strong, specific claims significantly improves approval chances
- Skipping the prior art search risks both rejection for lack of originality and financial losses from infringing existing patents
- A thorough patent search before filing prevents unnecessary costs and rejection
- These three mistakes — premature disclosure, weak claims, and no prior art search — are the root causes of most avoidable patent rejections
