## Overview

Startup Chuck provides a practical, example-driven walkthrough of the provisional patent filing process, using a personal cookie oven invention and other example applications to illustrate every step. The presenter explains that a provisional can contain multiple inventions simultaneously — a cost-saving strategy — and demonstrates how to structure drawings with numbered figure labels, write specifications that systematically reference every numbered element, and compose broad claim-like language appropriate for a provisional.

The video gives particular attention to entity status and USPTO fees. For micro entity filers (those meeting income and prior-patent count thresholds), the provisional filing fee is $60, compared to $120 for small entities and $300 for regular-sized applicants. The presenter walks through the EFS Web unregistered filer portal step by step, covering how to upload the cover sheet (SB/16), the micro entity certification (SB/15A), drawings, and specifications, and how to categorize each document type correctly to avoid validation errors.

A key practical lesson is the importance of broad provisional claims: the patent office will narrow claims during non-provisional prosecution, so starting with a claim as broad as possible preserves the option to narrow later. The presenter also warns against paying thousands of dollars to third-party "invention houses" that charge premium prices for provisional services an inventor can largely handle themselves.

## Key Takeaways

- Provisional applications can protect multiple inventions in a single filing to save money
- All figures should have numbered labels that are systematically described in the specification
- Broad claim language in a provisional preserves the option to narrow during non-provisional prosecution
- Improperly narrow provisional claims can permanently limit non-provisional scope
- Micro entity status reduces the provisional filing fee to $60; small entity pays $120; large entity pays $300
- Drawings should have a white background; color drawings are non-ideal but still submittable
- EFS Web unregistered filer portal handles the full provisional submission; no USPTO account required
- Third-party "invention houses" charge thousands of dollars for services inventors can largely do themselves

## Best For

Startup founders and independent inventors who want a detailed, example-driven tutorial on writing and filing a low-cost provisional patent application without hiring outside help.
