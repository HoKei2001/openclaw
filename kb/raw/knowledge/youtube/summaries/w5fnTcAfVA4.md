# 4 Steps to File Your Design Patent

**Channel**: J.D. Houvener / Bold Patents Law Firm
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5fnTcAfVA4
**Published**: 2023-10-10

---

## Overview

J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm presents a clear four-step process for filing a design patent application, framing each stage with practical detail about what happens and what the inventor needs to do. The video opens by distinguishing design patents (protecting the three-dimensional appearance/shape of a product) from utility patents (protecting functionality), using a water bottle as the example — the shape is a design patent issue; the ergonomic handle mechanism is a utility patent issue.

Step one is pre-filing work: defining the invention as a design rather than utility (or both), and conducting a design patent search to verify the design is novel. Searching for design patents is harder than searching utility patents because images require visual search rather than keyword search; inventors must use words describing the shape, features, and product type to surface visual prior art. Step two is preparing the application paperwork: the drawings are the most critical element, as the claims in a design patent are defined entirely by the solid lines in the drawings. Multiple angle views (top, side, angled, section) must collectively enable a reader to reconstruct the 3D object from 2D figures. Environmental context (e.g., a hand holding the product, or the product mounted in its normal setting) should be shown in dotted lines. The application also includes a single standardized claim, an application data sheet, and an inventor's oath.

Step three is prosecution: the examiner reviews the submission, conducts their own search, and may issue rejections if they find prior art making the design not novel. The inventor can respond by arguing for novelty or amending drawings to distinguish from prior art. Step four is grant: once allowed, the design patent issues with a "D" prefix on the patent number, provides 14 years of protection from grant date, and requires no maintenance fees (unlike utility patents, which require maintenance payments at years 3, 7, and 11).

## Key Takeaways

- Design patents protect the three-dimensional ornamental appearance of a product; utility patents protect functionality — both can be filed for the same invention
- Searching design patents requires visual searches supplemented by keywords describing the shape and product type
- Drawings define the claims in a design patent — solid lines are claimed, dotted lines show unclaimed context; all angles needed to reconstruct the 3D shape should be included
- The design patent application includes: drawings, one standardized claim, an application data sheet, and an inventor's oath
- Small entities and micro-entities pay lower USPTO filing fees than large companies
- Rejection and office action responses are common — responses can argue novelty or amend drawings to distinguish from cited prior art
- Design patents last 14 years from the grant date and require no maintenance fees, unlike utility patents

## Best For

Inventors who have decided to pursue a design patent and want a step-by-step procedural guide covering pre-filing, application preparation, prosecution, and post-grant responsibilities.
