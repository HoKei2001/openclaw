## Overview

This Trademark and Patent Law Experts video provides a clear, step-by-step explanation of how to convert a provisional patent application into a non-provisional patent application. The video begins by clarifying the fundamental difference between the two: a provisional application is a temporary filing that establishes an early filing date and grants patent-pending status, but it is never examined and expires after 12 months without converting into a patent. The non-provisional application is the formal filing that undergoes examination and can result in an issued patent.

The conversion process is straightforward in outline: file a complete non-provisional application within 12 months of the provisional's filing date, explicitly claiming the benefit of the earlier priority date by referencing the provisional's filing number and date. The non-provisional must include a detailed specification, claims defining the invention's scope, formal drawings (if applicable), and an inventor's oath or declaration. Required fees — filing fee, search fee, and examination fee — must also be paid.

A critical technical point is the continuity of disclosure requirement: any new content in the non-provisional that was not present in the provisional will not benefit from the earlier filing date. If the invention has been significantly developed beyond the initial disclosure, a continuation-in-part (CIP) application may be appropriate instead. The standard practice is to file a new non-provisional application claiming priority to the provisional rather than formally converting the provisional record itself.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application expires after 12 months; it must be converted (followed by a non-provisional filing) within that window or patent rights are lost
- The non-provisional application must explicitly reference the provisional's filing number and date to claim the earlier priority date
- Required elements of the non-provisional: detailed specification, patent claims, formal drawings if applicable, inventor oath/declaration, and all required fees
- New subject matter added in the non-provisional that was not in the provisional will not benefit from the earlier filing date
- If the invention has been significantly expanded beyond the initial provisional, a continuation-in-part (CIP) application may be appropriate
- Consistency between provisional and non-provisional content is essential — the non-provisional claims must be fully supported by the original provisional disclosure
- Professional guidance from a patent attorney is recommended to avoid mistakes that could undermine the priority date claim

## Best For

Inventors who have filed a provisional patent application and are approaching the 12-month deadline, and want a clear explanation of what the conversion process involves and how to preserve their priority date.
