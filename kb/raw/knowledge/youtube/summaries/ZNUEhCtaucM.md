## Overview

This video explains what patent claims are and why they are the most important part of a patent application. Using an analogy to a legal property description, the presenter explains how claims define exactly what is patented and why claim scope — broad vs. narrow — determines the ultimate commercial value of a patent.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent claims are the roughly 20 single-sentence statements at the end of a patent application that legally define what is protected — analogous to a property deed description.
- The focus of patent examination is the claims section; approximately 98% of rejections and issues during examination relate to the claims.
- Broad claims are more valuable because they cover more variations of an invention; overly narrow claims can make a patent effectively worthless since infringement becomes nearly impossible.
- The USPTO filing fee covers up to 20 claims; additional claims can be purchased, but strategic decisions may call for fewer claims.
- After filing a non-provisional application, it waits one to three years for examination; the examiner then performs a prior art search focused on the claims.
- Office actions rejecting claims are a normal part of the examination process — applicants can amend claims or argue against rejections to achieve the broadest possible allowance.
- The average application receives about three office actions before a notice of allowance is issued.
- A patent issuing does not guarantee commercial value — if claims are too narrow, no one can ever infringe them.
