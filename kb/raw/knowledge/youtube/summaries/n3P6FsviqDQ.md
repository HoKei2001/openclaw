# Patent Prior Art: What is Prior Art for Patents?

**Channel**: Patents Demystified
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3P6FsviqDQ
**Published**: 2022-12-11

---

## Overview

This video from the Patents Demystified channel gives a thorough explanation of prior art — what it is, what forms it can take, and how patent examiners use it to reject applications. The presenter emphasizes that understanding prior art is not just a technical requirement but a strategic foundation for anyone thinking about filing a patent application.

At its most basic, prior art is any technology disclosure that existed before an applicant filed their patent application. Most commonly this means issued patents and patent application publications, but it also includes physical products, product catalogs, scientific papers, blog posts, and even YouTube videos — anything that discloses how a technology works. The video then addresses two widespread myths. The first myth is that expired patents cannot be used as prior art. This is completely false: even if a patent has expired, its publication remains a public technology disclosure that examiners can and do cite against later applications. The second myth is that only issued patents constitute prior art. Also false: patent applications are automatically published 18 months after their earliest priority date, and those publications remain valid prior art permanently — whether the application is still pending, was abandoned, or was rejected by the examiner.

The video concludes with a strategic insight: since publications of your own pending application can act as prior art against competitors, filing your own application and allowing it to publish is an effective way to create a prior art block that prevents others from patenting similar inventions. In practice, about 90 to 95 percent of the prior art cited in examination comes from issued patents or patent application publications, but examiners do sometimes cite YouTube videos, scientific papers, and product catalogs.

## Key Takeaways

- Prior art is any technology disclosure that existed before your patent application filing date — not limited to patents.
- Examples of prior art include issued patents, patent publications, physical products, product catalogs, scientific papers, blog posts, and YouTube videos.
- Expired patents are still valid prior art — the expiration of a patent does not affect the status of its publication as a public disclosure.
- Abandoned patent applications that were published (18 months after filing) remain permanently valid prior art, regardless of whether they ever issued.
- In practice, 90 to 95 percent of cited prior art consists of issued patents or published patent applications.
- Filing your own patent application creates prior art that can prevent competitors from patenting similar inventions even if your application never issues.
- Understanding prior art usage is the foundation for responding to novelty (102) and obviousness (103) rejections during examination.

## Best For

Inventors who are preparing to file a patent application or who have received an office action citing prior art and want to understand exactly what prior art is and how it can be used against them.
