## Overview

Patent strategist Rich Goldstein provides a comprehensive breakdown of the real costs involved in obtaining and maintaining a patent, from government filing fees through attorney drafting costs, maintenance fees, and potential appeals. The video emphasizes that filing fees are just the beginning and that the largest expense is typically attorney time — primarily for drafting the patent application itself.

Government filing fees are relatively low and vary by entity size: provisional patent applications cost $75–$300; non-provisional (utility) applications cost $400–$800; design patent applications cost $250–$500. These fees alone do not cover the full process. Maintenance fees (required at approximately 4, 8, and 12 years after grant) range from $400 to $7,500 over the patent's lifetime. Attorney fees are where costs escalate significantly: drafting a utility patent application typically costs $10,000–$15,000 (more for complex inventions or software), provisional patent drafting runs $5,000–$7,500 for a quality application, and design patent drafting costs $3,000–$5,000. Professional patent drawings add $500–$1,500. Office action responses cost $1,000–$5,000 each depending on complexity. Appeals to the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (about 1–5% of cases) may cost $10,000–$20,000.

Total estimated ranges: a simple design patent costs $4,000–$8,000 total (with no maintenance fees after grant); a simple utility patent costs $15,000–$20,000; a complex utility patent can reach $20,000–$30,000 over the examination period. Goldstein's key insight is that the biggest waste of money in patent prosecution is not paying too much per service — it's paying for the wrong services because the strategy wasn't aligned with business goals at the outset. He offers free strategy calls to help inventors understand what their patent journey should look like.

## Key Takeaways

- Government fees alone are low ($400–$800 for utility non-provisional) — attorney fees are the primary cost driver
- Drafting a utility patent application typically costs $10,000–$15,000 in attorney fees; software patents can cost more
- Provisional patent drafting: $5,000–$7,500; design patent drafting: $3,000–$5,000
- Office action responses cost $1,000–$5,000 each; budget for multiple responses during examination
- Total estimated costs: $4,000–$8,000 for simple design patent; $15,000–$30,000 for utility patents depending on complexity
- Design patents have no maintenance fees after grant — a significant long-term cost advantage
- The biggest money-waster is wrong strategy, not high individual service fees — align patent strategy with business goals before investing

## Best For

Inventors and entrepreneurs who want a realistic, comprehensive picture of all the cost components involved in getting and maintaining a patent, to properly budget before committing to the patent process.
