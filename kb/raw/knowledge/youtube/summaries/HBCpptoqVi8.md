## Overview

This short video from OC Patent Lawyer provides a clear, concise explanation of what a prior art search is, why it matters, and how it differs from a general patent search. The core concept is straightforward: a prior art search is the process of finding existing patents, published applications, and other public disclosures that are similar to a new invention, in order to determine whether that invention is novel enough to be patented.

If an invention's core idea has already been patented or publicly described elsewhere, it is not eligible for patent protection — making the prior art search a critical early step that can save inventors from wasting time and money on a doomed application. Beyond qualifying the invention for patentability, the prior art search also provides guidance for writing the patent application: it helps identify which aspects of the invention are truly new and deserve emphasis, and which aspects overlap with existing art and should be treated more carefully.

The video notes that prior art searches are typically conducted by patent attorneys using professional databases, tools, and analytical expertise to identify relevant prior art and assess whether the invention clears the novelty bar. The search results are then analyzed to produce a patentability opinion.

## Key Takeaways

- A prior art search identifies existing patents and public disclosures similar to a new invention to assess novelty.
- If the core idea has already been patented or publicly published, the invention is not eligible for patent protection.
- Conducting a prior art search before filing prevents wasting resources on an application that is unlikely to succeed.
- The search results guide the drafting of the patent application by revealing which aspects are truly novel.
- Prior art searches are typically conducted by patent attorneys using professional databases and analytical tools.
- The search produces a patentability opinion that helps inventors and attorneys decide whether and how to proceed.
- A prior art search is distinct from a general patent search: it has the specific purpose of evaluating patentability.
