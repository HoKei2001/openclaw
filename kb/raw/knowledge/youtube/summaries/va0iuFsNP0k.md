## Overview

In this presentation from BARDEHLE PAGENBERG patent attorney delivered in Saint Petersburg, the speaker provides an expert walkthrough of the European Patent Office (EPO) framework for examining software patents. The European Patent Convention (EPC) covers 38 member states — distinct from the EU — and offers a single examination procedure resulting in a bundle of national patents that must be validated and enforced individually in each country. Germany is the most common litigation venue in Europe. The EPO receives roughly 175,000 applications per year and is one of the world's five largest patent offices (IP5).

The core principle for software patentability at the EPO is deceptively simple: a patent can be granted for software if it makes an inventive technical contribution to the prior art. There are two hurdles: patent eligibility (a coarse filter that is easily cleared by having any single technical feature in a claim — even just the word "computer") and inventive step (the real challenge, where only technical features count toward novelty assessment). Non-technical features — business logic, mathematical methods, subjective content decisions — are simply disregarded in the inventive step analysis. This contrasts sharply with the USPTO, where patent eligibility (post-Alice) is the difficult hurdle.

The speaker walks through six illustrative case examples: an anti-lock braking algorithm (clearly technical, patentable), a sorting algorithm without a computer (not patent-eligible), a computer-implemented sorting algorithm (eligible but not inventive), a neural network cardiac pacemaker (all features technical, strong case), a Microsoft spam classifier based on email content (non-technical because spam classification depends on human preferences, not technical criteria), and a pedestrian crowd movement simulation (outcome uncertain, referred to the EPO Enlarged Board of Appeal). The talk emphasizes that understanding the case law is essential and provides a reference to bardehle.com/europeansoftwarepatents for summaries of Board of Appeal decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- EPO software patent eligibility is easy to clear: any single technical feature (e.g., "a computer") satisfies the first hurdle
- The real challenge is inventive step: only technical features count; non-technical features (business logic, math, human preferences) are disregarded
- This is the opposite of the USPTO, where patent eligibility (Alice doctrine) is the difficult hurdle
- Embedded control software, medical devices with AI, and algorithm-hardware combinations are strong EPO patent candidates
- Pure sorting algorithms, business methods on computers, and content-based classification systems generally fail the inventive step test
- The European patent grants a bundle of national patents — enforcement must happen separately in each validated country
- Germany is the most active patent litigation venue in Europe; new smartphone/telecom/connected car patent wars are emerging
