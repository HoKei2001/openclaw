# How to Do a Google Patent Search

**Channel**: J.D. Houvener / Bold Patents Law Firm
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owFNROB39Bw
**Published**: 2019-12-24

---

## Overview

Patent attorney J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm conducts a live, screen-shared Google Patents search demonstration using a submersible underwater food-cooling container as the example invention. The session illustrates the full range of professional search techniques: starting with broad layman's terms to get oriented, then progressively narrowing using more precise terminology, patent classification codes (both USPTO and CPC), and citation-based searching (forward and backward citations).

A key lesson is the importance of vocabulary flexibility. The words an inventor uses to describe their invention often differ substantially from the technical language used in patents — for example, a thermometer might appear in a patent as a "thermistor" or "temperature regulation device." Finding a relevant reference early in the search reveals better search vocabulary for subsequent queries. The presenter also demonstrates how to use CPC classification codes found on relevant patents to search entire classification buckets, which often turns up related art that keyword searches miss.

The video also covers non-patent literature search using Google image search and product searches, noting that prior art can exist in commercial products, academic papers, and industrial applications — not just patents. The overall message is that patent searching is an iterative, multi-approach process requiring both breadth (shotgun keyword strategies) and depth (classification narrowing, citation chasing), and that convergence across different search methods is the sign of a thorough search.

## Key Takeaways

- Start with broad, layman's terms to orient the search, then progressively narrow using technical patent vocabulary
- Terminology used by inventors often differs greatly from patent claim language; discovering the right terms is part of the search process
- CPC and USPTO classification codes found on relevant patents provide highly targeted search buckets for related prior art
- Citation searching (patents cited by and patents citing a reference) is a powerful technique for finding related art
- Non-patent literature (products, academic papers, industrial applications) must also be searched for complete prior art analysis
- Google image search is useful for consumer products, often revealing prior art that text searches miss
- Finding a single good reference should prompt both similar-results searching and a re-evaluation of search terms
- Thorough patent searching requires multiple approaches and hours of iterative searching; convergence across methods confirms completeness

## Best For

Inventors and early-stage startups who want to learn professional-level Google Patents search techniques from a working patent attorney, including keyword strategies, classification code use, and citation-based searching.
