## Overview

This brief explainer from Berkeley Law & Technology Group outlines the key steps and considerations for pursuing a software patent. It begins by distinguishing software patents from software copyright: copyright protects the expression of code automatically in 151 countries under the Berne Convention (lasting the creator's lifetime plus up to 70 years), while a patent protects the underlying invention itself and requires a separate application in each country or through the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) for international coverage.

To be eligible for a software patent in the United States, the program must have a commercial or industrial application, be novel, and be non-obvious. Before applying, inventors are advised to define their software thoroughly — its overall purpose, what makes it unique, how it handles information, what problems it solves, the UI design, and which elements need protection. This information helps the patent attorney craft the strongest possible application.

The video recommends working with an experienced patent attorney, particularly for researching prior art and drafting the application. It emphasizes that the application should include flowcharts showing how the software works and how it achieves its purpose, and notes that passing the "machine or transformation" test — showing the software is tied to a machine or transforms one thing into another — makes patentability significantly easier to establish.

## Key Takeaways

- Software copyright protection is automatic in 151 countries; software patents require separate applications in each country or PCT filing
- U.S. software patent eligibility requires commercial/industrial application, novelty, and non-obviousness
- Define your software comprehensively before applying: purpose, uniqueness, data handling, UI, and elements to protect
- Include flowcharts in the application to clearly explain how the software works and achieves its goals
- The machine or transformation test: showing software is tied to a machine or transforms data makes patentability easier
- Conduct extensive prior art research — you may find existing patents that your software could infringe
- Working with a qualified patent attorney reduces the likelihood of USPTO rejection
