## Overview

This session from a patent terminology course provides a detailed examination of method claims: why they are used, how they should be structured, and their advantages and limitations in prosecution and enforcement. The presenter explains that method claims often provide the broadest patent protection because they recite fewer physical structural limitations than apparatus claims — a significant advantage particularly for computer-implemented inventions where reciting hardware components can introduce unnecessary limitations. Method claims also track naturally with flowcharts, making them an ideal starting point when drafting a computer-implemented patent application.

The video covers specific drafting conventions for method claims, including the "broad by narrow" format (e.g., "communicating by transmitting") that establishes broad scope while providing the narrower implementation as an example, and the importance of ordering dependent claim limitations to restrict the method first before adding structural (wherein) limitations. The presenter also addresses extraterritoriality challenges: computer-implemented method claims can be difficult or impossible to enforce if portions of the method are performed outside the U.S., and passive-voice "wherein" recitation may offer a stronger enforcement position than affirmative step language in such cases.

Practical guidance is given on the three independent claim types commonly used in U.S. practice — method, apparatus, and computer-readable medium — and the presenter notes that U.S. practitioners get three independent claims for free (no additional government fee), making it standard practice to include all three types for comprehensive coverage.

## Key Takeaways

- Method claims are often the broadest claims because they recite less physical structure than apparatus claims
- They are particularly advantageous for computer-implemented and telecommunications inventions
- Method claims track naturally with flowcharts, making them a useful starting point for application drafting
- Use "broad by narrow" format: "communicating by transmitting" — broad term first, narrow specific action second
- In dependent claims, limit the method first (further comprising) before adding structural wherein clauses
- Structural wherein clauses in dependent method claims may receive little patentable weight from examiners
- Extraterritoriality: method claims may be unenforceable if steps are performed outside the U.S.; passive-voice wherein clauses may help
- U.S. practice allows three independent claims for free — use method, apparatus, and CRM claims for comprehensive coverage
