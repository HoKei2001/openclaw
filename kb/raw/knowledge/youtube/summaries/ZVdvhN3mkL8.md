## Overview

This video from Trademark and Patent Law Experts provides a concise, accessible explanation of how to file an international patent application through the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT). The presenter explains that the PCT is not a patent itself but a procedural mechanism administered by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) that allows inventors to file a single application and preserve the right to seek patent protection in up to 157 member countries.

The video walks through the core timeline and mechanics of PCT filing: an inventor typically starts with a domestic patent application (such as a U.S. provisional or non-provisional), then files a PCT application within 12 months. After the PCT application is filed, WIPO generates an International Search Report and a Written Opinion on patentability, giving the applicant early feedback on the strength of their claims. The applicant then has up to 30 or 31 months from the original priority date to enter the national phase in individual target countries, during which they must pay local fees and comply with each country's requirements.

The presenter emphasizes two practical points: first, PCT filing is expensive, and inventors should carefully select target markets based on where commercial opportunity actually exists rather than filing everywhere. Second, working with a patent attorney experienced in international prosecution is critical because each national phase entry involves local rules, translation requirements, and separate attorney relationships. The video briefly discusses regional patent systems (such as the European Patent Office and the African Regional Intellectual Property Organization) as cost-effective alternatives to filing separately in multiple countries within a region.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT is a single international application mechanism covering 157 member countries, administered by WIPO.
- A PCT application must be filed within 12 months of the priority application.
- Applicants have 30–31 months from the priority date to enter the national phase in individual countries.
- WIPO provides an International Search Report and Written Opinion on patentability as part of the PCT process.
- PCT filing is expensive — strategically select target countries based on actual commercial opportunity.
- Regional patent systems (EPO, ARIPO, etc.) can reduce costs compared to separate national filings within a region.
- The PCT does not result in a "world patent" — patents are still granted by each national office separately.
- An experienced international patent attorney is essential for managing national phase deadlines and local requirements.
