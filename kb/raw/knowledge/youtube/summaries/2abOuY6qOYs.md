# How to Write the Basic Claim in a Patent Application

## Overview

This instructional video from OC Patent Lawyer walks viewers through the three-part structure of a patent claim — preamble, transitional phrase, and body — using a tripod as the example invention. The video is designed as part of a series on patent application writing and focuses on how to list parts, interrelate them, describe their purpose, and follow the key grammatical rules that the USPTO requires (one sentence, introducing terms with "a" before using "the," semicolons between elements).

## Key Takeaways

- A patent claim has three parts: the preamble (sets the context, e.g., "a tripod for supporting a camera"), the transitional phrase (links preamble to body; "comprising" is the most common), and the body (lists the parts of the invention).
- The body should list parts that achieve the claimed benefit, interrelate them, and explain the purpose of each part.
- Each part in the body starts a new paragraph and ends with a semicolon; the entire claim is one long sentence ending in a single period.
- The first mention of each term uses "a" (e.g., "a tripod"); subsequent mentions use "the" (e.g., "the tripod") — this is the antecedent basis rule.
- The claim should focus on the parts that make the invention unique (the inventive concept), not on every component of the device.
- Writing effective claims takes years of practice to master — viewers are encouraged to try but should seek professional help for a real application.
