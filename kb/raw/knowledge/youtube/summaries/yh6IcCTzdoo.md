# Multiple Provisional Patent Applications for One Invention

**Channel**: Inventor's Quick Tips
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yh6IcCTzdoo
**Published**: 2021-06-19

---

## Overview

This video from Inventor's Quick Tips explains the strategy of filing multiple provisional patent applications for a single evolving invention. The presenter walks through a concrete example where an inventor files an initial provisional application for features A, B, and C, then files a second provisional several months later adding features D and E, before ultimately filing a regular utility patent application that claims priority to both provisionals and also includes new features F and G.

The key insight is that each patent claim in the subsequent utility application can reference whichever provisional most strongly supports it, allowing different claims to have different effective priority dates. For example, a claim covering features A, B, and C gets the earliest priority date from the first provisional, while a claim covering feature D gets the priority date of the second provisional, and a claim covering entirely new feature G only gets the filing date of the utility application itself. During examination, patent examiners may cite prior art references dated after the earliest provisional but before the utility filing date to challenge specific claims.

The video notes that it is not uncommon to have three or four provisionals filed over the course of a year when innovating quickly. This strategy allows inventors to incrementally protect their intellectual property as the invention develops, rather than waiting to capture everything in one document.

## Key Takeaways

- Multiple provisional applications can be filed for the same invention as it evolves, capturing new features over time
- A utility patent application can claim priority to multiple provisional applications simultaneously
- Different claims in the utility application can have different effective priority dates based on which provisional first disclosed them
- A provisional application remains pending for exactly one year and cannot be extended
- The utility application must be filed within one year of the first provisional's filing date to preserve its priority
- Features introduced only in the utility application (not in any provisional) only get the utility application's filing date
- Patent examiners may cite prior art dated between an early provisional and the utility filing date to reject specific claims
- This strategy is cost-effective since provisionals are relatively inexpensive and allow continuous IP protection during development

## Best For

Inventors and startup founders who are actively developing and refining their inventions over time and want to understand how to strategically use multiple provisional applications to maximize patent protection.
