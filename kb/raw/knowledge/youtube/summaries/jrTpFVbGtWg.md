# 7 Mistakes to Avoid - Provisional Patent Application

**Channel**: Edel Patents
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jrTpFVbGtWg
**Published**: 2021-04-12

---

## Overview

Patent professional Edel Patents identifies seven critical mistakes inventors make when filing provisional patent applications, presenting them in an actionable format with explanations of both the problem and its consequences. The overarching theme is that these mistakes are avoidable with preparation, and that mistake number four (premature public disclosure) is the most catastrophic because it can permanently destroy patent rights.

The seven mistakes covered are: (1) not conducting a prior art search before filing, leaving inventors unaware whether their invention is actually new; (2) lacking a business plan, which leads to poor decisions throughout the patent process since patenting is ultimately a business investment; (3) not consulting a patent attorney, since patent mistakes are often permanent and irreversible; (4) making premature public disclosures before the application is filed, which can bar patent rights in the U.S. and internationally; (5) lack of focus — failing to concentrate the disclosure on the points of novelty rather than producing lengthy but unfocused narrative; (6) a bad disclosure, whether fatally incomplete (failing to teach how to make and use the invention) or inefficiently structured (failing to quickly orient the attorney to the key novel features); and (7) having no drafting plan, which leads to rolling changes during attorney drafting that cause budget overruns.

The video ends with a framework for avoiding mistake seven: ensure the attorney confirms full understanding of the invention, provide a complete disclosure, and agree on cost before work begins.

## Key Takeaways

- Conducting a prior art search before filing is essential to know whether the invention is novel
- Treating the invention as a business from the start clarifies money decisions throughout the patent process
- Patent mistakes are often permanent — consulting a patent attorney is worth the cost
- Public disclosure before filing is a critical mistake that can bar patent rights permanently in some jurisdictions
- The application should focus on points of novelty; unfocused lengthy narratives are less useful than a concise focused disclosure
- A disclosure must teach how to make and use the invention — legally required for a valid patent
- Provide the attorney with the most important novel features immediately to achieve an "aha moment" quickly
- Avoid cost overruns by confirming full understanding, providing a complete disclosure, and agreeing on cost before drafting begins

## Best For

Inventors preparing to file a provisional patent application who want to identify and avoid the most common and consequential mistakes before spending time and money on the filing.
