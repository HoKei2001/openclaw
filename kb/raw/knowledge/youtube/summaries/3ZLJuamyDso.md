# Patent Office Action Response - Claim Amendments

## Overview

This Inventor's Quick Tips episode provides a detailed, education-focused guide to patent office action responses, with particular depth on the mechanics of claim amendments. The video is framed for inventors who will be working with a patent attorney on their response and want to understand the document well enough to give informed feedback — rather than for inventors attempting to file their own response (which the video explicitly discourages due to the complexity of rules and procedures involved).

The video explains the structure of an office action and the typical response timeline: non-final actions give three months without fees and up to six months with extension fees; final actions are more restrictive and major changes may require filing a Request for Continued Examination (RCE) to convert the action back to non-final status. The composition of a proper response is laid out: claim amendments (with specific formatting conventions — underline for additions, strikethrough for deletions, double brackets for single-character removals), drawing amendments, specification amendments, and most importantly, remarks — the written arguments addressing each rejection and explaining why the claims should be allowed.

The video introduces the five claim states used in amendments: currently amended (changes being made in this response), original (unchanged), cancelled (removed entirely with text deleted), previously presented (changed in a prior response, now shown clean), and new (added for the first time). The no-new-matter rule is emphasized: any amendments must be supported by the original application as filed. A critical strategic principle is highlighted — the most effective responses ensure that the distinguishing differences argued in the remarks are actually embodied in the amended claims, since claims define what is protected and arguments that aren't reflected in the claims are much weaker. The MPEP section 714 is cited as the authoritative reference for further detail on claim amendment rules.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent office action responses should almost always be prepared by a patent professional, though inventors benefit from understanding the document to give informed feedback
- Non-final office actions allow three months without fees and up to six months with extension fees; final actions are more restrictive and may require an RCE to make significant changes
- The five claim states in an amendment are: currently amended, original, cancelled, previously presented, and new — each has specific formatting conventions
- Additions to claims are shown in underline; deletions use strikethrough; single-character deletions use double brackets
- The no-new-matter rule prohibits adding anything to claims that was not already present in the original application as filed
- Remarks (written arguments) must address every rejection and every cited reference, even if only briefly — incomplete responses are not fully responsive
- The strongest office action responses reflect the distinguishing differences in both the remarks AND the amended claim language — arguments not embodied in the claims are significantly weaker

## Best For

Inventors who have received a patent office action and want to understand the structure and mechanics of the response their attorney will prepare, so they can give meaningful feedback on claim amendments and remarks.
