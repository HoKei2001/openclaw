## Overview

This lecture from the CEC (Consortium for Educational Communication) features University of Delhi IP law professor Ashwin Evil and is part of an ongoing series on intellectual property rights in India. It focuses on the non-obviousness (inventive step) requirement for obtaining a patent in India, building on a prior lecture that covered novelty. The session covers the four patentability requirements under the Indian Patents Act: subject matter eligibility (Sections 3 and 4 exclusions), novelty, inventive step/non-obviousness, and industrial applicability.

The core of the lecture explores the concept of "inventive step" as defined in Section 2(1)(ja) of the Indian Patents Act — a feature involving technical advance as compared to existing knowledge or having economic significance or both, making the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art. The professor methodically explains the elements: there must be a technical advance or economic significance, and the invention must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant field. He draws on key case laws to illustrate how Indian courts have interpreted and applied this standard, emphasizing that the bar for demonstrating non-obviousness must overcome what was already known at the time of filing.

The lecture also distinguishes the Indian standard from jurisdictions like the U.S. and UK, noting that India uses the term "inventive step" rather than "non-obviousness," though the underlying concept is substantively similar. The session is designed for law students preparing for the patent agent examination and for practitioners needing a thorough, legally referenced understanding of the inventive step doctrine in India, complete with case law analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- India uses the term "inventive step" (Section 2(1)(ja)) rather than "non-obviousness" — but the concept is substantively equivalent
- Inventive step requires technical advance or economic significance (or both) making the invention non-obvious to a person skilled in the art
- The four patentability requirements in India: subject matter eligibility (Sections 3-4), novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability
- Case law is essential for understanding how inventive step is evaluated — Indian courts have developed specific interpretive principles
- Technical advance and economic significance are evaluated relative to existing knowledge at the time of filing
- The "person skilled in the art" standard is applied consistently: someone knowledgeable in the field but not inventively brilliant
- Sections 3 and 4 exclusions (including computer programs per se and traditional knowledge) must be checked before applying the inventive step analysis
