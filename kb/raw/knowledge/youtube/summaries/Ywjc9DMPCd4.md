## Overview

This video from Bold Patents Law Firm explains how inventors can use the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) to pursue international patent protection from a single filing. The presenter, J.D. Houvener, walks through the PCT process step by step, explaining how filing a single international application preserves the right to seek patent protection in over 150 countries simultaneously while deferring the cost of entering each country's national phase.

The video uses a practical example to illustrate the typical workflow: an inventor first files a U.S. provisional patent application to establish a priority date, then files a PCT application within 12 months, and finally enters the national phase in individual countries (such as Australia, Canada, Europe, or Japan) within 30 months of the original priority date. This timeline gives inventors roughly two and a half years to evaluate commercial potential and secure investment before incurring the substantial costs of pursuing patents country by country. The PCT application itself is handled through the USPTO as the receiving office when filed by a U.S. applicant.

The presenter explains that the PCT process does not result in a single "world patent" — patents are always granted by individual national or regional patent offices. Rather, the PCT is a procedural mechanism that streamlines the international filing process, provides a centralized prior art search (the International Search Report), and delays the significant costs of national phase entry. The video emphasizes that working with a patent attorney familiar with international prosecution is essential for managing the deadlines and strategic decisions involved in selecting target countries.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT allows inventors to file a single international application that preserves the right to pursue patents in 150+ countries.
- A PCT application must be filed within 12 months of the original priority application (typically the provisional).
- National phase entry in individual countries must occur within 30 months of the priority date, providing significant cost deferral.
- The PCT does not produce a single "world patent" — patents are still granted by each national or regional office separately.
- An International Search Report (ISR) is produced as part of the PCT process, providing early prior art analysis.
- U.S. applicants file the PCT application through the USPTO as the receiving office.
- The PCT is especially valuable for inventions with global commercial potential, giving time to validate the market before national phase costs.
- International patent prosecution requires an attorney experienced in managing multiple country deadlines and local patent law requirements.

## Best For

Inventors and businesses with commercially promising inventions who want to understand how to use the PCT process to preserve international patent rights while managing costs and timing strategically.
