## Overview

A 30-year patent attorney opens this video with a direct warning: choosing the wrong patent attorney can cost an inventor more than money — it can cost them their invention. The core critique is that most first-time inventors evaluate attorneys almost entirely on price, missing criteria that matter far more. The video presents five criteria for selecting a good patent attorney, emphasizing that the right attorney is a strategic advisor and business guide, not merely a technical document drafter.

The first criterion is plain-language communication: an attorney who cannot explain patent strategy in plain English without legal jargon is a red flag. The second is genuine understanding of the inventor's business model — the needs of an Amazon seller differ substantially from those of a tech startup, and an attorney who cannot distinguish them will produce a patent that may be legally valid but strategically useless. The third criterion is a focus on practical protection rather than upselling: not every product needs a utility patent, and a good attorney will guide clients toward design patents, trademarks, or trade dress when those are the better fit, especially when prior art makes a strong utility patent unavailable.

The fourth criterion is process transparency — an attorney should be able to explain exactly what happens after payment, what each milestone looks like, and what additional costs to expect. The fifth is a proven track record with first-time inventors or the specific type of client (Amazon seller, startup, etc.), since many experienced attorneys have never worked with a beginner and lack the communication skills to guide them effectively.

## Key Takeaways

- Evaluating attorneys solely on price misses the criteria that determine whether the attorney will actually serve the inventor's goals
- A great patent attorney is a strategic advisor who speaks plain English — not a technician who produces legally correct but strategically useless filings
- Attorneys must understand the specific business context: Amazon brand registry, startup investor requirements, and licensing goals each demand different IP strategies
- Not every product needs a utility patent; a good attorney proactively recommends design patents, trademarks, or trade dress when they offer better protection
- Transparency about process milestones and expected costs is a basic requirement — confusion about where money is going is a red flag
- Track record with first-time inventors is a distinct qualification; many seasoned attorneys lack experience guiding beginners through the process without overwhelming them

## Best For

First-time inventors and Amazon sellers evaluating patent attorneys who want a practical checklist of criteria beyond price to avoid costly mistakes.
