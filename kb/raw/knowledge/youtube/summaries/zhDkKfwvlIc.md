# What is the Patent Cooperation Treaty?

**Channel**: IPAustralia
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zhDkKfwvlIc
**Published**: 2022-10-30

---

## Overview

This short official video from IP Australia — Australia's intellectual property agency — provides a clear and concise explanation of the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) and how it helps inventors seek patent protection in multiple countries simultaneously without having to file separate applications in each country right away.

The PCT is an international agreement administered by WIPO (the World Intellectual Property Organization) that allows inventors to file a single international patent application that serves as a placeholder in over 150 member countries simultaneously. Australia is one of those member countries, along with the United States and most other major economies. By filing through the PCT, inventors get early feedback on whether their invention is likely to be patentable before committing to the cost of pursuing individual national patents. This can prevent expensive investment in countries where the application would ultimately be rejected.

The video makes clear that the PCT does not result in a single "world patent" — filing a PCT application is an entry point, not an end point. The inventor still needs to enter the national phase in each target country and meet that country's local requirements, including potentially different formal requirements, translation needs, and local attorney fees. However, the PCT process delays and consolidates the early stages of multinational filing, saving significant time and money compared to filing directly with each country's patent office from the start.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT allows inventors to file a single international application that preserves the right to pursue patents in over 150 member countries simultaneously.
- The PCT is administered by WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization), and Australia is a member country along with the United States and most major economies.
- Filing through the PCT provides early feedback on international patentability before committing to the cost of national phase entry in each target country.
- A PCT application is not a "world patent" — the inventor must still apply in each target country and meet local requirements, including translations and local attorney fees.
- The PCT process delays the national phase filing deadline (typically 30 months from the priority date), giving inventors more time to evaluate markets and secure funding.
- Using the PCT can save time and money compared to filing directly in multiple countries at once, especially when the ultimate choice of target markets is still uncertain.
