## Overview

This video from patent attorney Curt Lockhart provides a practical, screen-sharing walkthrough of filing a provisional patent application through the USPTO Patent Center. The video focuses on helping individual inventors, particularly those who may qualify for micro entity status, which reduces filing fees by 75%. The presenter walks through each step of the Patent Center's web-based application data sheet, demonstrating exactly what fields to fill in and which sections can be skipped for a straightforward individual inventor filing.

The video covers document preparation requirements: the specification (written description), optional drawings, and the certification of micro entity status form. For micro entity qualification, inventors must meet four criteria: qualify as a small entity (under 500 employees), have fewer than four previously filed patents as a named inventor, have a gross income below the current threshold (approximately $241,830), and not assign patent rights to an entity that doesn't qualify. The presenter fills out each section of the application data sheet live, including inventor information, title, entity status, and optional fields.

The submission process is demonstrated through Patent Center, with the presenter uploading documents in the correct format (DOCX or PDF), selecting the micro entity discount for filing fees, and explaining the review screen before submission. The video closes with important reminders about the provisional application's limitations: it is not an actual patent, it expires exactly 12 months from filing, and the deadline cannot be extended.

## Key Takeaways

- Micro entity status reduces USPTO filing fees by 75%, making provisional applications approximately $65 for qualifying individuals
- Micro entity requirements include: small entity status, fewer than four previously filed patents, gross income below ~$241,830, and no disqualifying assignment
- The main document needed is the specification (written description); drawings are helpful but not required
- Filing is done through USPTO Patent Center using a web-based application data sheet (ADS)
- Inventors can file as a guest user without creating a USPTO account, though an account is recommended for tracking
- The filing receipt contains the application number needed for future reference and conversion to non-provisional
- The more detailed the provisional application, the stronger the foundation for the later non-provisional application
- The 12-month deadline from filing date is absolute and cannot be extended
