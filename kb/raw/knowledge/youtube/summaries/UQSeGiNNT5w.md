# How To Select and Hire A Patent Attorney

**Channel**: Dallas Fort-Worth Trademarks and Patents
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UQSeGiNNT5w
**Published**: 2017-06-12

---

## Overview

Steven Thrasher of Thrasher Associates presents five structured factors for selecting a patent attorney, framing the decision as one of the most consequential choices an inventor will make in protecting and commercializing their idea. The video is practical and methodical, moving through each factor with clear explanations and real-world illustrations.

Technical alignment is the first factor: the attorney should have a background in and experience filing applications in the same technology area as the invention. Software patents, for instance, require an attorney familiar with how software claims are structured and examined. Experience is the second factor and largely reinforces the first — Thrasher draws an analogy to not taking a car to a mechanic who has never worked on that make, and warns against large TV-advertised companies that sometimes outsource applications overseas. The third factor is speed and urgency, which is tied directly to the America Invents Act's first-to-file rule: delays between invention disclosure and filing create a real window for competitors to file first and claim the patent.

Budget is the fourth factor, and Thrasher makes the important point that trying to patent every aspect of an invention can quickly generate costs exceeding $100,000. He recommends treating the patent attorney as a "Chief Intellectual Property Officer" who helps prioritize what to protect within a defined budget to maximize coverage per dollar spent. The fifth factor is long-term goals — whether the inventor intends to license, raise investment capital, sell through channels, or build a business — since those objectives should shape the type and scope of patents pursued from the start.

## Key Takeaways

- Technical alignment ensures the attorney has direct experience with patent applications in the specific technology field of the invention
- The America Invents Act's first-to-file rule makes attorney responsiveness and filing speed a competitive advantage, not just a convenience
- Trying to patent every aspect of an invention can cost over $100,000; strategic prioritization with the attorney as an IP partner is essential
- Long-term goals (licensing vs. investment vs. startup) should drive patent strategy from the first filing, not be addressed after the fact
- Outsourcing patent work overseas (common with large advertising-heavy services) risks quality and communication issues
- Asking attorneys to function as a Chief Intellectual Property Officer — budget planning, prioritization, and strategy — yields better results than treating them as document processors

## Best For

Inventors preparing to hire their first patent attorney who want a clear five-factor framework to evaluate and compare candidates before making a commitment.
