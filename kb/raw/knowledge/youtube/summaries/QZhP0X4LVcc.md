# Provisional Patent Application Drafting Guide: Physical/Mechanical Products

**Channel**: Patents Demystified
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QZhP0X4LVcc
**Published**: 2025-10-05

---

## Overview

Patent attorney Dylan Adams with over 20 years of experience provides an expert drafting guide specifically for provisional patent applications covering physical and mechanical products — including consumer goods, mechanical devices, machinery, and computerized systems with novel physical components. The video's central insight is that the USPTO will grant a filing date to almost anything submitted as a provisional application, but a poorly drafted provisional provides essentially no real protection.

Adams explains what inventors should not spend their time on: agonizing over the title (a few minutes is enough), writing a background section (it becomes admitted prior art and can be used against you), drafting formal patent claims (not required in provisionals, and a one-line placeholder is sufficient for foreign filing purposes), and perfecting drawings (rough hand sketches are far better than one polished CAD drawing). Instead, 95% of effort should go into creating multiple clear drawings from different angles and writing a thorough, detailed description in plain engineering language.

The video provides advanced guidance on describing all possible variations of the invention — including future improvements and even inferior versions a competitor might create — to build the broadest possible protection. A critical warning about "limiting language" is given: never use the phrase "the invention" or describe any feature as essential or required, as this language can be used to narrow your patent's scope in later litigation. For inventors planning to show their application to investors or enforce it in court, Adams recommends a "coached provisional" review by a patent attorney.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional application is a one-year placeholder; you only get protection for what you actually disclose — a thin filing is nearly worthless.
- Focus 95% of drafting effort on drawings and the detailed description; everything else is secondary.
- Include multiple drawing views: front, back, perspective, exploded, close-up details, and different configuration states.
- All drawings must be true black-and-white with no grayscale; each figure should fill its own page at maximum size.
- Write in plain language like an engineer, not like a patent attorney — formal patent style does not add protection value.
- Describe as many variations as possible, including hypothetical future versions and inferior competitor versions.
- Never use "the invention" or describe features as essential — use "in one embodiment" to keep scope broad.
- Claims are not required, but include one dummy claim ("the systems and methods shown and described herein") for international filing purposes.

## Best For

Inventors preparing to draft their own provisional patent application for a physical or mechanical product who want professional guidance on where to focus their effort for maximum protection.
