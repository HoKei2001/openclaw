## Overview

This Bold Patents live session features patent attorney J.D. Houvener and trademark attorney Matt discussing USPTO office actions for both patents and trademarks. The video explains that receiving two to three office actions per patent application is entirely normal — applicants should not be alarmed by a rejection, since it is part of the standard prosecution process. The session is a practical deep-dive into how office actions work and how to respond to them effectively.

A case study on GeoOrbital — a product that claimed to be "patented technology" for converting any bicycle into an electric bike using a special front wheel — is used to illustrate a critical lesson: a patent application abandoned after failing to respond to a USPTO office action in 2017 is not the same as a granted patent. J.D. walks through the actual application, explaining why it was rejected on grounds of lack of novelty and obviousness, and noting that GeoOrbital's marketing misrepresented the patent status to consumers. Meanwhile, the GeoOrbital trademark was successfully registered — showing that patent and trademark outcomes can diverge significantly.

Matt elaborates on the trademark office action process, including the differences in timeline and legal requirements compared to patent prosecution. The trademark registration process for GeoOrbital took over two years, illustrating that trademarks also commonly face office actions and require persistence to obtain registration.

## Key Takeaways

- Receiving two to three office actions (rejections) per patent application is entirely normal; first-office-action rejections happen in approximately 80–95% of all applications.
- Failing to respond to a USPTO office action results in the application being abandoned — the patent is not granted.
- Lack of novelty (102 rejections) and obviousness (103 rejections) are the most common reasons for patent rejections.
- Advertising a product as "patented" when the application was abandoned is misleading and legally problematic.
- Trademark and patent applications are separate proceedings and can have very different outcomes.
- Trademark registration also commonly involves office actions and can take 12–18+ months.
- Patent applications and trademark applications can and should be pursued simultaneously when both forms of protection are needed.
- Always confirm whether a competitor's claimed "patent" has actually issued before assuming infringement risk.
