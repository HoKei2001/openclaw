# Provisional vs Non-provisional Patent Applications

**Published**: 2023-09-13

## Overview

This video from Bold Patents Law Firm provides a clear comparison of provisional and non-provisional patent applications, helping inventors understand the strategic role of each filing type. The presenter explains that these two application types serve fundamentally different purposes and that choosing the right one — or the right sequence — can significantly affect both the cost and outcome of the patent process.

A provisional patent application is an informal, lower-cost filing that establishes a priority date and grants the inventor "patent pending" status for 12 months. It does not require formal claims, an abstract, or the formal drawings required for a non-provisional application, making it faster and cheaper to prepare. However, a provisional application is never examined and automatically goes abandoned after 12 months unless a non-provisional application is filed claiming its priority date. The video stresses that the provisional is a strategic placeholder — valuable for testing market interest or securing investment before committing to the full patent process.

A non-provisional patent application is the formal filing that undergoes substantive examination by a USPTO examiner. It must include formal patent claims (which define the legal scope of protection), an abstract, drawings that meet USPTO standards, and a complete written description. Once filed, the non-provisional application begins the examination process, which typically takes one to three years and often involves one or more office actions. If ultimately granted, the patent provides a 20-year monopoly from the non-provisional filing date. The video recommends inventors work with a patent attorney when drafting the non-provisional application, particularly the claims, as they determine the actual scope of legal protection.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional application is informal, lower cost, and establishes a 12-month priority date without formal claims.
- A non-provisional application is the formal filing that undergoes examination and can result in a granted patent.
- Provisional applications never become patents on their own — they must be followed by a non-provisional within 12 months.
- Non-provisional applications must include formal claims, an abstract, compliant drawings, and a full written description.
- The 20-year patent term for a utility patent runs from the non-provisional filing date.
- Examination of a non-provisional application typically takes 1–3 years and usually involves office actions.
- The provisional buys time to test market demand and refine the invention before investing in the full application.
- Patent claims in the non-provisional define the legal scope of protection and are the most critical part of the application.
