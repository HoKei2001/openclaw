## Overview

This video from Jon Liu's Patent Strategy Course for Inventors provides a straightforward cost comparison for obtaining a patent in the United States, contrasting the DIY (do-it-yourself) path against hiring a patent lawyer. The presenter offers a personal perspective as someone with experience navigating the patent system and also teaching others to do it themselves.

For inventors who file independently, the government fees total approximately $500–$1,000, plus the time invested in writing the patent application. Jon Liu offers a course that teaches inventors how to write and file their own patent in under 20 hours, representing a significant time investment but also significant cost savings. By contrast, hiring a lawyer for the same initial filing costs approximately $7,000–$15,000.

However, filing the application is not the end of the process. After filing, inventors must "negotiate" with the patent office — responding to office actions that outline why the patent should not be allowed, and either modifying claims or arguing back. This back-and-forth process typically takes 3–5 years depending on the technology area. Accounting for all fees over the life of the application (filing fees, allowance fees, maintenance fees, and office action responses), the total DIY cost is approximately $5,000. With a patent lawyer handling the entire process, the same patent in the U.S. typically costs around $35,000. International filings can roughly double these costs.

## Key Takeaways

- DIY patent filing: government fees $500–$1,000; total patent lifecycle cost approximately $5,000 including all USPTO fees
- Hiring a patent lawyer: approximately $7,000–$15,000 for initial filing; total lifecycle cost approximately $35,000
- After filing, expect 3–5 years of back-and-forth with the USPTO (office actions) before the patent is granted
- International patent filing can roughly double total costs compared to U.S.-only filing
- DIY patent courses can reduce attorney costs significantly — some estimate the process takes under 20 hours with proper guidance
- All of the above covers only the U.S. patent; separate filings are needed for each country of interest
- The "negotiation" phase with the patent office (prosecution) is where most time and attorney fees are spent

## Best For

Budget-conscious inventors who want a direct cost comparison between filing patents themselves versus hiring a patent lawyer, along with a realistic estimate of total patent lifecycle costs.
