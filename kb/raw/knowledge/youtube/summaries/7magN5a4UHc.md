## Overview

This official WIPO video uses clear animation to explain the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) and why it is the preferred route for inventors seeking patent protection in multiple countries. The video compares the PCT approach against the traditional Paris Convention route, where inventors must file separate applications in each target country — all within 12 months of the first filing — without yet knowing whether a patent is likely to be granted.

With the PCT, an inventor files one application, in one language, at one office, within 12 months of the original filing. This single application has the same legal effect as filing in over 150 participating countries. The PCT procedure then provides valuable patentability information through an international search and written opinion from one of the world's leading patent offices. WIPO publishes the application, informing the public and potential investors. Critically, major national fees — translation costs, local attorney fees, and national filing fees — are deferred to 30 months from the priority date, giving applicants 18 additional months compared to the traditional system.

The video notes that today approximately 60% of international patent applicants choose the PCT over the traditional route, and it is used by individual inventors, small businesses, universities, and global corporations alike. The clear message is that the PCT buys time, information, and flexibility to make informed decisions about where to invest in patent protection.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT allows one application in one language at one office to have legal effect in over 150 countries.
- The PCT application must be filed within 12 months of the first (priority) patent filing.
- An international search report and written patentability opinion are provided, helping applicants decide whether to proceed.
- WIPO publishes the PCT application, which can attract investor attention.
- Major national filing fees are deferred to 30 months — 18 months longer than the traditional Paris Convention route.
- Applicants choose which specific countries to pursue during the national phase (by 30 months).
- About 60% of inventors seeking international protection now use the PCT over the traditional direct-filing method.

## Best For

This video is most useful for individual inventors and businesses considering international patent protection who want a concise, authoritative overview of how the PCT process works and its advantages over filing separately in each country.
