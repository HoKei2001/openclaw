## Overview

This video provides a structured overview of what it costs to patent an idea in the United States, covering all three types of patents, USPTO government fees, attorney fees, and post-issuance costs. It also addresses the DIY filing option and the strategic use of provisional applications to manage costs while assessing commercial viability.

## Key Takeaways

- The USPTO recognizes three types of patents: utility patents (most common, broadest protection), design patents (appearance only, less expensive), and plant patents (asexually reproduced plant varieties).
- Basic USPTO government fees for a utility patent start at approximately $330 (filing), $540 (search), and $220 (examination) — but these are only the beginning.
- Patent attorney fees typically range from $5,000–$15,000 for simple inventions; complex technologies like biotech or software can exceed $20,000.
- DIY filing is possible but requires significant time investment for patent searches, drawings, and precise claim drafting — mistakes can result in weak patents or rejection.
- A provisional patent application costs only $60–$300 in government fees, establishes a priority date, and grants "patent pending" status for one year.
- Issue fees after approval range from $1,000–$2,000; maintenance fees are due at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after issuance, adding thousands more.
- International patent protection via PCT can cost upwards of $100,000 when accounting for translations, country fees, and local legal representation.
- Responding to office action rejections adds $1,000–$5,000 in legal fees; 97% of patents generate less revenue than they cost to obtain.
- The strategic approach for small businesses and individual inventors is to start with a provisional application and defer larger expenses until commercial viability is clearer.
