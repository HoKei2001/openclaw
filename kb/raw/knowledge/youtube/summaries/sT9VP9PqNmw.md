## Overview

This video from Dana Legal Services, presented by patent attorney Jubin, addresses one of the most stressful moments in the patent process: receiving a rejection from the USPTO. The core message is reassuring — receiving an office action is normal, expected, and not the end of the road. The video explains the three main categories of rejection and what each one means, helping inventors understand what they are facing so they can respond effectively.

The first category is a Section 101 rejection, which is a rejection based on patent eligibility — the patent office is saying that the subject matter of the claims is not the type of thing that can be patented at all. This is not a statement about whether the invention is new; it is a statement that the claims cover subject matter like abstract concepts (mathematical formulas), natural phenomena, or purely mental steps, which fall outside patentable subject matter. The second category is a formal rejection, covering errors in how the application was written and presented — typographical errors, improper claim language formatting, or claims reciting features that are not supported in the specification. These issues are generally easier to address and correct. The third category is the substantive rejection, which comes in two flavors: a Section 102 rejection for lack of novelty (where a single reference discloses everything in the claim) and a Section 103 rejection for obviousness (where the examiner combines two or more references to argue that the invention would have been obvious to a person skilled in the art).

The video also addresses the anxiety around "final rejection" — even a final rejection does not mean the application is dead. Specific procedural options exist to continue pursuing the application beyond a final rejection, and working with a patent attorney to understand and respond to all three types of rejections significantly improves the outcome.

## Key Takeaways

- Receiving an office action with a rejection is normal — almost all patent applications receive at least one rejection during examination.
- There are three main categories of rejection: Section 101 (subject matter not patent eligible), formal errors (presentational problems with the application), and 102/103 substantive rejections (prior art issues).
- A 101 rejection is not about novelty — it is about whether the subject matter qualifies for patent protection at all (abstract concepts, natural phenomena, and mental steps cannot be patented).
- Formal rejections address errors in the application's language, formatting, or claim structure and are generally correctable without major difficulty.
- A 102 rejection means a single prior art reference discloses everything in the claim; a 103 rejection means a combination of references makes the claimed invention obvious.
- Even a "final rejection" is not the end — specific procedural routes allow the application to continue being examined.
- A patent attorney is particularly valuable for understanding and responding to substantive 102 and 103 rejections, which require both legal and technical analysis.

## Best For

Inventors who have received a USPTO rejection notice and want to understand what the different types of rejections mean and what their options are for responding.
