## Overview

This short official USPTO video introduces the resources available to patent applicants who want to conduct or prepare for an examiner interview. The video is presented by Technology Center (TC) Interview Specialists from TC 2400 and TC 2600, who serve as contact points for applicants seeking guidance on the interview process at the USPTO.

The central resource highlighted is the USPTO's dedicated interview practice webpage at USPTO.gov/interviewpractice, which contains best practice guides, information about the USPTO AIR video conferencing system, and public interview room availability. A key procedural point emphasized in the video is that to conduct a video interview with a patent examiner, applicants must first submit an internet authorization form (Form SB/439) granting permission for the examiner to contact them via email. This form can be submitted through EFS-Web, central fax, U.S. postal mail, or the USPTO Customer Service Window — but not by email. The video also notes that TC Interview Specialists are available to assist applicants with questions or to help them practice video conferencing before the actual interview.

## Key Takeaways

- USPTO has a dedicated interview practice resource page at USPTO.gov/interviewpractice with guides, tools, and contacts.
- Before a video examiner interview can occur, the applicant must submit internet authorization Form SB/439.
- Form SB/439 can be submitted via EFS-Web, fax, postal mail, or the USPTO Customer Service Window — not by email.
- Technology Center (TC) Interview Specialists are available to assist applicants and help them practice video conferencing.
- The USPTO AIR video system is the recommended platform for conducting examiner interviews remotely.
- Conducting an examiner interview is a valuable prosecution tool for resolving disputes and advancing a stalled application.
- Preparing proposed claim language and arguments in advance of the interview significantly improves outcomes.

## Best For

This video is most useful for patent applicants and their attorneys who are planning to request an examiner interview and need to understand the authorization requirements and resources available to prepare.
