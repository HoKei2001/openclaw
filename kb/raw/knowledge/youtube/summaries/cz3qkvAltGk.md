## Overview

This Novel Patent Services video walks inventors through a nine-step process for converting a provisional patent application (PPA) to a non-provisional patent application (NPA), providing a practical, action-oriented checklist format suitable for inventors who are ready to begin the conversion process. The video is structured as a step-by-step guide with each stage clearly identified and explained.

The nine steps begin with verifying the provisional is still within its 12-month validity window — missing this deadline permanently forfeits the early filing date benefit. Step two is reviewing the provisional application's content for completeness and technical accuracy, with the recommendation to update and expand any sections that are unclear or incomplete. Step three is gathering the necessary documents: detailed specification, drawings if applicable, and an abstract. Step four — writing the patent claims — is highlighted as the most critical and strategic part of the process, requiring precision, thoroughness, and legal skill to define the invention's scope correctly.

Steps five through nine cover the administrative side: completing required USPTO forms (Application Data Sheet, declarations, and assignments), conducting a prior art search to refine claims and preempt rejections, paying the applicable filing fees (which vary by entity size — micro, small, or large), submitting the application through USPTO's EFS-Web or Patent Center with explicit reference to the provisional application to claim the earlier priority date, and following up by monitoring the application and responding promptly to any office actions. Professional assistance from a patent attorney or agent is recommended throughout.

## Key Takeaways

- Verify the 12-month validity window before starting the conversion process — a missed deadline means permanent loss of the early filing date
- Review the provisional for completeness and expand any unclear technical sections before drafting the non-provisional
- Patent claims are the most critical element of the non-provisional application; drafting them requires precision, strategic thinking, and legal expertise
- Required USPTO forms include the Application Data Sheet (ADS), declarations, and any assignments depending on the filing structure
- Conducting a prior art search before filing the non-provisional helps refine claims and anticipate likely rejections
- Entity size (micro, small, or large) determines the applicable USPTO filing fees — incorrect classification can cause application delays or rejection
- The non-provisional must explicitly reference the provisional application to claim the benefit of the earlier filing date
