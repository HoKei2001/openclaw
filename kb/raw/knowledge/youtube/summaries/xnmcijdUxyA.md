# After Filing Your Patent Application - Do This Next!

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell explains the critical steps inventors should take immediately after filing a patent application. For those who filed a provisional application, the 12-month window before conversion to a non-provisional is a vital period for market validation — only non-provisional applications are examined and can issue as patents. For non-provisional filers, the wait for first office action can take 18 months to two years, and initial rejection of claims is normal and expected rather than a sign of failure.

Beyond the patent process itself, Ferrell emphasizes that a patent with no underlying business is worthless. Inventors must use the post-filing period to conduct market research: identifying customers, understanding their needs and willingness to pay, developing prototypes, and securing funding. These steps apply equally whether the inventor plans to manufacture and sell the product themselves or license the invention to a third party.

The video closes with the reminder that even licensing deals require a thorough understanding of the licensee's market, costs, and profit margins in order to negotiate an optimal royalty. Filing the patent application is not the end — it is the starting gun for building the business or licensing strategy that gives the patent its real value.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional application gives 12 months to validate the market before the more expensive non-provisional filing
- Only non-provisional applications are examined and can result in an issued patent
- Initial rejection of all claims on the first office action is normal; it is the examiner doing their job
- The majority of patent applications are ultimately allowed and result in issued patents
- Post-filing time should be used for customer discovery: who will buy, why, how often, and at what price
- Prototype development — even crude cardboard or styrofoam models — clarifies product complexity and aids communication with manufacturers
- Funding strategy (self-funded, angel, bank loan) must be planned before product launch
- Licensing successfully requires understanding the licensee's market, costs, and profit margins

## Best For

Inventors who have just filed a patent application and want a clear, business-focused roadmap for what to do during the examination waiting period.
