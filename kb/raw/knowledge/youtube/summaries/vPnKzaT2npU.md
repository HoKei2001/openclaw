## Overview

This UpCounsel video provides a structured overview of patent eligibility, covering the four fundamental requirements for obtaining a patent — usefulness, novelty, non-obviousness, and patentable subject matter — along with a comprehensive survey of what can and cannot be patented. The video addresses utility, design, and plant patents separately, explains the US "first inventor to file" system, and discusses emerging fields such as biotechnology, AI, and software. It also explains the enablement requirement and how secondary factors can support a non-obviousness argument.

## Key Takeaways

- The four fundamental patent requirements are: useful purpose, novelty, non-obviousness to someone skilled in the relevant field, and patentable subject matter.
- Patentable categories include machines, processes, compositions/formulas, manufactured items, new plant varieties, and improvements to existing inventions.
- Emerging fields such as biotechnology (GMOs), medical devices, AI applications solving technical problems, and software producing tangible results may qualify — but algorithms alone, abstract ideas, laws of nature, and mathematical formulas cannot.
- Utility patents last 20 years; design patents last 15 years; plant patents last 20 years.
- The US uses a "first inventor to file" system — timing is critical and inventors should avoid public disclosure before filing.
- Provisional applications can secure an early filing date while the inventor refines the invention.
- Non-obviousness can be supported by secondary factors: commercial success, long-felt need unmet by others, and unexpected results.
- The enablement requirement demands that the application description allow someone skilled in the field to make and use the invention.
