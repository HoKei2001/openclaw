# How Much Does a Patent Cost in 2025?

## Overview

Patent attorney J.D. Houvener breaks down the full cost of obtaining and maintaining a U.S. patent in 2025, organized into three major phases: pre-filing, acquisition (prosecution), and post-grant. The video is designed to give inventors a realistic financial picture of the entire patent lifecycle so they can plan budgets appropriately rather than being surprised by escalating costs.

The pre-filing phase — including an invention disclosure consultation, professional prior art search, and attorney opinion — typically costs under $5,000. The acquisition phase, which covers preparing and filing the application, responding to office actions, conducting examiner interviews if needed, and ultimately receiving the patent grant, is the longest and most variable phase. It typically takes two to three years and costs between $1,000 and $40,000, with an average around $25,000. Complexity, the number of office actions, and whether appeals or petitions are needed drive the variability. The post-grant phase includes maintenance fees paid to the USPTO at the 3.5-, 7.5-, and 11.5-year marks (totaling approximately $7,000–$10,000 for a utility patent), plus costs for infringement opinions, validity opinions, and freedom-to-operate analyses if the patent owner wants to enforce or defend their rights. Patent litigation can add substantially more.

Adding the three phases together, Houvener estimates the average total cost of acquiring and maintaining a utility patent in 2025 is approximately $40,000.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent costs fall into three phases: pre-filing (under $5,000), acquisition/prosecution ($1,000–$40,000, average ~$25,000), and post-grant ($7,000–$10,000 in maintenance fees).
- Average total cost to acquire and maintain a utility patent in 2025 is approximately $40,000.
- The prosecution phase (from filing to grant) typically takes two to three years.
- Number of office actions, examiner interviews, and appeals are the main drivers of prosecution cost variability.
- Maintenance fees are due at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after grant to keep the patent in force.
- Enforcing patent rights adds significant additional costs: infringement opinions, validity opinions, and potentially litigation.
- Micro entity status provides a 75% discount on USPTO official fees.
- Pre-filing professional searches and attorney opinions are worth the investment to confirm patentability before committing to prosecution costs.
