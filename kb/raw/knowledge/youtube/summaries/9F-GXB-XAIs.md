# What to do FIRST With Your Invention Idea

**Channel**: Patents Demystified
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9F-GXB-XAIs
**Published**: 2021-06-15

---

## Overview

Patent attorney at Patents Demystified provides a strategic roadmap for inventors who have a new idea, arguing that filing a patent application is not the first step — and that doing it too early is one of the most common and expensive mistakes. The correct first step is market and customer research: understanding who the target customers are, whether there is sufficient demand, what competitors exist, and what makes the proposed product genuinely better. Without this research, inventors risk building or patenting products that no one will buy.

The second step is building a prototype or minimal viable product (MVP) — even a crude cardboard mock-up or a janky garage build — to learn about the product, discover unexpected complexity, and develop a proof of concept that can be refined. The presenter argues that people consistently underestimate how much is learned by actually building the thing, and that many product ideas look very different once physical prototyping begins. For software, even a mockup with fake data serves the same learning function.

Patent protection comes fourth on the list (after market research, prototyping, and planning monetization strategy), and is specifically timed to coincide with either approaching investors, making public disclosures, offering the product for sale, or conducting public demonstrations — all events that would start the clock on losing patent rights or that benefit from "patent pending" status. Filing before these events is essential; filing years before any of them is an expensive use of limited early-stage resources.

## Key Takeaways

- Filing a patent application is not the first step — market research and prototyping come first
- Determine whether the idea will make a good product: research competitors, target customers, price points, and market fit
- Use the "vitamin vs. painkiller" test: does your product solve an essential problem or is it merely nice to have?
- Build a prototype or MVP before filing — even crude, it reveals unexpected complexity and confirms feasibility
- Two monetization paths: license or sell the idea to another company, or build and sell the product yourself
- File a patent application before public disclosures, public use, offers for sale, or approaching investors
- Offers for sale (even private) can forfeit patent rights — timing matters for both public and private commercialization activities
- Spending money on patents before validating the market is premature and often wasteful

## Best For

Startup founders and individual inventors who have just conceived an invention idea and want a strategic, business-first framework for what to do before spending money on a patent application.
