## Overview

Patent attorney from King Patent Law presents five common mistakes DIY inventors make when filing provisional patent applications, with detailed explanations of why each mistake matters and specific guidance on how to avoid each one. The video is structured around the practical reality that the USPTO does not review provisional applications for quality — only for missing administrative items — which means inventors can file a legally incomplete document without realizing it until the non-provisional application is examined, often years and significant investment later.

The five mistakes are: (1) not describing the invention in sufficient detail to enable someone skilled in the field to make and use it, since any undisclosed aspects cannot claim the provisional's priority date in the non-provisional; (2) treating the provisional as a casual placeholder and allowing new matter to creep into the non-provisional that lacks basis in the provisional; (3) using overly broad or ambiguous language (phrases like "any material" or "not limited to") that sounds protective but actually weakens the disclosure's legal strength; (4) failing to include drawings or diagrams, which are almost always necessary to fully disclose an invention and can be the only evidence that a particular feature was disclosed; and (5) assuming that filing a provisional creates permanent patent pending protection, when in fact the provisional expires after exactly 12 months.

The attorney emphasizes that each mistake can be avoided with appropriate care and professional assistance, and that the provisional application is the foundation of all future patent rights — if the foundation is weak, everything built on it is at risk.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional application must enable someone skilled in the field to make and use the invention — thin descriptions permanently limit future claims
- New matter added in the non-provisional that has no basis in the provisional loses the provisional's priority date
- The USPTO does not review provisional quality — inadequate provisionals are only discovered at non-provisional examination
- Overly broad or vague language ("any material," "not limited to") weakens legal strength; be precise and specific
- Drawings are almost always necessary to fully disclose an invention; even basic hand-drawn sketches count
- A provisional expires after exactly 12 months — missing the non-provisional deadline loses all priority rights
- Public disclosure after the provisional filing but before a foreign application can block foreign filing in some countries
- The provisional application is the foundation of all future patent rights; cutting corners risks the entire patent strategy

## Best For

DIY inventors who plan to file their own provisional patent application and want to understand the five most serious and avoidable mistakes before committing to the filing.
