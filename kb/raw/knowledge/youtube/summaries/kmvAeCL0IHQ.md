# Roadmap to Filing a Patent Application

**Channel**: USPTOvideo
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kmvAeCL0IHQ
**Published**: 2015-06-08

---

## Overview

This official USPTO video provides a lighthearted but informative overview of the patent application process intended for first-time inventors. Using an engaging narrator voice and playful tone, the video explains what a patent is, who can file, what types of inventions qualify, and the broad steps involved in navigating the patent system. It is designed to help inventors understand the landscape before diving into the details.

The video explains the three types of patents — utility (for processes, machines, and compositions of matter), design (for ornamental designs), and plant (for asexually reproduced plants) — along with their protection periods: 20 years for utility and plant patents, 15 years for design patents. It emphasizes that a patent must be novel and non-obvious, and it should be practically reducible to practice. The USPTO is presented as the primary resource for all patent needs, with physical offices and an online portal.

The video encourages inventors to do a prior art search before filing, consider making a business plan, and seek the help of a registered patent agent or attorney when needed. Filing pro se (without representation) is acknowledged as possible but requires persistence and determination. The video closes by directing inventors to the USPTO's Electronic Filing System and the Inventor's Assistance Center (1-800-PTO-9199) for additional support.

## Key Takeaways

- A patent grants the inventor exclusive rights to their invention for a limited time (up to 20 years for utility patents).
- Patentable subject matter includes processes, machines, manufactured items, compositions of matter, and improvements to existing inventions.
- There are three types of patents: utility, design, and plant — each with different protection periods.
- The invention must be novel and non-obvious, and it must have a practical purpose or solve a real problem.
- Conducting a prior art search before filing is strongly recommended to confirm the invention is genuinely new.
- Developing a business plan (including market analysis and potential customers) before filing is encouraged.
- Filing without an attorney (pro se) is possible but requires significant persistence; USPTO resources are available to help.
- The USPTO Inventor's Assistance Center (1-800-PTO-9199) provides guidance for inventors navigating the process.

## Best For

First-time inventors who want a broad, accessible introduction to the US patent system, the types of patents available, and the general steps involved in filing a patent application.
