# Four Simple Tips for Writing Better Patent Claims

## Overview

This Inventor's Quick Tips episode offers four practical tips for writing patent claims, organized under the acronym DASA: Define the invention, Adjective check, Say what you mean, and Antecedent basis. The video uses a pencil with a mess-free eraser as a running example to illustrate each concept. While the presenter strongly recommends hiring a patent attorney, the video is designed to help inventors understand the claim-writing process so they can evaluate attorney work or, if necessary, attempt a basic claim themselves.

## Key Takeaways

- Tip 1 (Define): Claims must focus on what is actually new in the invention — the inventive concept — not on elements that already existed before.
- Tip 2 (Adjective check): Adjectives in claims (e.g., "abrasive," "soft," "flexible") must be quantifiable; vague adjectives without defined limits can invalidate or weaken a claim. Use scientific measures (e.g., Mohs scale, Taber abrasion test) where possible.
- Tip 3 (Say what you mean): Claims must be precise — ambiguous wording can be interpreted against the inventor. A claim's plain meaning may differ from its intended meaning, as illustrated by the "divisible by three" analogy.
- Tip 4 (Antecedent basis): Terms introduced in a claim for the first time must use "a" or "an," not "the." Subsequent references to the same term use "the." Violating this rule is a common formatting error that signals amateur drafting.
- If you write your own patent application and get a letter from the patent office telling you to hire someone, your claims likely need significant work.
- The written description supports claim terms and can help define the limits of adjectives even if not stated explicitly in the claims.
