## Overview

This short video from Daniel Orlando Patent Attorneys explains the two key patentability requirements of novelty and non-obviousness in the context of a US patent application. It clearly distinguishes between the two standards and describes how the USPTO uses them to determine whether a patent application will be granted.

## Key Takeaways

- Novelty and non-obviousness are the two primary factors the USPTO evaluates when determining whether to grant a patent.
- Novelty means that the invention is unique — it has not been previously disclosed, patented, or publicly known.
- Non-obviousness is a mechanism the patent office uses to ensure that an invention is not merely a minor or predictable variation of what already exists — it must represent a non-obvious advance over prior inventions.
- Both requirements must be satisfied for a patent application to be issued as a United States patent.
