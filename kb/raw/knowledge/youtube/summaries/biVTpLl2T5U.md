# How to Land the Licensing Deal of Your Dreams

## Overview

Stephen Key walks through the complete arc of landing a licensing deal — from first contact to signed agreement — with a focus on the attitude, sequencing, and relationship-building skills that separate successful inventors from those who almost close deals but consistently fall short. He opens by noting the most common failure mode: inventors who do everything technically right (find a good fit, build a sell sheet) but then derail the process by asking the wrong questions at the wrong time, jumping ahead in sequence, or projecting desperation or confusion to potential licensees.

The video provides a detailed script for the LinkedIn outreach phase, emphasizing that inventors should not "sell" in their initial message — simply ask for permission to submit an idea and inquire whether the company works with outside inventors. Once interest is confirmed, the next step is to get off LinkedIn and onto a Zoom or phone call to build a genuine human relationship. Stephen lists the five questions companies typically ask (IP status, prior art awareness, prototype, pricing, and costs) and advises calm, prepared responses rather than panicked over-disclosure.

The negotiation framing is a standout element of the video: Stephen argues that negotiation does not begin when a contract is presented — it begins the moment you first visited their website, filed your provisional patent application, and built your prototype. Everything before the contract is leverage, and weaknesses in those areas (a poorly written provisional, no prototype, asking out-of-sequence questions) hand leverage to the company. The video ends with a note that licensing agreements will be covered in a follow-up video.

## Key Takeaways

- Do not go through a company's submission portal; instead reach out via LinkedIn to find the right decision-maker directly
- Initial outreach should ask for permission and express curiosity — never sell, never promise billions, never ask about royalty rates on the first call
- Moving from LinkedIn to a live Zoom or phone call is the single most important step to building the relationship that closes deals
- The five questions companies always ask: do you have IP, are you aware of prior art, do you have a prototype, do you have cost estimates, and what is your pricing?
- Asking about royalty rates or minimum guarantees before genuine interest is confirmed is a red flag that will end conversations
- Negotiation begins at the start of the process — the quality of your provisional patent, prototype, and professionalism all affect your leverage
- NDAs at the early stage are typically unnecessary if you have filed a solid provisional and the company has a good reputation

## Best For

Inventors who have a well-developed product concept and provisional patent in hand and are ready to approach companies for a licensing deal for the first time.
