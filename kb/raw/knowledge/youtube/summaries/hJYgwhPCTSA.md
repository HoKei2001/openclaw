## Overview

This short-form video provides a quick, practical explanation of the difference between a patent and a trademark. The presenter contrasts the two protections using straightforward language suitable for a general audience unfamiliar with intellectual property concepts.

## Key Takeaways

- A patent is a government grant for a new invention — either a design/aesthetic invention or a utility/functional invention — that gives the inventor 15 to 20 years of exclusive monopoly rights in exchange for publicly disclosing how to make and use the invention.
- A trademark protects a brand: names, logos, word marks, and other identifiers that help customers associate goods and services with a specific source and distinguish them from competitors.
- Trademarks must be distinctive and different from existing marks to avoid customer confusion in the marketplace.
- Both protections are often needed together: a patent prevents competitors from copying the invention, while a trademark prevents them from using your brand name to sell competing products.
