# How Much Money Will You Make Licensing? (Royalties)

**Channel**: inventRightTV
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5pNsi_2H-J4
**Published**: 2016-01-26

## Overview

Stephen Key and Andrew Krauss of inventRight address one of the most common misconceptions among new inventors: that a high royalty rate is the single most important variable in a licensing deal. The conversation opens by acknowledging that many inventors arrive expecting 50% of profits or demand 20% royalty rates with large upfront payments — figures that are simply unrealistic and that regularly derail promising deals before they begin. The hosts reframe royalty rates as just one input in a calculation that also depends heavily on unit volume and product price point.

The core advice is to do the math before entering any negotiation. An inventor should estimate how many stores the licensee sells into, assume roughly one unit sold per store per week, and then run the numbers at different royalty rates (3%, 4%, 5%, 6%) to see what annual income each scenario produces. The exercise frequently reveals that the difference between a 3% and a 5% rate matters far less than the company's distribution reach. The hosts note that the average royalty rate they see across consumer product deals is approximately 5%, with rates dropping toward 3% for high-volume consumables and potentially reaching 7–8% when the inventor provides significant pre-work such as CAD drawings, testing, or tooling.

The discussion also covers the importance of minimum guarantees — contractual requirements that the licensee hit certain sales figures or return the patent — and non-exclusive licenses as a fallback when a licensee's volume is too low to justify exclusivity. The hosts contrast self-manufacturing (20% margin but 60–80 hour work weeks on 5,000–20,000 units) against licensing (5% but zero ongoing work on 500,000 units), reinforcing that the math almost always favors licensing when a well-distributed company is involved.

## Key Takeaways

- The average royalty rate in consumer product licensing is approximately 5%; demanding 10–20% upfront is a common deal-killer for new inventors
- Volume and price point matter more than rate: a 5% royalty on 500,000 units outperforms a 20% margin on 10,000 self-manufactured units
- Always build a royalty model: units × price × rate = annual income, then compare scenarios across different rate options
- Minimum guarantees — requiring the licensee to hit sales targets or return the patent — are often more valuable than squeezing for an extra percentage point
- Rates below 5% can be appropriate for high-volume consumables; rates above 7% are achievable when inventors bring finished tooling or significant development work
- Even Disney rarely exceeds 10–12% except in exceptional circumstances like peak Lion King licensing
- If a licensee's volume is too low to justify exclusivity, a non-exclusive license preserves the right to license to additional companies

## Best For

Inventors preparing for their first licensing negotiation who want a realistic, math-grounded framework for evaluating royalty rate offers before they enter the room.
