# Why Should I File A Provisional Patent Application?

**Channel**: Avoiding Common Legal Mistakes
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kqijXoEDUY0
**Published**: 2025-09-29

---

## Overview

This video explains the strategic reasons for filing a provisional patent application before disclosing or commercializing an invention. It emphasizes that the U.S. patent system is first-to-file, making the provisional application's primary function establishing an early official filing date. This date can be cited as priority against later filers of similar inventions, and it is the starting point from which the 12-month window for filing a non-provisional application begins.

The video covers the key practical benefits: the 12-month window allows time to develop and test the invention, gather data, and prepare a stronger non-provisional application, all while publicly sharing the invention, seeking investors, and promoting it as "patent pending." The patent pending status provides both a deterrent to competitors and a signal of seriousness to potential partners and investors. Provisional applications are less expensive than full non-provisional patents and require less formal documentation.

Important cautions are also addressed: the provisional application must clearly and sufficiently describe the invention — vague or incomplete provisionals weaken the claims that can be made in the subsequent non-provisional application. Filing before any public disclosure is essential, since revealing an invention without patent protection can result in losing patenting rights entirely.

## Key Takeaways

- The U.S. patent system is first-to-file; a provisional application establishes an early priority date
- Inventors get 12 months after filing to develop, test, and improve the invention without losing rights
- During the 12-month window, the invention can be publicly shared, promoted, and shown to investors
- Patent pending status deters competitors and makes the invention more attractive to investors and partners
- Provisional applications cost less and require less formal documentation than non-provisional applications
- The provisional application must clearly describe the invention — vague provisionals weaken future claims
- Filing must occur before public disclosure to preserve patenting rights
- A provisional is a starting point, not final protection — a non-provisional must follow within one year

## Best For

Inventors at an early stage who want to understand the strategic and practical benefits of filing a provisional patent application before disclosing or commercializing their invention.
