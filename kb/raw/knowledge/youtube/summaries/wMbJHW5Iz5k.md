# What it really costs to patent a product

## Overview

In this video, inventor and licensing expert Stephen Key explains the real costs of patenting a product, emphasizing that the financial cost of a patent is only one dimension — the bigger risk is patenting the wrong invention. He walks through preliminary steps inventors should take before spending money on a patent, and provides a realistic cost range for working with a patent agent vs. a large patent firm.

## Key Takeaways

- Before spending money on a patent, inventors should verify their idea is actually new through marketplace research (Google search, image search) and a thorough patent search.
- A licensing or commercialization strategy should be planned before filing — most inventions never generate revenue, and patents without a viable market path waste significant money.
- Working with a patent agent (like the one recommended in the video) can get an application filed with office actions handled for as little as $8,000.
- Working with a patent attorney at a larger firm typically costs $15,000–$30,000 total due to higher hourly rates and more comprehensive claim drafting.
- The overall cost range for patenting a product is approximately $8,000–$25,000.
- Patenting the wrong idea can lead to spending tens of thousands — or even over $100,000 — on something that never generates a return.
- A provisional patent application is a useful first step, and patent agents can also assist with that filing.
