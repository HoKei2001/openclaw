## Overview

This video from UpCounsel provides a practical breakdown of the costs associated with filing a provisional patent application, aimed at helping inventors budget realistically before starting the patent process. The presenter covers both the government filing fees and the attorney fees typically involved, and explains the entity status tiers that determine how much inventors pay to the USPTO.

The video details the USPTO's three-tier fee structure for provisional applications: micro entities (individuals earning under a certain income threshold with no more than four previous patent applications) pay $65, small entities (individuals, small businesses with under 500 employees, and qualifying nonprofit organizations) pay $130, and large entities pay $320. These fees are the minimum cost of filing. The presenter notes that while inventors can theoretically file a provisional application themselves, the quality of the provisional directly affects the scope of protection available in the later non-provisional application — which means cutting corners at this stage can be costly later.

Attorney fees for preparing a provisional application typically range from $1,500 to $3,500 depending on the complexity of the invention and the attorney's rates. The video warns inventors about common pitfalls: a poorly drafted provisional that omits important technical details cannot be used to support broader claims in the non-provisional application, since a patent claim can only claim priority to subject matter actually disclosed in the provisional. The presenter advises inventors to treat the provisional as a genuine technical disclosure rather than a placeholder filing, and to work with an attorney or at minimum have a professional review the document before filing.

## Key Takeaways

- USPTO filing fees for provisional applications: $65 (micro entity), $130 (small entity), $320 (large entity).
- Micro entity status requires meeting income limits and having filed no more than four previous patent applications.
- Attorney fees for preparing a provisional application typically range from $1,500 to $3,500.
- A provisional application establishes a priority date and grants 12 months of "patent pending" status.
- The quality of the provisional determines how broad the later non-provisional claims can be — omissions cannot be added later.
- DIY provisional filings risk losing priority to key aspects of the invention if technical details are inadequately described.
- Inventors should treat the provisional as a genuine disclosure document, not just a placeholder.
- The total cost of a provisional application (fees + attorney) ranges from under $100 (DIY) to $3,500–$4,000 (attorney-prepared).

## Best For

First-time inventors and entrepreneurs who want a realistic picture of what a provisional patent application costs and what factors affect the total investment before making a filing decision.
