## Overview

This video is a comprehensive webinar presented by USPTO Administrative Patent Judge Bill Baumeister as part of the 2025 National Patent Application Drafting Competition. It provides an in-depth tutorial on patent claims drafting, covering the rules, structure, and strategy behind writing effective patent claims. The session is designed for participants ranging from first-time drafters to more experienced practitioners.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent protection is defined solely by the claims — not the specification, drawings, or abstract — making claims the most critical part of a patent application.
- Claims must be drafted as a single sentence and should be arranged with the broadest independent claim first.
- Independent claims stand alone with all necessary limitations, while dependent claims add further limitations and incorporate by reference all elements of the claim they depend on.
- The more language added to a claim, the narrower its scope — brevity and precision are strategic necessities.
- Consistent terminology must be used throughout both the claims and the specification; terms in claims must have clear antecedent basis in the description.
- Claims can protect products/devices (apparatus/composition) or methods/processes, and the inventor's strategic goal determines which type to pursue.
- The USPTO allots three independent claims and 20 total claims per base filing fee; dependent claims are an efficient shorthand to expand coverage without repetition.
- Patent eligibility under 35 USC 101 excludes abstract ideas and mathematical formulas — only tangible, concrete inventions qualify.

## Topics Covered

- Role and legal significance of patent claims vs. specification
- Independent claims vs. dependent claims
- Claim drafting formalities and single-sentence requirement
- Types of claims: product/apparatus claims vs. method/process claims
- Claim scope and strategy (broader vs. narrower language)
- Consistent terminology and antecedent basis (35 USC 112)
- Patent eligibility considerations (35 USC 101)
- Overview of the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) structure
- Competition context: National Patent Application Drafting Competition
