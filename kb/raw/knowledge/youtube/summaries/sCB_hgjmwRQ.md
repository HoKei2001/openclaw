# About Software Patents from a Principal Engineer

## Overview

John Miller, who spent 30 years at major technology companies including Microsoft and Amazon and holds more than 30 patents, shares an insider's perspective on software patents from an engineer's viewpoint rather than a legal one. The video covers what software patents are, how they are evaluated and granted, how large tech companies use them strategically, how they are abused by patent trolls, and whether it is worth filing one as an individual developer.

A software patent describes an instantiation of an algorithm or software invention, with claims that stake out a portion of the intellectual landscape and give the patent holder legal force to demand compensation from infringers. Written patents typically run 10 to 80 pages. The evaluation process involves a patent office expert reviewing prior art and the claims, often disallowing some claims and suggesting revisions — a back-and-forth that can take six months to several years and costs approximately $25,000 from start to grant. Miller emphasizes that developers at large companies should not look at existing patents before submitting a pre-disclosure form for their own idea, since exposure to prior patents may taint the invention and invalidate a later application.

In large tech companies, patents primarily serve as defensive weapons: companies accumulate large patent portfolios and use them to negotiate cross-licensing agreements when accused of infringement, effectively granting mutual immunity. Patent trolls operate differently — buying patents from distressed or liquidated companies, then pursuing small and medium businesses that lack the resources to fight litigation, forcing them to pay "nuisance settlements" cheaper than legal defense. The three factors that make a software patent worth filing are: the technology is innovative (the bar can be surprisingly low), it has an associated revenue model, and infringement is detectable from outside the company. If infringement cannot be detected, the technology is better protected as a trade secret.

## Key Takeaways

- A software patent describes a specific instantiation of an algorithm or software invention and sets out claims that define the protected intellectual space.
- The full patent process from submission to grant typically takes six months to several years and costs approximately $25,000.
- Large tech companies use patent portfolios defensively — threatening infringement suits to force cross-licensing agreements that grant mutual use of each other's IP.
- Patent trolls acquire patents from distressed companies and target smaller firms with nuisance settlement demands that are cheaper to pay than to litigate.
- Three criteria for a software patent worth filing: innovation (even at a low bar), a path to revenue, and detectable infringement.
- If infringement cannot be detected externally, protect the technology as a trade secret rather than a patent.
- Never look at existing patents before filing a pre-disclosure form — exposure to prior patents can taint an invention and potentially invalidate a future patent.

## Best For

Software engineers and developers at technology companies who want to understand how the software patent system works in practice, whether it is worth pursuing a patent, and how large companies use patents strategically.
