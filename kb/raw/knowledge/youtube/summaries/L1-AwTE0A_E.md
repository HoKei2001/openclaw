# How to Write a Patent Application

**Channel**: J.D. Houvener / Bold Patents Law Firm
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L1-AwTE0A_E
**Published**: 2019-02-26

---

## Overview

J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm walks through the step-by-step process of writing a patent application, covering both the structural requirements and the strategic thinking behind each section. The video is framed around the provisional patent application but the principles apply to full utility applications as well. Houvener emphasizes that filing a patent application is actually step four of a larger process that begins with identifying the invention, doing a patent search, and understanding patentability.

The video covers each key component: the title (should be broad and simple, not overly specific — "liquid container closure" rather than "new silicone rubber seal for water bottles"); the written description (the heart of the application, which must explain how to make and use the invention to a "person of ordinary skill in the art," abbreviated as POSITA); the market need section (explaining what problem the invention solves and why current solutions are inadequate); and the embodiment structure (starting with the most basic, stripped-down version of the invention to capture the broadest claims, then describing alternative embodiments with different materials or configurations, then detailing the full preferred embodiment). The video stresses that the broader and more abstract the invention description, the broader the patent protection — but the more likely to face rejections.

Figures and drawings are discussed as essential tools for clarifying what words cannot fully describe. The final submission package includes the specification, claims, drawings, oath/declaration, application data sheet, information disclosure statement (IDS), and filing fees.

## Key Takeaways

- Choose a broad, simple invention title to maximize protection scope (avoid overly specific language).
- The written description must be detailed enough for a "person of ordinary skill in the art" (POSITA) to reproduce the invention.
- The market need section explains the problem being solved and why current alternatives are insufficient.
- Start the description with the simplest, most basic version of the invention to anchor the broadest claims.
- Describe alternative embodiments (different materials, configurations, use cases) to broaden the patent's scope.
- End with the full preferred embodiment, described in maximum detail with figures and diagrams.
- The more specific the claim language, the narrower the protection — balance specificity with breadth strategically.
- The full application package includes specification, claims, drawings, oath, application data sheet, IDS, and fees.

## Best For

This video is most useful for inventors who want to understand the structure and strategic logic of writing a patent application before working with an attorney or attempting to draft their own provisional.
