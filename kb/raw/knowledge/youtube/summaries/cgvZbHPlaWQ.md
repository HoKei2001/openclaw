# Provisional vs Utility Patent: 5 Reasons to File a Provisional Application First

**Published**: 2025-04-11

## Overview

This video from patent attorney John Ferrell makes the strategic case for filing a provisional patent application before a non-provisional (utility) patent application, presenting five specific situations where starting with a provisional is the smarter choice. Rather than treating the provisional vs. non-provisional decision as simply a matter of cost, the video frames it as a strategic question tied to the inventor's business situation, readiness, and goals.

The first reason to file provisional first is for pre-funded startups or solo inventors who need patent pending status quickly but cannot yet afford the full $8,000–$15,000 cost of a non-provisional application with attorney fees. The second reason is speed: when a trade show, product launch, or public disclosure is imminent, a provisional can be filed in days to establish a priority date, whereas a complete non-provisional typically requires months of preparation. The third reason is market validation: the 12-month provisional period provides time to test whether there is genuine commercial demand before committing to the full patent investment — a point the video backs with the statistic that 97% of patents never recoup their costs.

The fourth reason to start with a provisional is when the invention is still incomplete or evolving — additional embodiments and improvements discovered during the 12-month period can be included in the non-provisional, provided the core concept is already disclosed in the provisional. The fifth reason is collaboration: when an inventor needs to share detailed technical information with potential manufacturing partners, co-developers, or investors, having a pending patent application provides legal protection during those discussions and signals that the inventor is serious about IP protection. The video concludes by noting that the provisional is not always the right choice — if the invention is fully developed and the inventor is ready to file, going directly to non-provisional can save a year in the examination timeline.

## Key Takeaways

- Five strategic reasons to file a provisional first: budget constraints, speed before public disclosure, market validation, incomplete invention, and need for safe collaboration.
- A provisional can be filed quickly (days) to establish priority before a trade show, pitch, or product launch.
- The 12-month provisional period provides time to validate market demand before committing to full patent costs.
- Improvements made during the 12-month period can be incorporated into the non-provisional, strengthening the application.
- "Patent pending" status from a provisional provides legal cover when sharing technical details with partners and investors.
- 97% of patents never recoup their filing costs — market validation during the provisional period reduces the risk of wasted investment.
- If the invention is fully developed and the inventor is ready, filing non-provisional directly saves a year in the examination timeline.
- The provisional is a strategic tool, not just a cheaper alternative — the decision should match the inventor's specific situation.

## Best For

Inventors and entrepreneurs deciding whether to file a provisional or go directly to a non-provisional patent application, who want a strategic framework for making that decision based on their business readiness and goals.
