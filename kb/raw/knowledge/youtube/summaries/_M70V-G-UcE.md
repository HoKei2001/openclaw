## Overview

This concise instructional video walks through the key steps for self-filing a design patent application with the USPTO. Unlike utility patents, a design patent protects only the ornamental appearance of an object, and the application centers almost entirely on the quality and completeness of the drawings rather than a detailed written description or complex claims. The video emphasizes that drawings must clearly illustrate the design from every angle and meet USPTO formatting standards.

The filing process involves completing an application data sheet, an inventor's oath or declaration, and uploading all documents through the USPTO EFS web portal, with fees varying based on entity status (micro or small entity). After submission, the applicant receives a serial number and official filing date. The application then enters USPTO review, and the filer should be prepared to respond to office actions. The PAIR system (Patent Application Information Retrieval) allows online status monitoring.

A key point for inventors considering design patents is that they last 15 years from the issue date and require no maintenance fees — unlike utility patents, which require periodic maintenance payments to remain in force.

## Key Takeaways

- A design patent protects the ornamental appearance of an object, not its function
- Drawings are the most critical element — they must illustrate the design clearly from every angle
- The application includes only a single claim (the design as shown in the drawings)
- Required documents include drawings, a brief description, an application data sheet, and an inventor's oath or declaration
- Filing is done electronically through the USPTO EFS web portal; fees vary by entity status
- After submission, applicants receive a serial number and official filing date
- Application status can be monitored online via the PAIR (Patent Application Information Retrieval) system
- Design patents last 15 years from issue date with no maintenance fees required
