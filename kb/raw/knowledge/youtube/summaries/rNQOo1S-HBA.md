## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Farrell answers frequently asked questions about provisional patent applications, covering when to file, what gets examined, how to hire an attorney, how long preparation takes, and what drawings are required. The video takes a conversational Q&A format drawn from viewer comments and inbox questions. It provides practical, reassuring guidance for inventors who are new to the patent process.

## Key Takeaways

- Filing a provisional patent application before sharing your invention with manufacturers or investors is strongly recommended, though not legally required before disclosure
- A provisional patent application is never formally examined by the USPTO — it only gets reviewed if there is a future dispute about your invention's priority date (usually in litigation years later)
- The best way to find a patent attorney is through referrals from colleagues, other inventors, or companies in your industry — chemistry and comfort are key factors
- Provisional applications can be prepared very quickly (sometimes in a day) and require no formal drawings — hand sketches, photographs, PowerPoint decks, and even napkin sketches are acceptable
- Always include more figures and embodiments than you think you need; it is far better to have extra information than to be missing a critical detail years later
- Including at least one simple patent claim in your provisional is not required but helpful for remembering what the key points of novelty are when converting to a non-provisional application
