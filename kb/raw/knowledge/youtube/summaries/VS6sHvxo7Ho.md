## Overview

This short introductory video explains the concept and purpose of a patentability search (also called a novelty search or prior art search). The presenter defines it as a search to identify all prior publications and existing patents in a given domain that are relevant to an invention, conducted before filing to assess the likelihood of obtaining a patent grant. The search reveals what is already in the public domain — whether patented or non-patented — so inventors can avoid filing applications for inventions that are not novel.

The video describes two approaches to conducting these searches. The first is through free databases such as Google Patents, Espacenet, and WIPO PatentScope, where inventors can run keyword searches to explore what already exists. The second is through professional paid databases such as Orbit Intelligence and Derwent Innovation, which offer more powerful search capabilities for those conducting thorough commercial-grade searches.

A key benefit emphasized is strategic: if prior art is found, inventors can modify their claims to distinguish the invention and improve the odds of grant. Even partial prior art disclosure allows targeted claim drafting around what is actually new, resulting in a stronger, more defensible patent application.

## Key Takeaways

- A patentability search identifies all prior art (patents and non-patent literature) to assess the chances of obtaining a patent
- The search should be done before filing to avoid wasting time and money on non-novel inventions
- Free databases include Google Patents, Espacenet, and WIPO PatentScope
- Professional paid databases such as Orbit Intelligence and Derwent Innovation offer more powerful search capabilities
- Finding prior art allows claim modification to distinguish the invention and improve the chances of grant
- A patentability search is useful both for deciding whether to file and for drafting stronger claims
- Even inventors in their own field of expertise cannot know all relevant prior art worldwide
- The search outcome enables inventors to shape a grantable patent application from the outset

## Best For

Inventors and innovators who are new to the concept of prior art search and want a clear, concise explanation of why and how patentability searches are conducted before filing a patent application.
