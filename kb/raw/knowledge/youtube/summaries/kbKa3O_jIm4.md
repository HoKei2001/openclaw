## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell introduces a comprehensive video series designed to guide inventors through the entire process of writing and filing their own provisional patent application. The video serves as an overview and roadmap for the series, outlining what each subsequent video will cover. Ferrell positions the series as appropriate for inventors who are at an early stage, do not yet have funding for a patent attorney, or simply want to understand the process before engaging professional help.

The series promises to cover: how to determine if an invention is patentable (including what questions to ask), how to perform a simple three-step patent search, the business considerations behind getting a patent (whether it is worth it for a given invention), the detailed steps of preparing and writing a provisional patent application (what to include and what must not be omitted), and finally a step-by-step demonstration of electronically filing the application at the USPTO portal.

Ferrell also promotes his book "Provisional Patents," available on Amazon in both Kindle and print editions, as a companion resource with additional examples and detail. The video series is explicitly directed at inventors who need some protection quickly — whether because the product still needs market testing, the invention is not fully developed, or they simply cannot afford a patent attorney at this stage.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application is appropriate when a product needs market testing, the invention is not fully developed, or funding for a full patent is not yet available.
- The series covers patentability assessment, patent searching, business case evaluation, writing the application, and electronic filing.
- Ferrell's book "Provisional Patents" (available on Amazon) provides a deeper companion resource to the video series.
- Provisional applications are informal, allowing for misspelled words, handwritten content, sketches, and photographs.
- Provisional applications are never examined, but courts may review them later to confirm that the priority date is supported.
- The series is designed for pro se inventors (those filing without legal representation) who want professional-grade guidance.
- The USPTO's Patent Center portal is used for electronic filing of provisional applications.

## Best For

Inventors who want a complete, attorney-guided introduction to the DIY provisional patent process and are looking for a structured series of videos to follow from start to finish.
