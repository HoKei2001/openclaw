## Overview

This video from Innovent Law, presented by registered patent attorney Kima Gulik, provides a practical checklist for inventors and companies who want to license their patents. The video is directed at patent holders who have protection in place but are unsure how to translate that protection into licensing revenue.

The first and most important step is understanding exactly what you own. Gulik observes that many sophisticated companies and inventors believe they have patent protection on something when in reality they do not, or they do not understand the true scope of their claims. Sitting down with a patent attorney to review each claim, understand which specific elements are covered, and identify what is not covered is the essential foundation for any licensing strategy. The second step is building a list of potential licensees — not just direct competitors but also companies in adjacent market segments that could benefit from the technology. To deepen this research, Gulik recommends conducting patent searches on these potential licensees to understand whether they are actively building their IP portfolio and whether they appear likely to be interested in acquiring or licensing external technology.

Gulik also addresses the practical challenge of outreach — if the inventor is not comfortable making direct contact with potential licensees, licensing agents are available who handle that work in exchange for a percentage of any resulting deal. She emphasizes the importance of flexibility in negotiations, focusing on building long-term relationships rather than maximizing short-term gains, and maintaining compliance throughout the licensing period: patents must be actively maintained through timely fee payments, continuation applications should be filed before the initial patent issues to extend the portfolio, and the patent attorney should be informed of the patent holder's commercial goals so the overall IP strategy aligns with the business direction.

## Key Takeaways

- Before approaching any potential licensee, review your patent claims with an attorney to truly understand what your patent does and does not protect.
- Build a targeted list of potential licensees including direct competitors and companies in adjacent market segments.
- Conduct patent searches on potential licensees to gauge their IP expansion activity and likelihood of interest in licensing your technology.
- If direct outreach is uncomfortable, licensing agents can handle that work in exchange for a commission on any resulting deal.
- Approach licensing negotiations with flexibility and a long-term relationship mindset rather than focusing on maximum short-term gain.
- Maintain your patent portfolio actively: pay maintenance fees on time and file continuation applications before the initial patent issues to maintain ongoing coverage.
- Share your commercial goals with your patent attorney so that IP strategy and business strategy remain aligned.

## Best For

Patent holders — whether individual inventors or startup founders — who want a practical, step-by-step framework for turning their issued patent into licensing revenue.
