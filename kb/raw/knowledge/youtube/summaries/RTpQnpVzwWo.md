# Using Patent Examiner Interviews to help accelerate the patent application process.

**Channel**: Inventor's Quick Tips
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RTpQnpVzwWo
**Published**: 2020-08-04

---

## Overview

This practical guide from Inventor's Quick Tips explains how to use examiner interviews — direct conversations with the USPTO patent examiner assigned to your application — to accelerate the path toward patent allowance. Examiner interviews allow applicants to discuss rejections, propose amendments, and get real-time feedback before formally filing a written response, potentially saving multiple rounds of office actions and months of additional delay.

The video identifies the ideal timing for an interview: after receiving an office action containing rejections, but before filing the formal written response. This way, any useful guidance from the examiner can be incorporated into the response, avoiding another rejection. Interviews can be arranged by calling the examiner directly using the phone number listed at the bottom of the office action, or through the online scheduling link also included in the office action. Telephone interviews are the most common format; video conferences are available when demonstrations are needed.

Preparation is key: the video recommends preparing a written agenda focused on the key sticking points, drafting proposed claim amendments in advance, and writing brief supporting remarks explaining how the amendments differ from the cited prior art. These materials should be sent to the examiner at least three business days before the scheduled interview. Pro se inventors (those without an attorney) should identify themselves as such — most examiners will be more patient and helpful in that situation.

## Key Takeaways

- Request an examiner interview after receiving an office action but before filing your written response.
- Contact the examiner by phone or through the online scheduling system linked in the office action.
- Telephone interviews are the standard format; in-person interviews are largely suspended post-COVID.
- Prepare an agenda, proposed amended claims, and brief supporting remarks before the interview.
- Send prepared materials to the examiner at least three business days in advance.
- The best typical outcome is the examiner indicating your amendment "helps move the case forward" — not a guarantee of allowance.
- If the examiner says your amendment doesn't help, ask: "Which direction seems most promising for moving the case forward?"
- Pro se inventors should tell the examiner they are representing themselves — most examiners will provide additional guidance.

## Best For

Inventors and patent practitioners who are navigating office action rejections and want to use examiner interviews strategically to speed up the path to patent allowance.
