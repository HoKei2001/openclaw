## Overview

Bold Patents founder J.D. Houvener explains the key differences between provisional and non-provisional utility patent applications in a concise, accessible format aimed at inventors and entrepreneurs. The video covers what each application type is, why both confer "patent pending" status, the one-year provisional window, and the strategic importance of using the provisional period to prototype, iterate, and refine the invention before the formal non-provisional filing.

## Key Takeaways

- Both provisional and non-provisional applications are types of utility patent applications; utility patents protect how something works (functional claims), lasting up to 20 years.
- A provisional application is informal — no formal claims required, no formal drawings required — but the written description must be detailed enough for someone skilled in the field to make and use the invention.
- Both application types grant "patent pending" status, which is the same phrase regardless of type.
- The provisional locks in the priority date for one calendar year, with no extensions; the non-provisional must be filed (ideally by the 9–10 month mark) to keep the application alive.
- The one-year provisional window is a strategic opportunity to prototype, test the market, meet investors, and refine the invention before committing to formal claims.
- A common mistake is filing a non-provisional before the product has stabilized — claims should match the product you end up selling.
- Design patents protect aesthetics only; plant patents cover novel plant species — these are different from utility patents.
