## Overview

In this short clip, "Tom the Inventor in Paradise" provides a candid breakdown of the minimum government fees required to obtain and maintain a utility patent in the United States. He frames his explanation around the reality that most inventors, when they ask "what does a patent cost?", mean a utility patent — and regardless of whether the invention is a revolutionary computer or a simple toothpick, the minimum out-of-pocket government fees add up to at least $5,385 over the life of the patent.

Tom breaks down the fees step by step: the initial filing requires an application fee ($165), a search fee ($270), and an examination fee ($110), totaling $545 at filing. Upon allowance, the inventor pays an issuance fee ($755) and a publication fee ($300), bringing the total to $1,600 before the patent is even granted. Maintenance fees then follow: $490 at the 3.5-year mark, $1,240 at the 7.5-year mark, and $2,055 at the 11.5-year mark — summing to the $5,385 minimum total. This does not include attorney or agent fees, drawing fees, or costs for office action responses or amendments.

Tom also addresses the fundamental purpose of a utility patent: it gives the owner the right to exclude others from making, using, or selling the patented invention — essentially, the right to pursue litigation against infringers. He notes that even with the patent, enforcing it requires additional legal fees. A teaser for the next clip indicates he will cover how inventors can lose their patent even after paying for it.

## Key Takeaways

- Minimum government fees for a complete utility patent lifecycle (through 11.5 years of maintenance): approximately $5,385
- Initial filing fees: $545 (application $165 + search $270 + examination $110)
- Allowance fees: $1,055 (issuance $755 + publication $300)
- Maintenance fees: $490 (3.5 years) + $1,240 (7.5 years) + $2,055 (11.5 years) = $3,785
- A utility patent grants the right to exclude others — but enforcing it requires additional legal spending
- Attorney fees, drawing costs, and office action responses are all on top of these government fees
- These fees apply regardless of invention complexity — the same structure for a simple toothpick as for a sophisticated computer

## Best For

Inventors who want a quick, concrete minimum cost figure for the government fees involved in obtaining and maintaining a utility patent, without attorney fee variables.
