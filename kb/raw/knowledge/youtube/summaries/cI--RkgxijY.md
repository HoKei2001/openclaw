## Overview

This video provides a detailed breakdown of the total costs involved in obtaining a US utility patent, from the initial novelty search through issuance and maintenance fees. The presenter, a patent attorney, emphasizes an affordability strategy of staging costs over time — starting with a provisional application to reserve rights while assessing commercial viability before committing to the full expense of prosecution.

## Key Takeaways

- Total utility patent costs can range from $10,000 to $40,000 depending on complexity and the strength of prosecution — but costs can be managed strategically.
- A novelty/patentability search costs $600–$4,000; inventors should do an informal self-search on Google Patents first before hiring professionals.
- A well-written provisional patent application costs roughly 20% less than a non-provisional — not 50% less as some cheap-service providers advertise.
- Non-provisional patent application drafting costs $8,000–$25,000 depending on complexity (mechanical vs. software/electrical inventions).
- If a quality provisional is filed first, converting to a non-provisional should cost only an additional $2,000–$3,000 for drawings, government fees, and the formal claim set.
- Examination responses to office actions cost $1,000–$5,000 each; budget $5,000–$10,000 for the examination stage overall.
- Conducting a telephone or video interview with the examiner — while slightly more expensive upfront — typically reduces total prosecution costs by resolving issues faster.
- Upon allowance, an issue fee of $1,000–$2,000 is required, followed by maintenance fees at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years totaling $1,000–$3,000 each.
- Filing the provisional first delays downstream costs by about a year, making the overall process more financially manageable.
