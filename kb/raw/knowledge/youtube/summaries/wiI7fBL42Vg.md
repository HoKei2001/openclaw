# How to Profit from Your Patent: Selling, Licensing, and Protecting Your Invention

## Overview

This UpCounsel video provides a comprehensive guide to monetizing a patent, covering the three main strategies — marketing and selling the product yourself, selling the patent outright, and licensing usage rights — along with the practical steps, tradeoffs, and marketing tactics associated with each approach.

Selling a patent provides a one-time payment that can fund other projects or cover expenses, but permanently forfeits all future revenue from the invention. The video cautions inventors against selling too quickly or at too low a price by emphasizing patent valuation: the relevant factors include the size of the target market, existing and potential demand, whether the technology is disruptive or incremental, comparable market activity for similar inventions, and the current stage of commercialization. For most inventors, licensing is described as the more profitable long-term path. When licensing, the inventor retains ownership and earns royalty payments on sales, typically at rates between 5 and 20 percent. Three licensing models are explained: exclusive licenses (one licensee gets exclusive rights in a defined field or region), non-exclusive licenses (multiple companies can use the patent simultaneously), and cross-licensing (two companies license patents to each other). The licensee generally assumes product liability, which reduces risk for the inventor.

The video provides detailed guidance on how to find licensees and create a compelling licensing package, which should include a brief business plan, technical specifications, test data, and a working prototype where possible. Inventors should approach potential partners as product developers rather than inventors, participate in trade shows, and use patent brokers with caution — never pay upfront fees, and vet any broker through the United Inventors Association and Better Business Bureau. Non-disclosure agreements and provisional patent applications are important tools for protecting ideas during early-stage conversations.

## Key Takeaways

- The three main ways to profit from a patent are: selling it outright (one-time payment, forfeit future rights), licensing it (retain ownership, earn ongoing royalties), or commercializing the product yourself.
- Patent valuation depends on market size, demand, how disruptive the technology is, comparable transactions, and the current stage of development.
- Royalty rates typically range from 5 to 20 percent, requiring significant sales volume to generate substantial income.
- Licensing models include exclusive (one licensee), non-exclusive (multiple licensees), and cross-licensing (mutual patent exchange between companies).
- A licensing package should include a business plan summary, technical specifications, test data, and a working prototype if available.
- Approach potential licensees as a product developer, not an inventor; attend trade shows and use patent brokers only with careful vetting and never with upfront fees.
- Filing a provisional patent application provides 12 months of patent pending status, enabling marketing conversations before a full patent is granted.

## Best For

Patent holders and inventors who want a practical framework for deciding how to monetize their patent and understanding the mechanics of patent sales, licensing negotiations, and marketing to potential buyers or licensees.
