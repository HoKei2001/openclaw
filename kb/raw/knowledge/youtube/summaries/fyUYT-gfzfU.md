## Overview

inventRightTV co-founder Andrew Krauss challenges the common belief that a patent is a prerequisite for licensing an invention. Drawing on years of experience coaching inventors, he argues that while having a patent is ideal, it is not strictly necessary to begin licensing an idea — and that the patent protection most companies would enforce in court is largely theoretical for most product categories.

The video outlines what inventors actually need to license successfully. First, the idea must be genuinely new and have a clear point of difference compared to competing products — verified through a Google image search and a Google Shopping search. Second, inventors must target industries where companies actively embrace open innovation and seek ideas from outside inventors, such as toys, pet products, kitchen gadgets, and hardware. The tech and packaging industries are specifically identified as difficult and generally unfavorable for outside inventors.

The third key requirement is filing a provisional patent application (PPA), which provides patent pending status for one year and signals seriousness to potential licensees. The fourth is doing enough homework on target companies to ensure your product is a strong, natural fit in their existing product line. Finally, inventors must present themselves professionally and knowledgeably, as companies evaluate the inventor as much as the idea.

## Key Takeaways

- A full patent is not required to license an idea, though most companies prefer patent pending status.
- Your idea must be genuinely new with a clear point of difference compared to existing marketplace products.
- Target industries where companies actively want outside ideas (toys, pets, kitchen, hardware); avoid tech and packaging.
- Filing a provisional patent application is strongly recommended — it gives one year of patent pending status at low cost.
- During the one-year provisional period, you can negotiate for the licensee to cover the cost of the non-provisional patent application.
- Research potential licensees thoroughly to ensure your product is a great — not just possible — fit for their product line.
- Approach companies as a knowledgeable partner; presenting yourself as uninformed will cause companies to disengage.

Inventors with a consumer product idea who want to understand the licensing process and what the minimum viable requirements are before spending significant money on a full patent application.
