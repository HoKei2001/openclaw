How do you file a design patent
application?
Imagine holding a product that looks
unique and stylish, but you want to keep
others from copying its appearance.
Filing a design patent can protect that
visual look. Let's walk through how to
do it step by step. First, you need to
prepare detailed drawings or photographs
of your design from multiple angles.
These images must clearly show all the
visual features you want protected.
It's best to use professional patent
illustrators to ensure they meet the
United States patent and trademark
office standards. Next, perform a patent
search to see if similar designs already
exist. This step helps you avoid wasting
time and money on a design that isn't
new. You can do this yourself or hire a
patent attorney to help identify prior
art. Then draft your application
carefully.
It should include a title that describes
your design, a preamble stating it's for
a design patent, a description of the
figures or views in your drawings, and a
single claim that defines the design.
You also need to include the drawings or
photographs, and an abstract summarizing
your design. Make sure your application
follows the format rules set by the
patent office.
When your application is ready, submit
it electronically through the United
States patent and trademark offic's
electronic filing system. This method is
faster, provides confirmation, and
allows you to track your application
easily. You can also mail or handover
your application, but these options take
longer. After submission, a patent
examiner reviews your application to
verify that your design is new and
non-obvious.
They might send you office actions
asking for clarifications or amendments.
Respond promptly within the deadlines to
keep your application alive. It's wise
to work with a patent attorney during
this phase to handle any objections
effectively. If everything is in order
and your design meets all requirements,
the patent office will grant you a
design patent. This gives you exclusive
rights to your design for 15 years from
the date of grant. Unlike utility
patents, design patents do not requiring
ongoing maintenance fees. Protecting
your visual design with a patent can
prevent competitors from copying your
products look and strengthen your
overall intellectual property portfolio.
To sum up, filing a design patent
involves preparing detailed images,
conducting a prior art search, drafting
a precise application, submitting it
electronically, and actively managing
the review process.
Working with a professional can make
this process smoother and more
successful.
