I think the most common mistake in creating
a provisional patent application for a new
inventor is under-inclusion.
You have to think of the document as a word
bank and you gotta have all the words in there
that you’re ever gonna want to claim down
the road…
The common mistakes that I see in provisional
filings are not describing the invention in
enough technical detail.
Really a provisional is only as good as it
enables someone to make and use the technology,
because that’s the threshold that the Patent
Office is going to be looking at for whether
or not this can serve as a priority document.
You want to describe the invention in enough
detail and in enough variations so that you
get a little broader protection, but you’re
digging deep in you’re kind of depth of
description.
If you invented the pen with blue ink, you
don’t want a pen with blue ink, you want
a pen with any kind of ink or a writing implement
with any kind of writing modality.
You have to think at the early stage of writing
the patent, not just what is my invention,
but what will it compete with and what alternatives
to it would I be offended if I saw them come
into my competitive marketplace.
The patent has to be created with that in
mind, so that you can create a barrier around
a competitive marketplace, not merely protect
your invention.
One thing you can do today with provisional
practice, is you can file multiple provisionals,
you can re-file it once a week or once a month
or once quarter, adding in additional things
that you’ve thought of after you filed it.
There is some good news that you can evolve
the document as you go through time, but make
sure that you disclose the invention as fully
as you possibly can.
