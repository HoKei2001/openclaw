Have you ever wondered how inventors
ensure their brilliant new ideas are
truly unique before investing
significant time and money?
Finding relevant prior art on the United
States Patent and Trademark Office
website or USPTO primarily involves
using their comprehensive patent search
databases and applying systematic search
strategies. This crucial step helps
confirm the novelty of your invention.
A thorough prior art search is essential
because it directly addresses the
patentability requirement set by the
USPTO.
Patents are granted for inventions that
are novel, useful, and non-obvious.
Prior art includes any public disclosure
of an invention before a certain date,
such as existing patents, published
applications, or other technical
literature, and it helps determine if
your invention already exists or is
obvious in light of what is already
known. To begin your search, start by
brainstorming keywords that describe
your invention and its function,
components, and intended use. Next,
identify relevant cooperative patent
classification or CPC codes which are a
powerful way to categorize inventions by
technology area. You can find these
using the CPC scheme on the USPTO
website.
Once you have your keywords and CPC
codes, navigate to the USBTO's patent
full text and image database or PFT ID.
Here you can conduct searches using
boolean operators like and or and not
combined with field searching to target
specific parts of a patent such as the
abstract, claims, or detailed
description. Analyze your results
carefully, paying close attention to the
claims and drawings of similar patents.
Remember to also expand your search
beyond patents to include non-patent
literature like scientific papers,
technical journals, and product manuals
as these can also constitute prior art.
Leveraging the cooperative patent
classification system or CPC is an
insider tip for powerful searching. The
CPC is a detailed hierarchical
classification system used by both the
USPTO and the European Patent Office to
classify patent documents according to
their technical features. Using this
system can significantly narrow down
your search and reveal highly relevant
documents that might be missed with
keyword-only searches.
By systematically researching prior art,
you empower yourself with knowledge,
saving valuable resources and
strengthening your potential patent
application. This proactive approach
ensures your innovative ideas stand the
best possible chance of being protected
as truly novel contributions to the
world.
