patent. First, understand that a
provisional application secures an early
filing date for your invention. It gives
you 12 months to develop your idea
further and prepare a more formal
non-provisional application. This
initial step is less formal, requiring
no claims or inventor declarations,
making it a cost-effective way to
establish your priority. To prepare your
application, begin by thoroughly
describing your invention. Include
detailed drawings that illustrate its
various aspects. Make sure to list all
inventors and their specific
contributions. Your description must be
clear enough for someone skilled in the
art to understand and replicate your
invention. Organize all these documents
carefully before proceeding to the next
step. Next, you'll file your application
using the USPTO electronic filing system
known as EFS web. You'll need to
complete a cover sheet providing
essential details about your invention.
Then upload your prepared specification,
drawings, and any other supporting
documents. Finally, pay the required
filing fee. Remember, this fee can vary
depending on whether you are a micro,
small, or large entity. After
successfully filing, you will receive an
official filing receipt, which includes
your application's serial number. Your
provisional application remains
confidential for its entire 12-month
lifespan. During this period, you can
confidently mark your invention with
patent pending. Remember, you must file
a non-provisional patent application
within one year from your provisional
filing date to maintain your priority.
