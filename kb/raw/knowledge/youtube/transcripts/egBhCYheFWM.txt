How to convert a provisional patent
application.
Are you curious about how to convert a
provisional patent application into a
nonprovisional patent application? This
process is essential for anyone looking
to secure patent rights for their
invention. Let's break down the steps
you need to follow to make this
conversion happen smoothly.
First off, it is important to understand
the difference between a provisional
parent application and a non-provisional
parent application. A provisional parent
application is a temporary filing that
establishes an early filing date.
However, it does not get examined or
mature into a patent on its own. It
expires after 12 months. On the other
hand, a non-provisional patent
application is formal and undergoes
examination, which can lead to an issued
patent. To convert your provisional
application, you must file the
non-provisional application within 12
months of the provisional application's
filing date. Missing this deadline means
losing the priority date and potentially
jeopardizing your patent rights. The
non-provisional application should fully
describe your invention, including
detailed specifications, claims that
define the scope of your invention, and
formal drawings if applicable.
When filing the non-provisional
application, you must reference the
provisional application. This means
explicitly claiming the benefit of the
earlier filing date by including the
provisional application's filing number
and date. This step is crucial to
preserve your priority date.
You will also need to submit required
fees which typically include a filing
fee, search fee, and examination fee for
the non-provisional application.
Additionally, an inventor's oath or
declaration must be submitted affirming
the originality and ownership of your
invention.
It is vital to ensure consistency
between the provisional and
non-provisional applications. The
content of the non-provisional
application must be supported by what
was disclosed in the provisional
application. If you include new
information in the non-provisional
application that was not in the
provisional, that new content will not
benefit from the earlier filing date.
Consider seeking professional guidance
during this process. Consulting a patent
attorney or agent can help you avoid
mistakes that might undermine your
patent protection.
In some cases, you may file a formal
request for conversion of the
provisional application to a
non-provisional application with the
appropriate fees. However, the standard
practice is to file a new
non-provisional application that claims
priority to the provisional.
Lastly, keep in mind the consequences of
not converting your application. If you
do not convert within the 12-month
window, the provisional application will
expire without patent rights, leaving
your invention unprotected.
To sum it up, converting a provisional
patent application involves filing a
complete non-provisional patent
application within 12 months,
referencing the provisional filing,
paying all required fees, submitting
necessary declarations, and ensuring
that the non-provisional application
fully supports the invention as
disclosed in the provisional.
Following these steps will help you
transition from a temporary filing to a
robust patent application that can be
examined and potentially granted patent
protection.
