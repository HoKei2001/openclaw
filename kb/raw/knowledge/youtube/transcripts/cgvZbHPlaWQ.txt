Before diving in to which patent application we

a minute to distinguish the two.
A patent is a monopoly to exclude others
from making, using, or selling your invention for
a period of some years.
In the United States, a utility patent lasts
for 20 years from the date that you
file the utility patent application.
The government grants this monopoly in exchange for
the inventor taking the time to write down
the invention and to submit it and register
the invention in the patent office.
Once the patent expires, that's 20 years after
the filing of the utility patent application, then
the public is free to use the invention
described in the patent.
We say that the invention enters the public
domain and of course the benefit to society
is that the public knowledge increases with the
increasing number of patents and inventions that are
submitted and recorded in the patent office.
In some sense, we can think of the
patent office as a repository for all human
knowledge, everything that's been invented in the past
eventually to be stored and publicly available in
the patent office.
Now a provisional patent application isn't really a
patent, instead it's an option to file a
patent at a later time.
When you file a provisional patent application, you're
reserving the invention date as of the date
that you file.
That is, you're telling the public that I
have this invention as of this date and
so long as you file a non-provisional
utility patent application within 12 months of the
filing of your provisional patent application, then you
get the benefit of that original provisional filing
date.
We call this filing date the priority date
and the benefit of this priority date is
that it gives you priority over other inventors
who come up with the same invention later.
In the United States, as in most other
countries of the world, whoever files their patent
application first gets the benefit of priority over
others who invent later.
Sometimes we call this system a race to
the patent office.
So there is benefit to getting your patent
application on file as soon as your invention
is ready.
Now it's important to point out that a
provisional patent application will never be examined.
It's a placeholder application.
It only gets reviewed, it only gets looked
at later in case there is some dispute
in the future as to whether or not
you actually had the invention at the time
of filing of the provisional patent application.
So it's important to understand that when you
file a provisional patent application, a utility patent
will never be granted from that provisional application
unless within 12 months you convert that provisional
patent application into a utility patent application.
Now some of the benefits of filing a
provisional patent application are that the patent application
is informal.
It can be filed quickly.
It often costs much less to file a
provisional patent application than a utility patent application.
If you need to protect the priority date
of your application, that is of your invention,
