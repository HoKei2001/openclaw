## Overview

This Intellectual Coolie course provides a comprehensive, lecture-style introduction to international patent filing, covering both the Paris Convention route and the PCT route in detail. The video opens by clarifying a common misconception: there is no such thing as a single "international patent" or "global grant" — what the term refers to is the use of international filing systems (PCT or Paris Convention) to initiate separate patent applications in multiple countries, each of which must be independently examined and granted by its own national or regional patent office.

The Paris Convention route allows inventors to file patent applications in any member country within 12 months of their first (priority) filing date, preserving the original priority date in each country filed. The PCT route offers a more powerful alternative: a single PCT application filed within 12 months of the priority date initiates an international phase that extends the deadline for entering the national phase to 30 months from priority. This 30-month window is the PCT's primary advantage — it gives applicants time to assess market potential in different jurisdictions, raise funds for national phase filing fees, and make more informed decisions about which countries to pursue.

The video covers PCT Chapter I (mandatory: International Search Authority review, International Search Report issued) and Chapter II (optional: demand for International Preliminary Examination for a supplementary patentability assessment before national phase entry). National phase entry is explained in detail: each selected country conducts its own examination, requires compliance with its own rules and language requirements, and makes its own grant decision independently — the PCT search report may be referenced but is not binding. Filing strategy considerations include the choice of receiving office, the choice of International Search Authority (which affects both fees and search quality), and the availability of fee reductions for electronic filing.

## Key Takeaways

- There is no global patent grant — "international patent" refers to filing through PCT or Paris Convention, with each national office independently deciding grants
- Paris Convention route: file in each country directly within 12 months of the priority date, preserving the original filing date
- PCT route: file a single application within 12 months of priority, then have up to 30 months from priority to enter national phase in chosen countries
- The 30-month PCT deadline is the key advantage — it provides time to assess market potential, raise funds, and make country selection decisions
- PCT Chapter I is mandatory (International Search Report); Chapter II is optional (International Preliminary Examination for a patentability opinion before national phase)
- National phase entry still requires compliance with each country's specific rules, language requirements, and examination — the PCT report is advisory, not binding
- A foreign attorney is not required for the PCT filing stage; a local attorney who filed the home application can handle PCT filing, but foreign attorneys are needed for national phase entry in each country
- PCT filing typically costs $15,000–$25,000 for the international phase; national phase costs are additional per country
