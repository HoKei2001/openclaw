## Overview

The Patent Guru provides an in-depth tutorial on how to conduct a patentability search, covering both the strategic decision of whether to search at all and the practical mechanics of how to search major patent databases. The video distinguishes patentability searches (determining if an invention can be patented by searching all public sources) from infringement searches or freedom-to-operate searches (determining if a specific issued patent's claims cover your product), which search only issued patent databases.

On the question of whether to conduct a search, the presenter takes a balanced view: the USPTO charges a search fee as part of every patent application, so you are already paying for an official search. However, doing your own preliminary search beforehand can help you write a more precise and strategically targeted patent application. The cost of a professional patentability search ranges from $500 to $6,000 depending on the number of databases and depth of analysis. Key databases covered include the USPTO Public Patent Search, Google Patents, and various paid third-party subscription databases.

The tutorial demonstrates live searching on the USPTO Public Patent Search tool, showing how to combine keywords, use Boolean operators (AND/OR/NEAR), and refine searches based on results. The presenter explains that patent applications are not published until 18 months after their priority date, meaning a search may miss recently filed applications that could still be cited against your application later. Viewers are reminded that any references found during a personal search must be disclosed to the USPTO in an Information Disclosure Statement.

## Key Takeaways

- A patentability search looks at all public sources (patents, publications, journals) to evaluate whether your invention can be patented.
- This is different from an infringement or freedom-to-operate search, which only looks at issued patent claims.
- You are already paying the USPTO to conduct a search as part of your filing fees — doing your own search is optional but can improve your application strategy.
- Professional patentability searches cost $500–$6,000 depending on database coverage and attorney review time.
- Key free databases: USPTO Patent Public Search and Google Patents; paid third-party databases offer more sophisticated tools.
- Patent applications are not published until 18 months after filing, so recent applications may not appear in your search results.
- Any references found in your own search must be disclosed to the USPTO in an Information Disclosure Statement.
- If you are very familiar with the technical field, your existing knowledge may be as good as a formal patentability search.

## Best For

Inventors and patent applicants who want to understand how and whether to conduct a preliminary patent search before filing, and how to use the USPTO and Google Patents databases effectively.
