# Write Like a Pro: Insider Tricks to Patent Claims

## Overview

This video by Jon Liu provides a concise software-focused guide to patent claim types and structure for inventors in the technology sector. The presenter explains the three primary claim types used for software inventions: method claims (which claim a process or sequence of steps), computer-readable medium (CRM) claims (which claim a storage medium containing instructions), and system claims (which claim an architecture of connected components). Each type captures the invention from a different legal angle, providing complementary layers of protection.

The presenter explains the standard filing allocation: inventors receive three independent claims and up to 20 total claims in a basic filing. The recommended approach for first-time filers is to use one independent claim of each type — one method, one CRM, and one system — to cover all angles of the invention without exceeding the basic claim limit. Dependent claims, which always refer to another independent or dependent claim and must be narrower in scope, fill out the remaining 17 claim slots.

The video is notable for its clarity in explaining why all three independent claim types are worth pursuing: infringement can occur in different ways depending on how a competitor implements the technology, and having all three types maximizes the likelihood that at least one claim is infringed in each scenario.

## Key Takeaways

- Three primary patent claim types for software inventions: method, computer-readable medium (CRM), and system
- A method claim covers a process or sequence of steps
- A CRM claim covers a storage medium (hard drive, memory) containing instructions that cause a computer to perform the claimed steps
- A system claim covers an architecture of components connected in a particular way
- The basic USPTO filing fee includes three independent claims and 20 total claims
- Recommended approach for first filings: one independent method claim, one CRM claim, one system claim
- Dependent claims must refer to another claim and must be narrower in scope than the claim they depend from
- Using all three independent claim types maximizes protection against different infringement scenarios

## Best For

Software developers and technology inventors filing their first patent application who want a plain-language explanation of the three main software patent claim types and how to allocate their standard three independent claims.
