## Overview

This Inventor's Quick Tips episode covers patent drawings comprehensively — what they must show, what types of drawings are appropriate for different inventions, the formal conventions for labeling and line styles, and the distinction between informal drawings (acceptable for provisionals) and formal drawings (required for regular applications). The overarching rule is that drawings must cover everything claimed in the patent and must demonstrate enablement — showing enough detail that a person skilled in the field could make and use the invention.

The video walks through the range of drawing types inventors may need: orthographic views (top, bottom, left, right, front, back) for mechanical devices; cross-sections for revealing internal components; exploded views to show assembly; before-and-after sequential drawings for process patents; and flowcharts for software, manufacturing processes, and multi-step methods. The principle given is to err on the side of including more views rather than fewer — extra drawings cause no harm, while missing views can create problems during examination.

The formal drawing conventions are covered in detail: reference numbers with lead lines pointing to components (with the line touching the component but the number not touching it); slightly curved lead lines as a preferred style to distinguish them from structural lines; dotted lines for internal or hidden components; underlined numbers when a lead line is impractical; and shading using parallel lines or dots to convey cylindrical shapes or solid materials. The video shows a side-by-side comparison of an informal drawing (a digital photo with crude number overlays, acceptable for a provisional) versus the corresponding formal drawing with all extraneous detail removed and standardized line weights applied. Photographs are generally not allowed except in narrow circumstances where they are the only practical way to show the invention.

## Key Takeaways

- Drawings must cover all claimed elements and demonstrate enablement — enough detail for someone skilled in the field to make and use the invention
- Provisional patent applications accept any drawing; regular patent applications require formal drawings meeting USPTO standards
- Common drawing types: orthographic views, cross-sections, exploded views, sequential before/after views for processes, and flowcharts for software or multi-step methods
- When in doubt, include more drawing views — extra figures create no problems, missing ones can cause examination issues
- Lead lines connect reference numbers to components; lines touch the component, numbers do not; slightly curved lines are preferred to distinguish from structural lines
- Dotted lines indicate hidden or internal components; shading with parallel lines or dots conveys material solidity or cylindrical shape
- Photographs are generally not permitted; exceptions exist when a photographic effect or biological specimen cannot be adequately shown with line drawings
