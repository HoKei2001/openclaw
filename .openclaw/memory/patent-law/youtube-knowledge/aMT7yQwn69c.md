# The International Patent Process: How to Get Patents Worldwide

**Channel**: Patents Demystified
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aMT7yQwn69c
**Published**: 2021-04-22

---

## Overview

Dylan Adams of Patents Demystified delivers a comprehensive guide to international patent filing from a US-based patent attorney's perspective, structured for startups and small companies that need to make smart decisions about when and where to pursue foreign protection. The video opens with a critical correction: there is no such thing as a global patent — patents are jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction, and each country requires its own separate application, examination, and grant.

The two main routes to foreign protection are direct national filings (filing separately in each country within 12 months of the priority date) and the PCT route (filing a single PCT application within 12 months of priority, which then preserves rights in nearly all countries until the 30-month national phase deadline). Adams uses the analogy that the PCT functions like a provisional patent application for international filings — it buys time without locking in country choices. The typical workflow he uses with clients: file a provisional patent application first, then at the 12-month mark file the US non-provisional and a PCT application simultaneously, gaining 30 months total from the provisional date to decide which foreign markets to enter.

The video provides realistic cost benchmarks: PCT filing is approximately $3,000–$5,000; national phase entry in English-speaking countries costs roughly $25,000 per jurisdiction over time; non-English-speaking countries cost approximately $35,000 per jurisdiction (due to translation requirements). Adams explicitly warns against over-investing in foreign patent protection too early — he has seen startups fail from this mistake. Regional patent offices (EPO for Europe, Eurasian Patent Office, African Regional IP Organization) are explained as a way to prosecute once and then validate in individual countries upon grant. Taiwan is noted as the most commercially significant jurisdiction not covered by PCT, requiring a direct national filing.

## Key Takeaways

- There is no global patent — each country grants patents independently, requiring separate applications, examinations, and grants per jurisdiction
- PCT acts like a provisional for international filings: file once, preserve rights in ~150+ countries, then make national phase decisions at the 30-month mark
- Typical workflow: provisional → US non-provisional + PCT at 12 months → national phase entry decisions at 30 months from provisional date
- PCT filing costs approximately $3,000–$5,000; national phase per jurisdiction costs ~$25,000 (English) or ~$35,000 (non-English, due to translation)
- Over-investing in foreign patent protection too early is a common and serious startup mistake — the 30-month PCT window is designed to delay these costs until market and investor clarity emerges
- Regional offices (EPO, Eurasian, African) allow single examination with country-by-country validation at grant, reducing overall prosecution costs for multi-country Europe coverage
- Taiwan is not a PCT member and requires a direct national filing within 12 months of priority — relevant for chip, semiconductor, and hardware companies

## Best For

Startup founders and inventors ready to understand the full international patent filing landscape, including PCT mechanics, realistic cost benchmarks, and the strategic considerations that should drive when and where to file.
