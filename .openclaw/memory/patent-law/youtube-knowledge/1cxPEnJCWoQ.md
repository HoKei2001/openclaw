## Overview

This official tutorial from IP Australia walks inventors through the basics of patent searching, explaining why searches are important and demonstrating how to use free online tools. The video covers three major search platforms — Google Patents, Lens.org, and AusPat — and teaches practical techniques including synonym expansion and use of the International Patent Classification (IPC) system. It is framed as preparation for a first meeting with a patent attorney.

## Key Takeaways

- Start with Google to broadly search for products or services similar to your invention before moving to patent databases.
- Google Patents is easy to use for global searches but does not include Australian results; Lens.org covers global databases including Australia.
- AusPat (IP Australia's own tool) is the best choice for Australian-specific searches and includes full case history.
- Use the "inventive concept" as your search focus and generate multiple synonyms to maximize relevant results.
- The International Patent Classification (IPC) system — with about 70,000 categories — can help narrow search results to the most relevant technology area.
- Pay attention to patent claims, which define the legal boundaries of protection, and check patent status (pending vs. in-force) when assessing infringement risk.
- Bring your search results, invention details, and business plan to your attorney meeting for best results.

## Topics Covered

- Why patent searching matters (novelty check and infringement avoidance)
- How to use Google, Google Patents, Lens.org, and AusPat
- Developing synonyms and search terms for your inventive concept
- Reading patent documents: abstract, bibliographic info, description, claims
- Using the IPC classification system to refine results
- Interpreting patent status and geographic coverage
- Preparing for a patent attorney consultation

## Best For

Inventors new to the patent system who want to conduct a preliminary search before consulting a patent attorney. Particularly relevant for Australian inventors, but the methodology applies globally.
