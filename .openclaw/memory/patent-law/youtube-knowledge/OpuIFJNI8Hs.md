## Overview

This video provides a systematic side-by-side comparison of the three main forms of intellectual property protection — patents, copyrights, and trademarks — covering 25 key points of distinction. The comparisons are organized across multiple dimensions: what each protects, where to register, how long the process takes, how long protection lasts, the costs involved, common law rights for unregistered IP, and proper notice marking requirements.

Patents protect new inventions and discoveries that are novel, non-obvious, and useful, granting the inventor exclusive rights to make, use, sell, and distribute the invention for 20 years from the filing date. Trademarks protect brand identifiers — words, phrases, symbols, logos, or designs that distinguish one company's goods or services from another's — and can last indefinitely as long as they remain in use and are properly maintained. Copyrights protect original works of authorship (literature, music, art, film, software, architectural designs) and last for the author's life plus 70 years, or 95 years from publication for corporate or anonymous works. Both patents and trademarks are registered at the USPTO (different divisions), while copyrights are registered at the Library of Congress's Copyright Office — not the USPTO.

Notably, unregistered patents have no legal protection at all; unregistered trademarks and copyrights have limited common law rights. For marking, granted patents must be labeled with the patent number; registered trademarks may use the ® symbol; copyrights use © plus year and owner name.

## Key Takeaways

- Patents protect inventions (novel, non-obvious, useful) for 20 years from filing; they cannot be renewed.
- Trademarks protect brand identifiers and can last indefinitely with continued use and maintenance.
- Copyrights protect original works of authorship and last for life of the author plus 70 years.
- Patents and trademarks are registered at different divisions of the USPTO; copyrights are registered at the Library of Congress.
- Registration timelines: patents take 1–3 years; trademarks 12–18 months; copyrights a few weeks to months.
- Filing costs: patent applications cost hundreds to thousands in USPTO fees; trademarks $350 per class; copyrights approximately $50.
- Unregistered patents have no legal protection; unregistered trademarks and copyrights have limited common law rights.
- Proper notice: patents must be marked with the patent number; registered trademarks may use ®; copyrights use © + year + owner name.
