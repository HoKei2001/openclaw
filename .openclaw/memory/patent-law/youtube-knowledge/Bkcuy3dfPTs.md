# Jeffrey Schox: Who to Choose as a Patent Attorney?

**Channel**: Stanford eCorner
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bkcuy3dfPTs
**Published**: 2016-11-04

---

## Overview

Jeffrey Schox, a patent attorney speaking at Stanford eCorner, delivers a focused talk on what to look for when selecting a patent attorney — particularly for startup founders. He opens with a striking quote from the Supreme Court: writing a patent application is the hardest legal document there is. From this foundation, he argues that patent claim drafting is genuinely an art form, requiring not just legal knowledge but the ability to identify which specific words — often just 200 in an independent claim — will distinguish an invention from all prior art while providing the broadest possible future protection.

The key selection criteria Schox emphasizes are experience (at least 100 patent applications written), technical background alignment (an attorney who cannot describe your invention back to you is a red flag), and startup-specific expertise. He draws a sharp contrast between Fortune 500 patent law and startup patent law: large companies don't typically use provisional applications or PCT filings, work through in-house patent counsel, and operate on a fundamentally different timeline and budget structure. Startup attorneys need to understand technology development curves, funding rounds, and the strategic timing of provisionals — for example, filing a provisional on a seed round and converting after a Series A.

Schox's most distinctive recommendation is physical proximity: he argues that being able to work on the same whiteboard, visit the lab, and see demos in person is crucial when every word in an independent claim matters. He acknowledges this perspective may be unusual in an era of remote work but maintains that the nuance required for excellent claim drafting benefits significantly from in-person collaboration.

## Key Takeaways

- The Supreme Court has called writing a patent application the hardest legal document — claim drafting is an art that requires substantial experience (at least 100+ applications)
- If an attorney cannot describe your invention back to you after hearing it, that is a major red flag indicating they lack the technical grasp to write strong claims
- Startup patent law is fundamentally different from Fortune 500 patent law: provisionals, PCT filings, and funding-aligned timing strategies are startup-specific skills
- Understanding a startup's funding curve is essential: provisional filing on seed, conversion after Series A is a common and important strategic pattern
- Physical proximity to the attorney enables whiteboard sessions, lab visits, and demo viewings — all valuable for ensuring every word in a claim is accurate
- Large Fortune 500-focused patent attorneys typically lack experience with provisional processes, PCT strategies, and the cost constraints of early-stage companies

## Best For

Startup founders who want a perspective from an experienced startup-focused patent attorney on how to evaluate attorney quality and why startup patent needs differ from corporate patent needs.
