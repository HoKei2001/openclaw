# International Patent Application - Don't File

**Channel**: Elias Borges
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IWqn_mU_2qs
**Published**: 2021-08-10

---

## Overview

Patent attorney Elias Borges provides a balanced and candid assessment of PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty) international patent applications, explaining both when they make sense and when they can be "just about the worst thing you can do." The video explains the PCT system: a single application filed in one country (or directly with the PCT office in Geneva) that designates multiple countries, giving inventors up to 30 months from the first filing to enter the national phase in each desired country. The PCT has two phases — an international phase where the application is reviewed and a prior art search is conducted, and a national phase where applicants must file individually in each desired country.

The presenter walks through the advantages (extending the international filing deadline by 18 months, streamlining multi-country filing) and significant disadvantages (high costs for translations, national filing fees, and foreign patent counsel in each country). The costs for international patent protection can run into tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars, making it largely impractical for individual inventors.

Borges offers practical budget-based guidance: if the budget is a few tens of thousands, focus on the US and one or two strategically chosen countries (Canada, UK, Australia); if the budget is in the hundreds of thousands, then PCT filing across multiple countries becomes appropriate. He suggests that for budget-constrained inventors, multiple related US patents may provide better value than spreading funds across international filings.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT system allows one application to designate up to 156 countries, with up to 30 months to enter the national phase
- The PCT has two phases: an international phase (review and prior art search) and a national phase (individual country filings)
- Major advantages include extending the international filing deadline by 18 months and streamlined multi-country processing
- Filing internationally is very expensive — translations alone can cost thousands of dollars per country, plus national fees and foreign counsel
- Most individual inventors cannot afford international patent protection and should consider it carefully
- Budget guidance: low budget = US + 1-2 countries; medium budget = a few international applications; high budget = PCT across many countries
- Multiple US patents often provide better value than spreading a limited budget across international filings
- Missing the 12-month deadline for PCT priority claim from the first application is a critical error to avoid

## Best For

Inventors and small business owners who are considering international patent protection and need a realistic, budget-aware assessment of whether a PCT application makes strategic sense for their situation.
