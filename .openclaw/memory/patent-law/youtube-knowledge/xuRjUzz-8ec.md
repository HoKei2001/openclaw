## Overview

This video is an interview with Dylan Adams, the patent attorney behind the Patents Demystified channel, tracing his unconventional path from biochemistry undergraduate to founding a patent practice during law school to eventually joining a top-tier AmLaw 100 firm. The conversation offers a candid look at how careers in patent law are built, the role of technical credentials, and what it takes to grow a patent practice at different firm sizes.

Adams's path was anything but linear. He began a biochemistry degree, quit school for nearly three years to tour with an acapella-turned-pop band, and returned with clear focus and motivation. His combination of a biochemistry degree and an interest in business led him to law school, where he passed the patent bar examination in his first semester and immediately began taking clients as a patent agent. He and a classmate formed their own firm while still in school, gaining practical experience before graduating. After briefly working at a boutique firm, Adams recognized that his lack of a formal computer science or electrical engineering credential was limiting his ability to attract technology company clients. He went back to school for a master's degree in electrical engineering, which immediately opened doors at larger firms including Orrick — one of the largest law firms in the country — and eventually at Davis Wright Tremaine, where he has remained and where the patent group has grown from roughly 10 to 50 attorneys.

The video discusses the nature of patent practice as a federal discipline with no geographic restrictions on clients, the importance of building a client base gradually starting with small inventors and startups, the value of mentorship and access to large-company clients that joining an established firm provides, and the balance between developing technical credibility and legal expertise in an increasingly specialized field.

## Key Takeaways

- To become a patent attorney, you must pass the patent bar examination, which requires a science or engineering degree, in addition to passing the state bar.
- The patent bar can be taken while still in law school — passing it opens the door to practicing as a patent agent and taking clients before graduating.
- Patent law is a federal practice with no geographic restrictions; patent attorneys can represent clients anywhere in the US and internationally.
- A technical background that matches the technology sector you want to serve is often as important as legal credentials for attracting clients.
- Returning to school for an advanced technical degree (such as a master's in electrical engineering) can significantly increase access to technology company clients.
- Building a patent practice typically starts with small inventors and startup clients and grows toward larger corporate clients as experience and reputation develop.
- Choosing a law firm should weigh infrastructure, mentorship access, the quality of the client base, the path to partnership, and the team culture.

## Best For

Law students, engineers, and science graduates who are considering a career in patent law and want to understand how a realistic career path in the field is built from early training through partnership at a major firm.
