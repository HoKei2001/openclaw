## Overview

This NBG Drafting & Design video focuses on the critical importance of drawings in a design patent application, citing the USPTO's own position that the drawings "constitute the whole disclosure of the design." The core message is that the quality of design patent drawings is not merely a formality — it directly determines what is protected, how infringement is evaluated, and whether the application survives examination without costly corrections.

The video addresses the specific technical requirements the USPTO imposes on design patent drawings: all surfaces must be shaded to clearly convey the character and contour of every surface represented, and the views must be consistent and complete across all required perspectives. A significant practical warning is issued about using photographs as the initial filing medium: once photographs or informal drawings are filed, inconsistencies generally cannot be corrected without adding new matter, which is prohibited. This means errors locked in at the time of filing can permanently limit the scope of protection.

The recommended solution is to have a professional draftsperson prepare the initial drawings. The video explains that photograph-based approaches fail because maintaining perfectly consistent positioning, camera height, and camera distance across all views is technically difficult — and even well-taken photographs introduce perspective distortion that must be corrected when tracing into formal line drawings. A trained draftsperson understands perspective theory and can correct inherent photographic distortion when creating the final formal drawings.

## Key Takeaways

- The USPTO states that drawings in a design patent "constitute the whole disclosure of the design" — they are the entire patent, not a supplement to text
- All surfaces must be shaded to clearly show the character and contour of every surface represented
- Inconsistencies in photographs or informal drawings filed with the application generally cannot be corrected later without adding new matter — making initial drawing quality critical
- Photographs are difficult to use correctly because maintaining consistent camera position, height, and distance across all required views is technically demanding
- Photographs inherently contain perspective distortion; a professional draftsperson can correct this when tracing into formal line drawings using knowledge of perspective theory
- Infringement of a design patent is typically based on the visual design as depicted in the drawings, so drawing quality directly affects enforceability

## Best For

Inventors and design patent applicants who want to understand why professional drawing preparation matters and the specific risks of submitting inconsistent or photograph-based initial drawings.
