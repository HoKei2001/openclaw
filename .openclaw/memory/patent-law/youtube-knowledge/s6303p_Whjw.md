## Overview

This UpCounsel video provides a concise step-by-step overview of the US patent application process, from understanding what a patent is and whether an invention qualifies, through filing and examination, to the commercial value a patent can provide. It is designed as a broad introduction for first-time inventors.

The video defines a patent as a government-granted property right giving an inventor exclusive control over their invention for up to 20 years. To qualify, an invention must meet three criteria established by the USPTO: it must be novel (new), useful (having a practical purpose), and non-obvious (not an obvious improvement to someone skilled in the field). The video covers four types of patents — utility (most common, covering how something works), design (covering unique visual features), plant (covering new plant varieties), and software (covering software functionality, noted as having additional complexity).

Before filing, inventors are strongly advised to conduct a patent search through the USPTO database, scientific journals, and global databases to confirm no one has already patented the idea. The application itself must include an abstract, background information, a summary, detailed description, and conclusion highlighting key advantages, along with drawings. Inventors who are not ready for a full application can file a provisional patent application for 12 months of patent pending status at lower cost. The examination process takes one to three years and may include office actions requiring the applicant to respond with clarifications or amendments. If approved, the patent can be monetized through licensing, sale, or use as leverage in business negotiations.

## Key Takeaways

- A patent grants exclusive rights over an invention for up to 20 years and prevents others from making, using, or selling it without permission.
- The three USPTO criteria for patentability are novelty, usefulness, and non-obviousness.
- The four main patent types are utility, design, plant, and software — each covering a different aspect of an invention.
- Conducting a prior art search before filing is essential to confirm the invention is novel and avoid wasting time and money on an application likely to be rejected.
- A provisional patent application provides 12 months of patent pending status at lower cost, giving inventors time to develop and test the invention before committing to the full application.
- The examination process takes one to three years and may involve responding to office actions from the USPTO examiner.
- An issued patent can be licensed to other parties, sold outright, or used as leverage in business negotiations and fundraising.

## Best For

First-time inventors and entrepreneurs with no prior patent experience who want a concise overview of the entire patent process from eligibility through commercial value.
