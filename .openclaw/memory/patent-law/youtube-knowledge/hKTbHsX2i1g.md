## Overview

This OC Patent Lawyer video presents four concrete strategies for crafting effective responses to USPTO patent office actions, with a focus on the tactical and relational aspects of patent prosecution that are often underemphasized in basic guides. The video is structured around a clear, practical framework applicable to any rejection type.

The first strategy is point-of-novelty focus: every response should lead with the specific feature that makes the invention unique, demonstrating from the beginning that the applicant understands what the patent is actually protecting. If the point of novelty is not clearly highlighted, even a technically successful argument may result in a narrow or low-value patent. The second strategy is claim language alignment: arguments in a response must track the actual language of the claims. A common failure mode is responding with narrowly-interpreted arguments when the examiner is reading claims under the Broadest Reasonable Interpretation (BRI) standard — the examiner can simply dismiss arguments that don't map to their reading. The recommended technique is to reverse-engineer the examiner's position: assume they are correct, and ask how to change your understanding to arrive at the same conclusion, which reveals how the claims are being read under BRI.

The third strategy is maintaining a positive working relationship with the examiner, who controls whether the application proceeds to grant. If issues arise, requesting an after-final interview is more effective than attempting to challenge the examiner through complaint channels. The fourth strategy — and the one most frequently underutilized — is the examiner interview itself: a 30-minute phone or web conference where misunderstandings can be resolved directly, positions can be clarified, and the path to allowance can often be established far more efficiently than through written exchange alone.

## Key Takeaways

- Every office action response should clearly highlight the point of novelty — the specific feature that makes the invention unique — from the outset
- Arguments must track the actual claim language as the examiner reads it under the Broadest Reasonable Interpretation (BRI) standard, not the inventor's preferred narrower reading
- A useful diagnostic: assume the examiner is correct and reverse-engineer how they arrived at their conclusion to understand how the claims are being read under BRI
- Maintaining a constructive, professional relationship with the examiner is strategically important — the examiner controls whether the patent is granted
- When disputes arise, request an after-final interview rather than escalating through complaint channels
- Examiner interviews (phone or web conference) are the fastest and most effective tool for resolving misunderstandings and establishing a path to allowance — use them proactively
