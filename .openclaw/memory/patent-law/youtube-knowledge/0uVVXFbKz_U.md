## Overview

In this short FAQ episode from the Bold Today's Show, J.D. Houvener provides a high-level explanation of the difference between a provisional and a non-provisional patent application. The video emphasizes that a provisional locks in the priority date, giving inventors 12 months to test the market, build prototypes, and meet investors before committing to the formal non-provisional with its full claims. The presenter frames provisional and non-provisional as complementary tools that work best in tandem.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application is informal — its primary purpose is to lock in a priority date, establishing that the inventor was the first to file.
- The US patent system is a race to the Patent Office: if someone else independently invents the same thing, the person who files first wins the rights.
- The 12-month window after filing a provisional allows inventors to test the market, prototype, talk to investors, and refine the invention before the formal filing.
- A non-provisional application is the formal, structured filing with specific sections, formal drawings, and — most importantly — the written claims that define what the inventor owns.
- Claims are articulated in collaboration with a patent attorney and represent the legal scope of the invention.
- The provisional and non-provisional work in tandem: the non-provisional references the provisional to claim the earlier priority date.
