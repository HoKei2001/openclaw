## Overview

This brief video from ipaware introduces the "trinity test" — the three substantive criteria that must be satisfied for a patent to be granted: novelty, non-obviousness, and utility (industrial application). The presenter frames this in the context of Indian patent examination (substantive examination), noting that these three criteria form the core of any patent examination and that a separate video series covers each in detail. The "trinity" framing is designed as a memorable framework for patent practitioners and exam candidates.

The video also briefly mentions that beyond the trinity test, Section 3 of the Indian Patents Act defines categories of inventions that are not patentable regardless of whether they satisfy the trinity — including computer programs per se, mathematical methods, business methods, algorithms, and traditional knowledge. The examiner applies the trinity test and the Section 3 exclusions together during substantive examination. The presenter notes that a separate video is available specifically covering non-obviousness in detail.

While brief in content, the video serves as a useful orientation piece for understanding the architecture of the patent examination process in India, where novelty, non-obviousness (called "inventive step" in Indian law), and industrial applicability are the three gatekeepers that every patent application must pass before grant consideration.

## Key Takeaways

- The "trinity test" for patentability: novelty (newness), non-obviousness/inventive step, and utility/industrial applicability
- All three criteria must be satisfied for a patent to be granted in substantive examination
- Section 3 of the Indian Patents Act defines additional non-patentable subject matter categories that operate independently of the trinity
- Non-obvious in Indian patent law is termed "inventive step" — requiring technical advancement or economic significance beyond prior art
- These three criteria apply globally (with slight terminology differences) — the USPTO and EPO use the same fundamental framework
- The trinity test is evaluated holistically during substantive examination alongside subject matter eligibility
- Understanding both the trinity test and Section 3 exclusions is essential for Indian patent agent exam candidates
