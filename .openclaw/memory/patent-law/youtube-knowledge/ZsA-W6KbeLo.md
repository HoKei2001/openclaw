## Overview

This video provides a brief, accessible guide to filing a provisional utility patent application. It explains the fundamental purpose of a provisional application: to secure an early filing date for an invention, giving the inventor 12 months to further develop the idea and decide whether to pursue a non-provisional patent. The video emphasizes that this initial step is less formal than a non-provisional application and helps establish patent pending status quickly.

The preparation requirements are outlined: a thorough written description detailed enough for someone familiar with the field to understand and replicate the invention, along with clear drawings illustrating every aspect of the invention. The video notes that filing is done electronically through the USPTO website, with a typical filing fee of approximately $1.75 for small entities (note: this appears to be an error in the transcript; the actual fee is significantly higher). After filing, inventors receive a receipt confirming their application number and official filing date.

The video highlights the strategic value of patent pending status: it can deter potential infringers, make the invention more attractive to investors, and provide time to refine the invention and assess its market potential. The one-year window after filing must be used wisely to determine whether to proceed to a full non-provisional patent application.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional utility patent application secures an early filing date and grants patent pending status
- Inventors have 12 months after filing to develop their idea and decide on a non-provisional application
- The application requires a detailed written description and clear drawings illustrating all aspects of the invention
- Filing is done electronically through the USPTO website with a required filing fee
- Upon submission, inventors receive a filing receipt with the application number and official filing date
- Patent pending status can deter competitors and attract investors during the 12-month window
- The provisional application is a starting point, not final protection — a non-provisional must follow within one year
- This approach gives inventors time to refine their invention and assess market potential before committing to full patent costs

## Best For

Beginning inventors who want a quick, straightforward introduction to the provisional utility patent application process and its strategic benefits for protecting an early-stage invention.
