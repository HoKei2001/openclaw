# How to Engage A Patent Lawyer

**Channel**: Patents Demystified
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pj8i5FnQn10
**Published**: 2022-10-24

---

## Overview

Patent attorney Dylan Adams from Patents Demystified offers a detailed guide on how to initiate and develop a productive relationship with a patent attorney. He explains that patent attorneys are typically very busy — billing up to seven and a half hours of client work per day — so the best way to make first contact is via a concise, personalized email rather than a phone call. The email should briefly describe who you are, your company, your invention, and your availability for a consultation.

During the initial consultation (usually 30 minutes), inventors should be completely open about their invention since attorney-client privilege protects all disclosures. Adams strongly advises against asking the attorney to sign an NDA, as it is both unnecessary and off-putting. Inventors should come prepared with questions about patentability, filing strategy (provisional vs. non-provisional), timing, and business goals. A good patent attorney will tailor the patent strategy to the specific business context.

A key piece of advice is not to insist on a specific filing strategy before the consultation. Adams warns that filing too early is a common and costly mistake for startups, since the 12-month provisional window expires quickly and forces an expensive non-provisional filing before the product and business are ready. Letting the patent attorney guide the strategy based on business readiness yields much better results.

## Key Takeaways

- Start with a short, personalized email rather than a phone call when reaching out to a patent attorney.
- Include a brief bio, description of your invention, and your availability for a call in the initial email.
- Do not ask a patent attorney to sign an NDA — attorney-client privilege already covers all disclosures.
- Be fully open about your invention during the initial consultation to get the most valuable advice.
- Do not insist on specific filing actions before consulting — let the attorney assess the best strategy.
- Discuss business plans, funding stage, and upcoming public disclosures so the attorney can advise on optimal filing timing.
- Avoid filing a provisional application too early; wait until you are approaching public disclosure, investor pitches, or trade shows.
- After the consultation, expect a conflict check and engagement letter before the attorney can formally represent you.

## Best For

First-time inventors and startups who want to understand the right way to approach and work with a patent attorney to maximize the value of their initial consultation.
