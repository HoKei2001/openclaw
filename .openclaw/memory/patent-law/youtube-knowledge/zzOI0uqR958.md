## Overview

This practical tutorial from Inventor's Quick Tips explains the USPTO's patent maintenance fee system in detail, including how to check fee schedules and due dates. Maintenance fees are periodic payments required to keep an issued utility patent in force throughout its 20-year term. Without these payments, the patent goes abandoned and loses its legal protection — which can be a deliberate choice if the patent is no longer commercially valuable.

The maintenance fee schedule has three payments, each due within a six-month window: the first fee at 3.5 years after grant (currently $800 for small entities), the second at 7.5 years ($1,800 for small entities), and the third at 11.5 years ($3,700 for small entities). After the six-month window closes, there is a grace period during which the fee can still be paid with an additional late fee. If the grace period is also missed, the patent goes abandoned — though in some cases it may be possible to revive the patent by paying fees plus surcharges and showing the delay was unintentional. The video demonstrates the USPTO's online maintenance fee lookup tool, where inventors can enter a patent number to see which fees have been paid and when future windows open.

The video draws on the Manual of Patent Examining Procedure (MPEP), Section 2500, which covers the complete rules and exceptions for maintenance fees. Key insight: by the time the 11.5-year fee of $3,700 is due, patent owners should evaluate whether the patent still provides strategic or commercial value — if not, allowing it to lapse saves a significant sum.

## Key Takeaways

- Maintenance fees are required at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after the patent grant date to keep utility patents in force
- Small entity fees: $800 (3.5 years), $1,800 (7.5 years), $3,700 (11.5 years) — fees change periodically, check USPTO for current amounts
- Micro entity status (if qualified) reduces all maintenance fees by 50% compared to small entity rates
- Each payment window is 6 months wide; after the window closes there is an additional grace period with a late fee surcharge
- If you miss both windows, the patent goes abandoned — limited revival options exist for unintentional delays
- Use the USPTO online maintenance fee lookup tool to track payment status and upcoming due dates by patent number
- At 11.5 years, evaluate whether the patent still provides value before paying the largest fee
