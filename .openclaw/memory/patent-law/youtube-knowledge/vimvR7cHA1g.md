## Overview

This brief video explains the two core patentability requirements of novelty and non-obviousness as defined under US patent law. It provides clear, concise definitions of each requirement and notes the subjective nature of the non-obviousness standard.

## Key Takeaways

- US patent law requires that an invention be novel, non-obvious, and useful to qualify for patent protection.
- Novelty means the invention is different from anything previously known or patented — it must be new to the world.
- Non-obviousness is a subjective standard: the invention must be different from prior inventions in a way that is not obvious to a person of ordinary skill in the relevant field.
- Both novelty and non-obviousness are evaluated against the prior art — the existing body of publicly known knowledge, inventions, and patents.
