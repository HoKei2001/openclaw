## Overview

Patent attorney J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm addresses the key considerations involved in protecting a software app or mobile application through the patent system. The video covers two major obstacles to software patentability — abstract idea rejections and detectability of infringement — and explains how inventors should decide between patent protection and trade secret protection for their app's core processes.

## Key Takeaways

- Not all software is eligible for patent protection; the USPTO prohibits patenting abstract ideas that lack sufficient technical detail or simply mirror human behavior in digital form
- To qualify for a patent, a software invention must be described with enough specificity that someone skilled in the field could understand, make, and use it
- A second critical consideration is detectability: if competitors could copy your software process without you being able to detect the infringement, a patent may provide little practical value
- When a software process is difficult to detect externally, trade secret protection combined with strong employment agreements and confidentiality controls may be a better strategy
- The 20-year patent monopoly is granted in exchange for fully disclosing the invention; vague or purely abstract claims will not meet this standard
- Consulting a patent attorney to evaluate which protection strategy best fits the specific software innovation is strongly recommended
