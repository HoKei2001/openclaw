## Overview

This short, practical tutorial walks inventors through the process of filing a provisional patent application on the USPTO website without the help of an attorney. The presenter, a non-attorney inventor who has filed multiple provisional patents himself, frames the provisional patent as an affordable tool to test the market before committing to a full non-provisional patent. The micro entity filing fee at the time of filming was around $70, making the provisional a low-cost way to achieve "patent pending" status.

The video demonstrates the step-by-step process on the USPTO's online filing system, beginning with account creation and walking through the submission of four required documents: the transmittal form (SB-16), the micro entity status form (SB-15), the drawings, and the specification. Drawings should be in black and white with numbered parts, each described in the specification. For a provisional application, drawings do not need to meet the strict standards required for a non-provisional patent.

The presenter emphasizes that the provisional patent is a short-term placeholder — ultimately, inventors will need a full (non-provisional) patent — but it provides one year to test the product, gauge market interest, and decide whether the investment in a full patent is warranted. The entire process is presented as achievable by any inventor willing to invest a small amount of time.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application can be filed by an inventor without an attorney for a low government filing fee (around $65–$70 for micro entities).
- Four documents are required: the SB-16 transmittal form, the SB-15 micro entity certification, drawings, and a written specification.
- Drawings should be in black and white with numbered parts, each described in the specification.
- The micro entity status discount applies to individuals earning under approximately $150,000 annually.
- A provisional patent lasts only 12 months and does not itself lead to a patent — it must be followed by a non-provisional application.
- The provisional gives inventors time to test the market and validate demand before spending more on a formal patent.
- All files must be in PDF format for upload to the USPTO system.
- After uploading and validating documents, you pay the filing fee online to complete the submission.
