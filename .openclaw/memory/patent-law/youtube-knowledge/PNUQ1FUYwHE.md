## Overview

This webinar, presented by the New York Small Business Development Center in partnership with the USPTO, provides a comprehensive introduction to the U.S. patent application process. USPTO primary examiners Monica Shin and Gam Ubal walk through the key differences between provisional and non-provisional utility patent applications, covering requirements, costs, timelines, and strategic considerations for small business owners and first-time inventors.

The video explains the three core patentability requirements — novelty, non-obviousness, and utility — and walks through the full examination process, from the initial filing to receiving office actions and eventually achieving patent allowance. Practical guidance is given on using the USPTO's Patent Center online filing system, understanding entity status tiers (micro, small, large) for fee discounts, and preparing the required documents for submission.

The session also covers important topics such as the Pro Se Assistance Program for inventors filing without attorneys, the Patent Pro Bono Program for low-income inventors, and the network of Patent and Trademark Resource Centers (PTRCs). The Q&A format makes this especially useful for understanding real-world questions from first-time applicants.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application reserves a priority filing date for 12 months at lower cost but will not result in a patent unless followed by a non-provisional application.
- A non-provisional utility patent application is the formal filing that undergoes examination and can result in a granted patent valid for 20 years.
- Three patentability requirements must be met: novelty (new), utility (useful), and non-obviousness (not an obvious variation of prior art).
- USPTO fees are tiered: micro entity, small entity, and large entity rates provide significant discounts for independent inventors and small businesses.
- The Patent Center (patentcenter.uspto.gov) is the official online filing system and requires identity verification through ID.me since September 2025.
- After a non-provisional application is filed, examination typically begins 1–3 years later and usually involves one or more office actions before allowance.
- Free resources are available for inventors without attorneys, including the Pro Se Assistance Program and the Patent Pro Bono Program.
