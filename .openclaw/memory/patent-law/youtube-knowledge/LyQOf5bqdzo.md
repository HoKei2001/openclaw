## Overview

Attorney Soluto Uba of the Uba Law Group provides a quick, accessible explanation of the three most common forms of intellectual property protection — trademarks, copyrights, and patents — aimed at entrepreneurs and small business owners who frequently confuse them. The video addresses a real and common problem: business owners calling to "copyright their brand name" or "patent their logo," indicating widespread misunderstanding of which tool applies in which situation.

Trademarks are explained as source identifiers — they tell the world which company a product or service comes from. The Nike name and Swoosh logo are used as examples: seeing either instantly communicates brand, quality, price point, and endorsements. Trademarks protect brand identity and the goodwill built around it. Copyrights, by contrast, protect original creative works fixed in a tangible medium — songs, books, movies, courses, curricula. Copyrights arise automatically upon creation, but formal registration is important because it enables the owner to claim statutory damages (a set monetary amount per infringement) even if the infringer was unaware of the copyright. Patents protect ideas and inventions — new products, processes, apps, or technologies — and require demonstrating novelty, utility, and non-obviousness. A helpful cheat code mentioned in the video: "trademarks are for branding; copyrights are for content."

## Key Takeaways

- Trademarks protect brand identifiers (names, logos, symbols) that tell consumers the source of a product or service.
- Copyrights protect original creative works fixed in a tangible medium (songs, books, films, courses, software).
- Patents protect new inventions and ideas that are novel, useful, and non-obvious.
- A useful shorthand: "trademarks are for branding; copyrights are for content."
- Copyright arises automatically upon creation, but formal registration enables statutory damages claims against infringers.
- Patents require formal application and approval; they have strict technical requirements and typically require an attorney.
- Patents are divided into utility patents (protecting function) and design patents (protecting appearance).
- Business owners should understand which type of protection applies to their specific asset before seeking legal assistance.
