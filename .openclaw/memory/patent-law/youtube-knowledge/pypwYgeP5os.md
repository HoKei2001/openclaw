# How to Write Your Own Provisional Patent Application

**Channel**: John Ferrell, Esq.
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pypwYgeP5os
**Published**: 2022-05-23

---

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell presents a practical five-element framework for inventors who want to write their own provisional patent application. The video emphasizes that provisional applications are highly informal — misspelled words, handwritten text, photographs of napkins, and PowerPoint slides are all acceptable — but that the content must still be substantive enough to prove that the invention existed as of the filing date, since courts may review the provisional if the resulting patent is ever litigated.

The five elements Ferrell recommends are: first, a strong and descriptive title that distinguishes the invention clearly (for example, "dog leash with integrated ultrasonic whistle" rather than just "dog leash"); second, a plain-language summary of the invention covering what it does, what makes it novel, how it is built, and how it is used; third, a specification or detailed description organized around the drawings, explaining each functional component and how the parts work together; fourth, a simple patent claim written in plain English that lists what the inventor believes is novel — this is optional for provisional applications but Ferrell recommends including it because the exercise helps ensure all important elements are described in the application; and fifth, figures, drawings, sketches, and photographs, which Ferrell describes as the most important element of the application. Screenshots, PowerPoint slides, handwritten sketches, and informal photographs are all acceptable and allow inventors to convey a great deal of inventive detail without having to write extensive text.

Ferrell uses a smartphone app for matching shirt and tie colors as a running example throughout the video, walking through how to describe the block diagram architecture, the process flow steps, and the algorithm that drives the matching logic. He encourages inventors to be overinclusive with respect to figures and to comment in the section below if they would like a video on how to use the USPTO's Patent Center portal to file the completed application.

## Key Takeaways

- Provisional patent applications are informal — spelling errors, handwritten content, photographs, and sketches are all acceptable.
- Courts may review the provisional application in litigation to verify that the invention was fully described as of the filing date, so content completeness still matters.
- A descriptive title that clearly distinguishes the invention is the first element and helps differentiate multiple related filings.
- The summary section should cover what the invention does, what is novel about it, how it is constructed, and how it is used.
- The specification should be organized around the drawings, describing each functional component individually and how components work together.
- Including a simple plain-English patent claim (even informally worded) helps ensure all novel elements are described in the application.
- Figures are the most important element — screenshots, sketches, photographs, and PowerPoint slides are all acceptable and can convey far more than written text alone.

## Best For

Independent inventors in the early stages who want a clear, attorney-guided framework for writing a provisional patent application on their own without needing to understand formal patent drafting conventions.
