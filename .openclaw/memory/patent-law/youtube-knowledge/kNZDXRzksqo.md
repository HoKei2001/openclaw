## Overview

This is a recording of a detailed webinar presented by the Virginia SBDC in collaboration with the USPTO. The presenter, Cynthia — a member of the USPTO's Information Technology Resource Provider program — delivers a structured training session on how to conduct a prior art search using USPTO tools. The webinar is the third in a four-part series on patents for small business owners and inventors.

The session covers the USPTO's recommended seven-step patent search strategy. These steps include brainstorming keywords and classification codes related to the invention, searching the Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC) schema, reviewing CPC definitions to identify additional keywords, searching the USPTO patent database by CPC code, conducting in-depth analysis of the most relevant patents, repeating the search for published patent applications, and finally broadening the search to include keyword searches, foreign patents via Espacenet, and non-patent literature such as journals, books, and websites.

The presenter uses a hands-on demonstration throughout, showing how to navigate the USPTO website, use the CPC classification system, retrieve patents by classification code, and read patent documents including claims, figures, and specifications. The video emphasizes that a thorough prior art search not only helps determine patentability but also reveals how to improve an application and design around existing patents.

## Key Takeaways

- Prior art search is the critical first step before filing — it determines novelty, helps shape claims, and reveals design-around opportunities.
- The USPTO recommends a seven-step search strategy covering brainstorming, CPC classification lookup, definition review, patent retrieval, in-depth analysis, application database search, and broadening.
- Use the Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC) schema to find specific technology category codes relevant to your invention.
- Search the USPTO's public patent search tool using CPC codes to retrieve all patents in a relevant technology class.
- Espacenet (espacenet.com) provides access to foreign patents using the same CPC classification system.
- Non-patent literature — including journals, books, websites, and catalogs — can also serve as prior art and should be searched.
- Reading the claims section of retrieved patents is more informative than reading only the abstract for determining relevance.
- Patent examiners conduct their own comprehensive searches when an application is filed, but inventors benefit from doing preliminary searches to refine their applications.

## Best For

Small business owners, solo inventors, and startup founders who want a thorough, step-by-step introduction to using USPTO databases for conducting their own prior art search.
