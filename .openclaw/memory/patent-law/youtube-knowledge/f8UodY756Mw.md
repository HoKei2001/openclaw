## Overview

This explainer from Trademark and Patent Law Experts provides a comprehensive overview of how patent royalties function within licensing agreements. The video uses accessible language to explain how inventors and patent owners can generate income from their inventions without building factories or handling sales and distribution. Patent licensing allows a patent holder (the licensor) to grant permission to another party (the licensee) to use, make, or sell the patented technology in exchange for a fee called a royalty — typically expressed as a percentage of the licensee's revenue from products using that technology.

The video covers several royalty payment structures: running royalties (ongoing payments based on sales or earnings, e.g., 4% annually on net sales of a patented drug), upfront fees (a one-time payment at the start of the agreement to provide immediate revenue to the patent owner), milestone payments (fixed amounts triggered by specific goals like regulatory approval or reaching a sales threshold), and minimum royalties (guaranteed floor payments regardless of actual sales). Agreements can also include tiered royalty rates — for example, 3% on the first $1 million in sales and 5% on everything above that — as well as deductions for returns and caps on total payments.

Determining a fair royalty rate involves multiple valuation approaches: the income approach (estimating future earnings), the market approach (comparing similar license deals in the industry), and the cost approach (based on development cost). Legal principles from patent infringement cases — particularly the "reasonable royalty" standard — also guide negotiations. License agreements must specify royalty terms, payment schedules, audit rights, and remedies for infringement or missed payments to protect all parties.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent licensing lets inventors earn royalty income without manufacturing or selling — the licensee handles commercialization
- Running royalties are ongoing payments (usually a percentage of sales or net revenue) — the most common royalty structure
- Other royalty structures: upfront fees, milestone payments, minimum royalty guarantees, and tiered rates
- The royalty base (what the percentage is applied to) must be clearly defined in the agreement to prevent disputes
- Three valuation methods for setting royalty rates: income approach, market approach, and cost approach
- Exclusive licenses typically command higher royalty rates than non-exclusive licenses
- License agreements must include payment schedules, audit rights, and clear remedies for non-payment or infringement
