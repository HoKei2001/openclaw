## Overview

This educational webinar hosted by Inventors Groups of America (IGA) features patent attorney Eric Blatt, who spent six years as a USPTO patent examiner before entering private practice. Blatt provides a rare insider perspective on how the patent examination process actually works, what examiners are thinking, and how inventors and attorneys can use that knowledge to achieve better outcomes. The session includes a structured presentation followed by audience Q&A.

Blatt explains that examiners operate under a "Count System" — a productivity metric that rewards processing applications efficiently. This creates a dynamic where examiners are incentivized to issue rejections in the first office action (to show they are working), and where effective communication and cooperation with examiners tends to produce better results than adversarial arguments. He advises against phrasing responses in ways that imply the examiner made an error, since examiners are human and respond better to collaborative language. He also notes that examiner statistics (allowance rates, art unit tendencies) are publicly available and can be used to tailor prosecution strategy.

Additional topics include: how to think strategically about which inventions to patent (patent what competitors will need to do, not just what you're currently doing), when to use Trade One prioritized examination, the advantages and limits of trade secrets versus patents, and the importance of filing patents that are "detectable" — i.e., where infringement would be visible in the marketplace, giving you a chip to negotiate with rather than a right you can never enforce.

## Key Takeaways

- The USPTO examiner Count System incentivizes first-office-action rejections; nearly all applications receive at least one rejection before allowance.
- Examiner statistics (allowance rates, art unit tendencies) are publicly available and can inform prosecution strategy.
- Frame responses cooperatively rather than accusatorially — examiners respond better to respectful, collaborative language.
- Patent what competitors will need to do in the future, not just what you are doing now.
- Prioritize patenting inventions where infringement would be detectable in the marketplace.
- Trade secrets can be a viable alternative to patents when the underlying technology is difficult to reverse-engineer.
- Track One prioritized examination is handled with greater care by examiners and results in faster, more thorough review.
- Examiner interviews are a highly effective tool for breaking an impasse; experienced attorneys use them strategically.
