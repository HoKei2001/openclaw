# Filing a Patent Without An Attorney

**Channel**: John Ferrell, Esq.
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJd6smEOhZ0
**Published**: 2023-03-16

---

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell addresses one of the most common situations facing independent inventors: what to do when you cannot afford a patent attorney. The video is framed around a viewer's comment about having a great invention but no money for professional legal help, and Ferrell responds with five practical tips to make the self-filing journey as smooth as possible without an attorney.

The first tip is to start with a patent search using Google Patents before writing anything. This serves two purposes: it reveals what others in the space have patented, giving you a sense of the competitive landscape, and it provides sample patents in a similar field that you can use as formatting and language templates when drafting your own application. The second tip is to invest in at least one hour of attorney time to review your completed draft before filing — even a single review session can catch critical errors and potentially help with claim drafting. The third tip is to use the USPTO's Pro Se Assistance Program, which offers free resources, videos, and phone consultations for unrepresented inventors, though Ferrell cautions that USPTO staff attorneys may write narrower claims than you might be entitled to. The fourth tip is that most individual inventors qualify for micro entity status, which provides a 75% discount on USPTO fees — bringing the non-provisional filing fee down to approximately $350. The fifth tip is strict document organization: maintain a well-structured folder system for all patent-related documents, because the process generates considerable paperwork that must be tracked over 18+ months.

## Key Takeaways

- Do a basic Google Patents keyword search before drafting your application — it provides landscape context and formatting templates.
- If possible, hire an attorney for just one hour to review your application before filing; claim drafting help is especially valuable.
- The USPTO Pro Se Assistance Program offers free resources and phone consultations for unrepresented inventors.
- Be aware that USPTO examiners helping you draft claims may write narrower claims than what you could obtain commercially.
- Most individual inventors qualify for micro entity status, reducing USPTO non-provisional filing fees to approximately $350.
- Maintain a well-organized folder system for all patent documents — the prosecution process generates many documents over multiple years.
- After filing, expect approximately 18 months before receiving a first office action; use this time to mark products "patent pending."
- The 18-month secrecy period (before publication) gives you competitive cover while competitors cannot see your claims.

## Best For

This video is most useful for budget-constrained independent inventors who want to file their own patent application without professional representation and need practical tips to navigate the process more successfully.
