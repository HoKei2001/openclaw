## Overview

This detailed guide from Patent PC covers best practices for filing a provisional patent application (PPA), particularly for startups and first-time filers. The video begins by reviewing the pros and cons of the PPA: the key advantages are low cost and speed (since no formal claims or rigid structure are required), while the main downside is that filing a provisional triggers the one-year international filing deadline simultaneously with the conversion deadline — adding scheduling pressure. One mitigation strategy is to file a PCT application claiming priority from the provisional, which defers U.S. and international national-phase entry costs by roughly 30 months from the provisional date (at the cost of delaying examination).

The presenter strongly recommends structuring the provisional to mirror the format of a non-provisional application: a title (under 500 characters), an abstract (about 150 words), extensive drawings (the cheapest way to capture the invention), and a detailed description that follows and elaborates on the drawings. This approach is inspired by the pharmaceutical industry's practice of filing non-provisional-quality provisionals to ensure perfect possession of the invention. When the conversion happens a year later, it becomes a near-one-to-one mapping, minimizing questions about whether the priority date is valid.

Although claims are not required in provisionals, the presenter recommends including them anyway to demonstrate complete possession of the invention as of the filing date. Including claims prevents later challenges suggesting the inventor overclaimed when converting, since the claims were already present in the provisional. The video also emphasizes the value of providing as much detail as possible in the provisional, since no two independent inventors working separately will arrive at exactly the same solution — specificity differentiates your invention from any potential concurrent development.

## Key Takeaways

- Provisional applications are informal and have no claims requirement, but quality directly determines the value of the priority date
- Structure the provisional like a non-provisional: title, abstract, extensive drawings, and detailed description
- Filing a provisional triggers the one-year international filing deadline simultaneously — plan PCT filing if international protection is needed
- Including claims in the provisional (though optional) demonstrates full possession and prevents later challenges
- Pharmaceuticals routinely file non-provisional-quality provisionals to ensure perfect correspondence on conversion
- Startups can add new developments when converting to the non-provisional, capturing both the original priority date and new advancements
- Provide maximum detail: no two inventors solve the same problem exactly the same way, so specificity protects your priority

## Best For

Startup founders and first-time patent filers who want expert guidance on how to structure a provisional patent application that will hold up during conversion and avoid priority date challenges.
