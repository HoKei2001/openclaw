# The Truth About Provisional Vs Non-Provisional Patent Applications!

**Channel**: The Patent Guru
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wamuRXOxByw
**Published**: 2021-10-04

---

## Overview

The Patent Guru's Jubin Dana debunks a common misconception about provisional patent applications: that they can be written quickly and cheaply with minimal detail. The video explains that the quality and completeness of the disclosure — not just the claims — must be essentially the same in a provisional and a non-provisional application. Undisclosed information added later only receives the later filing date, potentially losing the priority date advantage that makes the provisional valuable in the first place.

## Key Takeaways

- The main difference between a provisional and non-provisional application should be only that claims are written for the non-provisional — not that the non-provisional has more detailed description.
- Provisional applications have a filing fee of ~$200 vs. ~$850 for non-provisionals, but the fee difference does not justify spending significantly less time on the disclosure.
- Any information not disclosed in the provisional is treated as "new material" when converting to non-provisional and only receives the later filing date.
- If an attorney spends only 2 hours on a provisional vs. 8–10 hours on a non-provisional, the provisional will lack critical detail that cannot be recovered later.
- About 70–75% of non-provisional attorney time is spent drafting the detailed description and figures; 25–30% is drafting the claims.
- Saving on the provisional by cutting description detail is a false economy — it undermines the entire purpose of filing provisionally.
- An emergency provisional (e.g., filed the day before a public disclosure) is a legitimate exception where a quick, rough filing makes sense.
