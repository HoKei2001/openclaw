## Overview

This video is a first-person account from an inventor who obtained his first U.S. utility patent at age 17 for a barbell collar product called Snap Flips. Rather than advice from an attorney, this is a real inventor's ground-level walkthrough of the entire patent process — from initial idea through provisional filing, utility patent prosecution, and final grant — including actual dollar amounts spent at each stage.

The inventor explains that he started with only $200 and filed a provisional patent himself using Patent Wizard software ($150 at the time, now $99/year), which guided him through a questionnaire about his invention and produced a draft provisional application. He mailed it to the USPTO, locking in a 12-month priority date while he continued developing and market-testing the product. After confirming market demand, he hired patent attorneys to convert the provisional into a formal utility patent application. After some back-and-forth with the patent examiner and minor claim adjustments, the patent was granted. He also paid for expedited examination (Track One equivalent), which reduced the wait time to about one year instead of the potential one-to-three years.

The video provides a detailed cost breakdown: USPTO fees total approximately $2,000–$4,000 (at micro/small entity rates); attorney fees run $5,000–$15,000; total expected cost is $8,000–$20,000. The inventor notes he paid over $30,000 — an amount he now considers excessive and avoidable.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent can be filed by the inventor alone using tools like Patent Wizard, costing $70–$140 in USPTO fees.
- The provisional locks in a 12-month priority date during which the inventor can validate market demand before committing to the full patent.
- Filing a formal utility patent application requires a patent attorney; attempting to do it alone risks significant errors.
- Expedited (prioritized) examination costs approximately $1,000–$2,000 and reduces wait time to about one year.
- USPTO fees for a utility patent total approximately $2,000–$4,000 at small/micro entity rates.
- Attorney fees typically run $5,000–$15,000; total patent cost should be $8,000–$20,000.
- The patent examiner may issue office actions requiring claim amendments; this is normal and usually resolvable.
- Design patents protect appearance; utility patents protect function — most product inventions need a utility patent.

## Best For

This video is most useful for first-time inventors with limited budgets who want a realistic, first-person account of what the patent process actually costs and involves from start to finish.
