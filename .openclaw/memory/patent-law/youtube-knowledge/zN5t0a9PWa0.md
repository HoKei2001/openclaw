## Overview

This webinar, hosted by the Virgin Islands Small Business Development Center (VI SBDC) and presented by USPTO patent examiner and trainer Edgar Orazo, provides a comprehensive introduction to patenting apps and software under US patent practice. The presentation covers the full spectrum of intellectual property rights relevant to app development — patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets — along with USPTO resources available to inventors and small businesses.

## Key Takeaways

- Intellectual property should be viewed as a business strategy, not just a legal formality; an IP portfolio can attract investors, deter infringement, and increase a company's value in mergers and acquisitions
- There is no such thing as a global or worldwide patent; patents are territorial, and a US patent only provides protection in the United States
- An app can be protected by multiple overlapping IP rights: utility patents for novel functionality, design patents for the app's graphical user interface or icon appearance, trademarks for the app name and logo, copyrights for code and graphical works, and trade secrets for confidential algorithms
- A provisional patent application is not examined or published, but it establishes an early effective filing date and is only valid for utility patents for one year
- Non-provisional applications are examined, published 18 months after filing, and must meet the requirements of novelty (35 USC 102), non-obviousness (35 USC 103), and utility (35 USC 101)
- Best practices for software patent claims include describing technical challenges, explaining engineering solutions in flowcharts, and articulating how the invention improves computer functionality over conventional approaches
- USPTO offers extensive free resources including the Patent Pro Bono Program, Patent and Trademark Resource Centers (PTRCs), an Inventor Assistance Center, and pro se applicant assistance programs
