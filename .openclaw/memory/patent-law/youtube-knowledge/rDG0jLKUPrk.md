## Overview

This instructional video is part of a patent primer series and explains the methodology for conducting a systematic prior art search using free patent databases. The presenter frames prior art search as an essential step before filing, since a patent requires novelty and inventive step, and searching is the only reliable way to assess whether an invention meets those thresholds. Free databases covered include Google Patents, Espacenet (spasPat), the USPTO database, WIPO PatentScope, and Lens.org.

The core methodology involves four steps: identifying keywords and key phrases that describe the invention; composing Boolean search strings using AND, OR, and NOT operators; incorporating International Patent Classification (IPC) codes to narrow results to relevant technical fields; and collecting and comparing relevant prior art documents against the invention. The video uses a mousetrap with a remote sensor as a running example to illustrate keyword selection, Boolean string construction, and the effect of adding IPC codes to search strings.

A practical shortcut for finding IPC codes is to enter keywords into the Espacenet classification search, which returns relevant IPC codes that can then be incorporated into search strings in Google Patents or Espacenet. The presenter notes that a targeted, methodical search strategy dramatically improves the quality of prior art found, giving inventors a realistic preview of how a patent examiner would evaluate their application.

## Key Takeaways

- Prior art search is the only reliable way to assess novelty and inventive step before filing
- Free databases for prior art search include Google Patents, Espacenet, USPTO, WIPO PatentScope, and Lens.org
- Step 1: Generate exhaustive keywords and key phrases covering the invention's purpose, parts, and synonyms
- Step 2: Combine keywords using Boolean operators AND, OR, and NOT to build targeted search strings
- Step 3: Incorporate IPC (International Patent Classification) codes to filter results to the correct technical field
- Step 4: Collect and compare relevant prior art documents against the invention's features
- Using Google Patents Advanced Search allows separate field-by-field entry, with each field treated as an AND relationship
- IPC codes can be found quickly by entering keywords in the Espacenet classification search tool
