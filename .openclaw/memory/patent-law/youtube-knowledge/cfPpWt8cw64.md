## Overview

Patent lawyer Jeff Schell (founder of Shell IP, with experience building and selling two eight-figure companies) delivers a comprehensive Patent Licensing 101 overview, covering the three types of licenses, key licensing agreement terms, negotiation factors, and how to maximize earnings from a patent. The video is designed for inventors and business owners who want to understand how to turn a patent into a revenue stream.

The three license types are: exclusive license (one licensee has sole rights, excluding everyone including the patent holder — highest royalties), non-exclusive license (multiple licensees can pay to use the patent — lower royalties per licensee but cumulative income from many), and sole license (one licensee plus the patent holder can use the invention — falls between exclusive and non-exclusive in royalty levels). Key terms in any license agreement include the royalty definition (fixed fee or percentage per unit sold), territory (geographic scope of where the licensee can sell), improvement ownership (who owns improvements made to the patented invention and for how long), and enforcement (who bears the responsibility and cost of enforcing the patent — particularly important because enforcement is very expensive).

Schell advises that in exclusive license negotiations, accepting slightly lower royalties in exchange for placing the enforcement burden on the licensee can be strategically wise, since patent enforcement can easily cost millions of dollars. Factors affecting royalty negotiation include market demand, patent strength and scope, industry standards, and whether the deal is exclusive. Proper patent valuation — using either internal analysis or third-party valuators — is critical before entering negotiations. Long-term IP protection, not just short-term royalties, must be considered in all negotiations.

## Key Takeaways

- Three license types: exclusive (highest royalties, no one else including patentee can use), non-exclusive (multiple licensees), and sole (patentee + one licensee)
- Key license agreement terms: royalties, territory, improvements ownership, and enforcement responsibility
- Enforcement of patents is extremely expensive — consider shifting enforcement burden to the licensee in exchange for slightly lower royalties
- Royalty rates are influenced by market demand, patent strength and scope, industry norms, and exclusivity
- Proper patent valuation (income, market, or cost approach) is essential before negotiating licensing terms
- Third-party patent valuation services and experienced IP attorneys are recommended for negotiating strong deals
- Successful licensing balances maximizing short-term royalty revenue with protecting intellectual property for the long term
