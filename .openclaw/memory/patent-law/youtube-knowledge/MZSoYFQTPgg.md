# What are some effective patent searching strategies?

**Channel**: Stanford Biodesign
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZSoYFQTPgg
**Published**: 2014-10-23

---

## Overview

This Stanford Biodesign video features an experienced patent professional sharing a practical, battle-tested philosophy for conducting effective patent searches. The core approach is distinctive: rather than building elaborate Boolean search strings with many AND/OR/NOT operators, the speaker advocates for running many short, simple, independent keyword searches — each starting from the full patent database — to avoid the "vertical trap" of only seeing results that match a tightly pre-filtered query. The goal is to find one or two highly relevant patents quickly, then use their classification codes and citation networks to navigate to related art.

The speaker recommends using an early, expired patent in the relevant technology space as a search term itself: later patents that cite it as prior art will surface naturally. This citation-network approach supplements keyword searches and helps find patents that may not use the same terminology. For record-keeping, the speaker advises maintaining two separate lists — relevant patents (kept with patent number, title, inventors, assignee, and a brief neutral description) and discarded patents — with no gray zone in between. Keeping comments neutral and factual is important, since over-interpreting a prior art document can inadvertently attribute more scope than it actually claims.

On the topic of "problem patents" — prior art that appears to block a design — the speaker urges calm: the actual scope of a patent's claims is often narrower than the patent's title or abstract suggests, and there are systematic tools (validity challenges, design-arounds, licensing negotiations) available to address potential infringement risks.

## Key Takeaways

- Run many short, simple, independent keyword searches rather than one complex Boolean string — this avoids creating a narrow "vertical trap."
- Once a relevant patent is found, use its classification code to find other patents in the same technology category.
- Use an early expired patent in the field as a search term to find later patents that cite it.
- Maintain two lists: relevant patents (on the list) and discarded patents (off the list) — no gray zone.
- Record patent information neutrally — patent number, title, inventors, assignee, and a brief factual note.
- Avoid over-interpreting prior art; describe what the document actually says, not what you think it implies.
- Problem patents (potential freedom-to-operate risks) are usually not as broad as they first appear; design-arounds, validity challenges, and licensing are available tools.
- Patent searching is a continuous activity throughout the company lifecycle, from development through funding rounds and exit.

## Best For

This video is most useful for engineers, researchers, and startup founders who are doing their own patent searches for patentability assessment or freedom-to-operate analysis and want a practical, experienced-practitioner perspective on search methodology.
