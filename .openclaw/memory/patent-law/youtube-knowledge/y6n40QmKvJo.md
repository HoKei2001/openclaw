# Prior Art Search - 7 Steps to a Thorough, Comprehensive Patent Search

## Overview

Patent attorney April Mosby of Monarch Intellectual Property Group presents the USPTO's recommended seven-step patent search strategy, distinguishing between a basic federal patent search (limited to US patents and published applications) and a comprehensive search that also includes foreign patents and non-patent literature. The video uses a bicycle garage door sensor as a running example and includes live screen demonstrations of each step in the USPTO database.

The seven steps proceed as follows. Step one is brainstorming the invention — identifying the purpose, physical components, possible materials, communication protocols, and all relevant keywords and technical terms, using a dictionary or thesaurus to expand the vocabulary. Step two is accessing the Cooperative Patent Classification schema at the USPTO website and searching for classification codes relevant to the invention. Step three is reviewing the CPC definitions for those codes to find additional, more specific keywords. Step four is using the selected CPC code to retrieve patents from the USPTO public patent search database, reviewing results by toggling between text and image modes, and downloading promising patents as PDFs for detailed review. Step five is conducting an in-depth analysis of the closest patents — examining the front page, abstract, and especially the claims section to understand exactly what is protected and how closely it matches the invention. Step six repeats the CPC-based search but in the patent application database rather than the granted patent database. Step seven broadens the search using keyword searches in the USPTO database, US patent classification codes, foreign patent databases (specifically Espacenet at espacenet.com), and non-patent publications including Google searches, academic journals, technical articles, books, and library resources.

Mosby notes that some patent attorneys prefer not to conduct a pre-filing search and instead allow the examiner to conduct the official search, since examiner searches are highly comprehensive. However, a pre-filing search helps confirm there is no obvious infringement risk, avoids wasting money on applications for already-patented concepts, and can reveal ways to strengthen the specification and increase the probability of issuance.

## Key Takeaways

- A federal patent search covers only US patents and applications; a comprehensive search also includes foreign patents and non-patent literature.
- The seven-step USPTO strategy: brainstorm keywords, look up CPC codes, review CPC definitions, retrieve patents by CPC code, analyze the closest patents, search the application database, and broaden with keyword and non-patent searches.
- The CPC schema is the key organizational tool for finding all patents in a specific technology area — use it before and after keyword searches.
- Reviewing patent claims (not just abstracts) provides the most accurate assessment of whether a prior patent covers your invention.
- Espacenet at espacenet.com provides access to foreign patent publications using the same CPC classification system.
- Non-patent literature — Google searches, journals, books, and library databases — can also constitute prior art and must be included in a comprehensive search.
- A pre-filing search is not required but can prevent infringement risk, wasted application costs, and missed opportunities to strengthen the specification.
