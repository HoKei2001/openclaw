## Overview

This video features a candid interview with Brian Walker, a self-taught inventor who earned over $10 million in royalties from a single toy product — the "light chaser" — that has been sold in Disney theme parks, Universal Studios, and Barnum & Bailey circuses for over 18 years. Brian describes his unconventional background: raised with dyslexia and ADHD without formal education beyond high school, he taught himself everything from metalwork to fiberglass construction, and eventually prototyped more than 800 inventions throughout his life. The light chaser itself took just three hours to build from parts he already had, and earned him nothing initially until it broke into theme park environments where the dark atmosphere made it captivate crowds.

The conversation explores the trajectory from near-failure to massive royalty payments — ranging from $75,000 to $350,000 per quarter at peak — and the personal challenges of managing that success as a bipolar individual who finds inventing effortless but adult responsibilities like follow-up calls and business development extremely difficult. Brian explains why he joined inventRight's coaching community: not primarily for the money, but for the accountability structure and human connection that kept him on track. He warns aspiring inventors about the mental fortitude needed to handle rejection, having faced approximately 40 rejections in his recent licensing campaign.

The video closes with practical advice that applies broadly to creative inventors: finish one invention before moving to the next, think through every possible angle of your invention before filing a provisional patent, and understand that licensing is ideal for those who want to create without running a business. Brian emphasizes that licensing timelines can range from six months to over a year depending on product complexity, tooling requirements, and how much additional development the manufacturer must invest.

## Key Takeaways

- A single invention built in three hours generated $10 million in royalties over 18 years — proof that simplicity and right placement matter more than complexity
- Inventors prone to "jumping around" between ideas must discipline themselves to complete and license one product before starting another
- Provisional patents are only valuable if you push toward conversion within the one-year window; filed-and-forgotten provisionals expire worthlessly
- Theme park and closed-environment distribution can transform a struggling product into a breakout hit by controlling the demonstration context
- Royalty checks grow with sales volume and product placement — quarterly checks scaled from $75K to $350K as Disney and Universal expanded distribution
- Licensing is the right model for creative inventors who do not want to manage manufacturing, inventory, distribution, or a business
- Rejection is the normal state for inventors; stomach for it, and accountability from coaches or communities, makes the difference between persisting and quitting

## Best For

Inventors and creators at any stage who want an honest, story-driven perspective on the emotional and financial realities of licensing consumer products.
