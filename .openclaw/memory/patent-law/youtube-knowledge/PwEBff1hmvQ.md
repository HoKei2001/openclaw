# How to File a Foreign Patent Application

**Channel**: John Ferrell, Esq.
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PwEBff1hmvQ
**Published**: 2022-12-15

---

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell provides a practical overview of the foreign patent filing landscape for inventors who have already filed a U.S. patent application. The video focuses on country-by-country (national phase) filing rather than treaty-based routes such as the PCT or the European Patent Office, covering key concepts like absolute novelty requirements, translation costs, and the wide variability in examination rigor across jurisdictions.

A critical point is that many countries have an absolute novelty requirement, meaning inventors may need to file simultaneously in those countries on the same day as their U.S. filing — or at least before any public disclosure. Ferrell notes that most of the world's approximately 195 countries have patent systems, with translation costs running roughly 20 to 40 cents per word and national filing fees varying widely (from roughly $100 in Honduras to over $1,000 in the UK). Annual maintenance fees are also charged in most countries as a condition of continuing ownership.

Ferrell's practical recommendation is to work through a U.S. patent attorney who maintains relationships with trusted foreign associate attorneys, rather than searching for foreign counsel independently. Since U.S. patent applications are generally prepared to the highest standard and are accepted with minor formatting adjustments in most other jurisdictions, starting with a well-drafted U.S. application provides the best foundation for international filing.

## Key Takeaways

- Foreign patent filing can be done country by country, via the PCT (156 countries), the European Patent Office (38 countries), or regional African treaties
- Most countries require translation into the native language, at approximately 20–40 cents per word
- Many countries have an absolute novelty requirement — requiring filing before any public disclosure or simultaneously with the U.S. filing
- Filing fees vary enormously: approximately $100 in Honduras, over $1,000 in the UK depending on claims and page count
- Examination rigor varies widely: some countries conduct rigorous prior art review, others are essentially registration-based
- Patent laws change constantly, so always verify current requirements with local counsel before filing
- Work through a U.S. patent attorney who maintains established relationships with vetted foreign associate attorneys
- The U.S. patent application format is generally acceptable worldwide with minor formatting adjustments

## Best For

U.S. inventors considering international patent protection who want a practical, cost-conscious introduction to country-by-country foreign filing strategy before consulting their patent attorney.
