## Overview

Attorney Elizabeth Potts Weinstein walks through a real, live trademark office action response in this tutorial, using an actual office action on her own trademark ("Elizabeth Off-Grid") as the demonstration example. While the video title mentions trademarks, the process is instructive for understanding USPTO office action mechanics generally. The particular office action addressed is a disclaimer requirement — one of the simpler and more common types — making it an accessible demonstration of what an office action looks like and how the response process works.

The video begins by showing how office action notifications work: an email notification arrives, but the actual office action document must be retrieved from the USPTO's TSDR (Trademark Status and Document Retrieval) system, not the email itself. The document includes the issuance date, the response deadline (three months, with a possible three-month extension for a fee), the examining attorney's name (who can be emailed directly for simple resolutions), and the specific objection. For this case, the objection requires a disclaimer of the phrase "off-grid" as merely descriptive — the examiner attached dictionary evidence to support the requirement.

The tutorial walks through the actual response submission via the USPTO's online form system, showing how to navigate to the response form, select the applicable response type (disclaimer, in this case), enter the disclaimer language from the office action, sign, validate, and submit. The video also covers nuanced strategic thinking about when to agree to a disclaimer (usually yes, for genuinely generic or descriptive terms) versus when to resist (when the term has a distinctive meaning in the specific context and legal arguments can be constructed).

## Key Takeaways

- USPTO office action notifications arrive by email but the actual office action document must be retrieved from TSDR using the application serial number
- Response deadlines begin from the mailing date of the office action, not the date it is read; for trademarks, three months are allowed with an optional three-month extension for a fee
- For simple issues (like disclaimers), emailing the examining attorney directly can resolve the matter without a formal response filing
- Emails to the examining attorney become part of the publicly available record — email address and content are visible in TSDR
- The online response form is accessed through the USPTO's apply-online portal, not through the TSDR system
- Agreeing to a disclaimer of a merely descriptive term is usually appropriate; resisting requires a legal argument about the term's distinctive use in the specific trademark context
- Unsolicited outreach from attorneys after an office action is issued is common and legally permissible but may not reflect quality representation

## Best For

Trademark applicants who have received an office action requiring a disclaimer and want a step-by-step live walkthrough of how to navigate the USPTO online form and file the response themselves.
