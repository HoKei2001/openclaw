## Overview

This instructional video from Miami University Libraries provides a general overview of how to search for U.S. patents using the USPTO's recommended six-step search strategy. Presented by a Science and Engineering Librarian, the video is designed for students, researchers, and inventors who are new to patent searching and need a structured starting point. The video is clear about its scope: it is a general introduction, not a substitute for professional search assistance when preparing an actual patent application.

The six steps are organized into three phases. The basic phase involves brainstorming terms that describe the invention (purpose, composition, use, component parts, synonyms) and then conducting a keyword search in the USPTO's Patent Public Search interface, followed by an in-depth review of the documents found (drawings, specification, claims). The expansion phase involves using CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) codes to find additional patents grouped by technical category, and reviewing cited references listed on the front pages of relevant patents. The broad phase involves searching international patents through Espacenet (the European Patent Office's database), non-patent literature in library databases, and potentially engaging a patent search professional.

The video uses a unicycle frame as a running example throughout, demonstrating how terms like "bicycle," "frame," "wheels," "pedals," and "seat" can serve as search starting points, and how CPC codes help group similar inventions together for more thorough discovery.

## Key Takeaways

- The USPTO recommends a six-step patent search strategy covering basic search, expansion with CPC codes, and broadening to international sources.
- Step 1: Brainstorm keywords including purpose, components, use, synonyms, and related terms for your invention.
- Step 2: Search the USPTO's Patent Public Search database using those keywords.
- Step 3: Review found patents in depth — examine drawings, specification, and especially the claims.
- Step 4: Use CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) codes to find additional patents grouped by technical field.
- Step 5: Review cited references on the front page of relevant patents to discover additional prior art.
- Step 6: Expand to Espacenet (EPO international patents), non-patent literature, and professional search services.
- This video is a general overview only; actual patent application searches should involve a PTRC or patent attorney.
