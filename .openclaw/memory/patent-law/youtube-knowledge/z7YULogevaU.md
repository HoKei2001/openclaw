## Overview

This video is a recording of an online IMPACT lecture series session hosted by Cherry Technological University (India), featuring IP lawyer Hanish Krishnan and patent engineer Santosh. The session is aimed at budding entrepreneurs and university students who need a foundational understanding of intellectual property rights, patent drafting, and prior art search. Hanish Krishnan opens with an overview of the major IP types — trademarks, patents, designs, copyrights, and geographical indications — emphasizing how each protects a different aspect of innovation (name, technology, shape, artistic work) and why startups should understand these distinctions when choosing how to protect their ideas.

The session then moves to the core patent requirements under Indian patent law: the invention must be new, involve an inventive step (meeting at least one of three criteria — economic significance, technological advancement, or both), and be capable of industrial application. The territorial nature of patents is explained: a patent granted in India protects only within India, requiring separate filings in each additional country where protection is desired, though major patent systems (Indian Patent Office, EPO, USPTO) follow largely similar substantive rules with differing formalities.

Patent engineer Santosh then covers practical patent search methodology and drafting concepts, building on the IP framework established in the first part. Throughout, the session emphasizes that patents are a startup's primary intangible assets and that commercializing ideas through IP protection is both a legal strategy and a business necessity in the current entrepreneurial environment.

## Key Takeaways

- Major IP types for startups: trademarks (name), patents (technology), designs (shape), copyright (artistic/literary/software works)
- Patents require three conditions under Indian law: novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability
- Inventive step under the Indian Patents Act requires economic significance, technological advancement, or both
- Patents are territorial — a patent granted in India only provides rights in India; separate filings are needed in each target country
- Every country has its own patent office with largely similar substantive requirements but different formal requirements
- IP protection for startups is fundamentally about preventing copying and enabling commercialization and financial gains
- Prior art search and patent specification drafting are the two core practical skills for protecting innovations
- Startups' primary intangible assets are their intellectual property rights — understanding IP protection is essential from day one

## Best For

Entrepreneurship students and early-stage startup founders in India who want a foundational introduction to the types of intellectual property, core patent requirements under Indian law, and the basics of patent drafting and prior art search.
