## Overview

This video from UpCounsel provides a comprehensive breakdown of the costs associated with obtaining and maintaining a patent throughout its full lifecycle. It covers all major cost categories from the initial filing decision through ongoing maintenance fees, helping inventors create realistic budgets before pursuing patent protection.

The video distinguishes between the three main patent types and their associated cost ranges. A provisional patent application is the lowest-cost entry point at $130 to $300, providing 12 months of patent pending status without resulting in an actual patent. Utility patents, which protect how an invention works, range from $10,000 to $20,000 or more in total. Design patents, protecting the ornamental appearance of an invention, are less expensive at $2,000 to $4,000. The complexity of the invention, number of claims, need for detailed drawings, and potential revisions all push costs higher.

The video also addresses the cost of professional patent searches ($1,000 to $3,000), the value of patent attorneys despite their hourly rates of $200 to $500 (as they help avoid costly mistakes and handle office actions efficiently), and the significant costs of international protection through the Patent Cooperation Treaty ($3,000 to $10,000 or more depending on countries, plus translation fees, national phase entry fees, and local attorney costs). Importantly, the video reminds inventors that utility patent costs do not end at issuance — maintenance fees are due at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after grant, ranging from approximately $400 to $7,700 and increasing over time.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application costs $130 to $300 and is the most affordable way to establish a filing date, but it does not result in an issued patent.
- Utility patents typically cost $10,000 to $20,000 or more in total; design patents are comparatively less expensive at $2,000 to $4,000.
- Patent searches cost $1,000 to $3,000 and are a valuable investment to avoid wasting money on an application for something already patented.
- International protection via PCT filing adds $3,000 to $10,000 or more, plus per-country translation fees, national phase fees, and local attorney costs.
- Patent attorneys charge $200 to $500 per hour but can save money by avoiding errors, ensuring completeness, and handling office actions efficiently.
- Utility patent maintenance fees are due at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after issuance and increase over time, representing a significant ongoing cost.
- Filing a patent yourself reduces upfront costs, but incomplete applications, insufficient searches, and weak claims often result in rejections, additional fees, or inadequately protective patents.
