# You Cannot Hire a Patent Attorney Without Asking These Questions First!

**Channel**: The Patent Guru
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NLowcrt-aYk
**Published**: 2021-06-28

## Overview

Jubin Dana of Dana Legal Services presents two critical mistakes that inventors make when selecting a patent attorney, framing both as avoidable with the right questions asked upfront. The video is concise and practical, structured around the insight that most inventors approach attorney selection in ways that reliably lead to poor outcomes — either by focusing on the wrong metric (price) or by failing to probe whether the attorney truly understands the business context of the invention.

The first mistake is asking about price rather than time. When an inventor asks "how much will this cost?" they receive a number that tells them nothing about the quality of work they will receive. Dana illustrates this with a simple calculation: a $4,000 quote at a $400/hour billing rate implies only 10 hours of work — far less than most experienced attorneys consider adequate for a quality patent application. Instead, inventors should ask how many hours the attorney plans to spend on the application. This allows for a like-for-like comparison across attorneys and reveals whether a low price reflects efficiency or corner-cutting.

The second mistake is failing to investigate whether the attorney will connect the invention to the inventor's revenue model. Most attorneys ask to understand the idea, provide a cost estimate, and proceed — never asking how the inventor intends to make money. Dana argues this produces a patent that may protect the idea legally while being strategically useless because it does not protect the revenue stream. An attorney who asks about the business model can draft claims that protect the mechanisms by which the invention generates revenue, not just its technical features.

## Key Takeaways

- Asking about price when selecting an attorney reveals nothing about work quality; ask for estimated hours instead, then use the billing rate to derive the cost
- A low fixed fee may imply only a few hours of work — far less than a quality patent application typically requires
- Most attorneys never ask about the inventor's revenue model, producing patents that protect the idea but not the business
- An attorney who understands the revenue model can draft claims that protect the commercial mechanisms of the invention, not just its technical features
- Inventors who lack a developed revenue model should clarify that before approaching any attorney, since the patent strategy depends on knowing how the invention generates value
- The right questions to ask an attorney: How long will it take you? What is your billing rate? How do you incorporate the revenue model into patent claim strategy?

## Best For

First-time inventors who are ready to hire a patent attorney and want to avoid the two most common mistakes that lead to expensive, strategically weak patent applications.
