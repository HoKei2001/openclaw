# Conducting a Search for Prior Art Using Google Patents

**Channel**: Fast Forward Medical Innovation – Commercialization Education
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lJoqSlonZjM
**Published**: 2020-09-28

---

## Overview

This short, practical video from the Fast Forward Medical Innovation channel demonstrates how to use Google Patents to conduct a prior art search. The video is screen-recorded and walks through the full search workflow from entering keywords to reading a complete patent document.

The demonstration uses the tongue depressor patent as an example. The presenter shows how to type keywords into the Google Patents search bar and how to check the "include non-patent literature" option to extend the search to Google Scholar articles. Results appear ranked by relevance and can be filtered using the left-hand sidebar — for example, to show only granted US patents. Each result displays the patent office, patent number, inventor, current assignee, filing and grant dates, and a brief excerpt.

When a specific patent is opened, the viewer can access the abstract, drawings, classifications, the panel showing key dates and assignment history, the claims, the detailed description, a list of cited supporting patents, and a list of publications where the patent is cited. The video also shows how to download a full PDF copy of the patent directly from the top of the page.

## Key Takeaways

- Google Patents covers patents from multiple countries worldwide and can also search Google Scholar articles via the "include non-patent literature" checkbox.
- Results are ranked by relevance to the search terms, with left-hand filters to narrow by country, status (granted), and date.
- Each result shows patent number, inventor, assignee, filing date, grant date, and a short excerpt.
- The full patent record includes the abstract, drawings, classifications, claims, and detailed description.
- The claims section is more informative than the abstract for determining whether a patent is truly relevant to your invention.
- Cited patents and cited publications on the patent page are useful entry points for finding additional prior art.
- Full PDF copies of patents can be downloaded directly from the top of the patent page.

## Best For

Inventors and researchers who want a quick, beginner-friendly introduction to using Google Patents for conducting a prior art search before filing.
