## Overview

Trademark attorney Brian Hall of Traverse Legal PLC provides a step-by-step walkthrough of how to file a trademark application with the USPTO using the Trademark Electronic Application System (TEAS). The video guides viewers through the TEAS Plus and TEAS forms, applicant information entry, entity type selection, and the mark entry section — including standard character marks versus special form stylized and design marks.

## Key Takeaways

- To file a trademark application, go to uspto.gov, navigate to Trademarks, and use the TEAS (Trademark Electronic Application System) to file online
- The TEAS Plus form ($275 filing fee) is cheaper than the TEAS form ($345) because it requires more complete information upfront; for most applicants following a step-by-step guide, TEAS Plus is recommended
- The owner of the mark is the entity or individual who will be the registrant; choosing an individual owner can expose that person to personal liability for trademark infringement, which is why many people form a corporate entity (LLC or corporation) to own the mark
- Standard character marks protect only the word or term itself; special form or stylized marks protect a specific logo, font, color, or design element — if you have a logo, you need the special form
- All information entered in a trademark application becomes public record and is searchable by anyone
- Providing an email address and authorizing email communication from USPTO is recommended to avoid delays from postal correspondence
- Optional fields on the mark entry screen include disclaimers, prior registrations, translations, transliterations, meanings of specific terms, and Section 2(f) acquired distinctiveness claims — a trademark attorney can help navigate these

## Topics Covered

- Navigating to the USPTO trademark filing system (TEAS)
- Choosing between TEAS Plus and TEAS filing forms and their fee differences
- Principal register versus supplemental register
- Entering applicant information: individual versus corporate or LLC entity types
- Liability implications of trademark ownership structure
- How to enter the trademark mark itself: standard character versus special form/stylized design
- Previewing the mark and attaching JPEG images for logo marks
- Claiming or not claiming color as a feature of the mark
- Mark description requirements for logo trademarks

## Best For

Individuals and business owners filing their own trademark application for the first time, particularly those protecting a word or phrase (standard character mark) or a logo (special form mark) in the United States.
