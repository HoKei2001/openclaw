# What is the first step to patenting my invention

**Published**: 2023-02-07

## Overview

This is a short, focused clip from Bold Patents Law Firm in which managing partner J.D. Houvener addresses the question of what the very first step should be for someone who wants to patent their invention. The advice is directed at inventors who may be in the early stages of developing an idea and are unsure when or how to engage with the patent process.

Houvener emphasizes the distinction between an idea and an invention. He points out that while ideas are common, true inventions — ones that have been thought through sufficiently that someone else could go and build them based on your explanation — are rare and valuable. He describes this as the threshold at which an inventor is ready to reach out to a patent attorney. Once an inventor can clearly explain their concept to another person who could then build it, even without a physical prototype, that idea has matured into an invention.

The recommended first step after reaching this threshold is to consult a patent attorney to explore patent eligibility and patentability. The video recommends education — reading about patent law, understanding the differences between patents, trademarks, and copyrights — as an important preparatory step before that consultation.

## Key Takeaways

- The first step is education: learn what a patent is, how it differs from a trademark, and what makes something patentable.
- Ideas are a dime a dozen; an invention is an idea developed to the point where someone skilled in the field could build it based on your description.
- You do not need to have built a prototype — you just need to be able to clearly explain the invention.
- Once you reach the stage of having a describable invention, contact a patent attorney to explore patent eligibility and patentability.
- Patent eligibility and patentability are two distinct questions that an attorney can help you navigate.
- Early engagement with a patent attorney can save time and money by ensuring you are on the right track before investing in a full application.

## Best For

First-time inventors in the very early stages who are unsure whether they have something patentable and want to understand when to take their first formal step toward protection.
