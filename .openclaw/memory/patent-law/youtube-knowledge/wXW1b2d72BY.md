## Overview

StartupWise presents a beginner's guide to filing a provisional patent application using LegalZoom as the filing platform. The video covers both the conceptual foundations — what a provisional patent is, why to file one, and what should be in it — and a full walkthrough of the LegalZoom submission interface. The presenter frames the provisional patent as the first protective step after creating an invention, enabling a 12-month iteration window during which inventors can refine their product and file multiple provisional patents as the invention evolves.

The video outlines seven elements that a strong provisional patent application should include: a description of the problem solved, a clear solution description with story-driven drawings, identification of novel features versus competitors, variations and alternatives, manufacturing and materials strategy, detailed explanation of use and operation, and anticipation of potential objections. The LegalZoom walkthrough covers the intake form (brief invention description, prior disclosure questions, inventorship information, invention type), options for professional illustration and patent search add-ons, entity status selection, and payment. Total cost for a micro entity through LegalZoom is shown as $259 ($199 service fee plus $60 USPTO fee).

A useful FAQ section addresses whether a provisional can be filed for a design patent (no — provisionals are only for utility patents), the 12-month development window, and the distinction between utility patents (how an invention works) and design patents (how it looks).

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application protects an invention for 12 months, allowing iteration before the full patent is filed
- Multiple provisional patents can be filed during the 12-month window as the invention evolves
- Seven elements for a strong provisional: problem description, solution with drawings, novel features, variations, manufacturing strategy, use/operation explanation, and anticipated objections
- Provisional applications cannot be filed for design patents — they are for utility patents only
- LegalZoom charges $199 in service fees plus the USPTO filing fee ($60 for micro entity), totaling approximately $259
- LegalZoom provides peace-of-mind review, illustration digitization, and USPTO submission services
- Entity status (micro, small, or large) determines the USPTO filing fee portion of the total cost
- The 12-month window should be used to refine the invention, gather market feedback, and prepare the non-provisional application

Beginning inventors and entrepreneurs who want a clear, step-by-step walkthrough of both the conceptual requirements and the LegalZoom platform process for filing a provisional patent application affordably.
