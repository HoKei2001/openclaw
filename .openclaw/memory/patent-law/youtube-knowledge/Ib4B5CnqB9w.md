## Overview

This brief video explains the first step in the patent application process: conducting a prior art search. It defines prior art search as researching all existing products, inventions, patents, patent applications, and non-patent literature from around the world that are similar to the invention being considered for protection. The fundamental patentability criteria of novelty and non-obviousness make this search essential before investing in a full application.

## Key Takeaways

- A prior art search is the first step in the patent application process
- The search covers existing patents, patent applications, and non-patent literature worldwide
- The two core patentability requirements are novelty (newness) and non-obviousness
- Conducting a prior art search before filing prevents wasting money on a non-patentable invention
- The search should look at both patent databases and broader literature sources

## Best For

Beginning inventors who want a one-minute introduction to why a prior art search is the essential first step before pursuing a patent application.
