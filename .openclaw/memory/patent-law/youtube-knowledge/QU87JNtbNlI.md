## Overview

This brief Q&A clip from Bold Patents features J.D. Houvener responding to a viewer who holds both a Canadian and US patent on an oil drilling invention that has been granted for three years but never commercialized. The exchange is notable for its candid acknowledgment that capital-intensive industrial inventions face a different commercialization landscape than consumer products — manufacturing and engineering costs are high enough that building the product independently is often not realistic, making licensing the most viable path.

Houvener outlines two practical approaches. The first is a formal licensing process: commission a third-party patent valuation using a market-based methodology (available even pre-revenue), prepare a professional sales sheet, and then engage a broker — specifically mentioning Bold Patents' own brokering service — to approach potential licensees and present the valuation and marketability assessment. The second approach is a more proactive monitoring strategy: watch the market for companies that may be infringing or approaching infringement on the patent claims, then use a polite letter to open a conversation framing infringement as an opportunity to partner rather than a legal threat. He also recommends assembling a board of advisors with subject matter expertise in the target industry, noting these individuals are often the first investors as well.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital-intensive patents (e.g., industrial equipment, oil drilling) often require licensing because self-manufacturing is cost-prohibitive
- A market-based patent valuation — available even pre-revenue — is the foundation for a credible licensing conversation
- Patent brokers can represent patent holders in outreach, presenting valuations and commercialization assessments to potential licensees
- Monitoring the market for near-infringers and reaching out diplomatically can convert enforcement situations into licensing partnerships
- A board of advisors with domain expertise serves a dual purpose: commercialization guidance and early investor relationships
- Patent trolling (aggressive enforcement-first outreach) is distinguished from strategic licensing — Houvener frames the latter as the preferred approach

## Best For

Inventors and patent holders in industrial or high-cost-to-manufacture sectors who need a realistic framework for commercializing a granted patent that has not yet generated revenue.
