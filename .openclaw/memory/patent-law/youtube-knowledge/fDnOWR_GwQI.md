# How to Get a Provisional Patent Application on File Fast

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Farrell explains how to prepare and file a "quick and dirty" provisional patent application when time is short — such as before a manufacturer meeting the next day or when an inventor lacks the resources for a formal filing. The video covers when a provisional application actually gets reviewed (rarely), what must be included to make the application sufficient, and a recommended four-section structure for a complete but expedient provisional.

## Key Takeaways

- There are no formality requirements for a provisional patent application — applications can be handwritten, drawn in watercolor, or assembled from PowerPoint decks and photographs; the only legal requirement is that the provisional contain sufficient detail to fully support the teaching of the invention
- A provisional patent application is never examined by the USPTO, and there is no reminder when the 12-month expiration is approaching; inventors must track this deadline themselves
- A provisional application may be reviewed in two circumstances: (1) during examination of the non-provisional if an examiner questions whether claim amendments are supported by the original disclosure, and (2) during litigation if the priority date of the provisional needs to be established to predate a piece of prior art
- Because these scenarios do occur (even if rarely), it is critical that the provisional contain enough detail to support future claim elements — using the bicycle handlebar example, a photograph of handlebar streamers in the prototype could save a patent from rejection years later
- Every provisional patent application should include four key sections: (1) a specific, descriptive title; (2) a brief summary or abstract of the invention (how it is built and used); (3) figures — which can be informal sketches, photographs, or PowerPoint slides; and (4) an explanation of how the invention is built and used
- Figures are especially important: they can be informal but should be comprehensive; include more figures rather than fewer, organized from high-level overview down to component detail; annotating figures with notes is acceptable and often helpful
- Supporting documents such as trade show brochures, white papers, conference papers, or PowerPoint presentations can be attached directly to the back of the provisional as the detailed description section

- Why the provisional is never examined and what the 12-month deadline means
