## Overview

This video from Patents Demystified directly refutes the widespread claim that software and ideas cannot be patented, explaining the technical nuance that gives rise to this misconception and clarifying how software inventions are routinely and effectively protected. The presenter, a practicing patent attorney, notes that he works on software patent applications on an almost daily basis.

The confusion stems from a technical distinction: the law specifically prohibits patent claims that say "I claim software that does X, Y, and Z." However, the same protection is fully achievable through three alternative claim formats that are well established in patent practice. The first is a computer-implemented method claim: "A computer-implemented method of doing X, Y, and Z, comprising the steps of..." The second is a computer-readable medium claim: "A computer-readable medium that, when executed by a processor, performs the steps of X, Y, and Z." The third is a system claim: "A computer configured to perform X, Y, and Z." While you are technically not claiming the software itself, you are claiming the method, the storage medium, or the hardware system — and these claims are functionally equivalent to protecting the software. Similarly, ideas are routinely patentable as long as the inventor can describe the idea in sufficient detail for a person of ordinary skill in the relevant field to make and use it.

The video emphasizes the real-world harm caused by this misconception: inventors and software companies who believe their technology cannot be patented often do not file an application before publicly disclosing the product. Public disclosure, public use, or an offer for sale starts (and in some jurisdictions immediately forecloses) the clock on patent rights, meaning delay can result in permanently forfeiting protection for commercially valuable intellectual property. The presenter urges anyone with questions about patentability to consult a patent attorney rather than rely on general statements found online.

## Key Takeaways

- The statement "software cannot be patented" is misleading — software can be effectively patented through computer-implemented method claims, computer-readable medium claims, or system claims.
- These three claim formats achieve the same protection as a direct software claim while complying with the technical legal requirement against claiming software itself.
- Ideas are also generally patentable as long as the inventor can describe them in sufficient detail for a person of ordinary skill in the field to make and use the invention.
- No prototype is required — inventors can describe multiple variations and embodiments without having built anything.
- Believing that software or ideas cannot be patented leads inventors to delay filing, which can result in permanently forfeiting patent rights after public disclosure.
- Public disclosure, public use, or an offer for sale can start or immediately terminate the window for filing a patent application, depending on the jurisdiction.
- When in doubt about patentability, consult a patent attorney with experience in the relevant technology area rather than relying on general online information.
