## Overview

This video from The Patent Guru provides a practical guide to drafting patent claims for a non-provisional utility patent application. Claims are the most legally significant part of any patent — they define the precise scope of protection granted and are the basis for determining both infringement and validity. The presenter explains that claim drafting is a specialized skill that requires understanding not just the invention itself, but how to frame it in the language and structure that patent law requires.

The video distinguishes between the two primary types of utility patent claims: system claims (also called apparatus claims) and method claims. System claims describe a physical device or system in terms of its structural components and how those components are connected or interact. Method claims describe a process as a sequence of steps. Both claim types must satisfy the same structural requirements: each element or step must be connected to the others by functional language that shows how they relate, and the claim must be complete — no element can be introduced that is not later referred to, and no element should be missing if it is essential to the invention.

The presenter walks through the anatomy of a well-formed claim: a preamble (identifying the claim category, such as "A system for..." or "A method of..."), a transitional phrase (typically "comprising" for open-ended claims or "consisting of" for closed claims), and the body (listing each element or step). The use of "comprising" is strongly preferred in most patents because it means the claim covers the listed elements plus any additional elements, providing broader protection. The video also explains the relationship between independent claims (which stand alone and define the broadest scope) and dependent claims (which narrow the independent claim by adding additional limitations), and recommends a claim strategy that includes multiple independent and dependent claims to maximize protection.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent claims define the legal scope of protection and are the most important part of any patent application.
- The two primary claim types are system/apparatus claims (structural components) and method claims (process steps).
- Each element or step in a claim must be functionally connected to the other elements — isolated or unrelated elements weaken a claim.
- A preamble identifies the claim category; a transitional phrase links it to the body; the body lists each element or step.
- "Comprising" is the preferred transitional word because it creates open-ended coverage (the invention plus any additional elements).
- "Consisting of" creates closed claims — the invention contains only the listed elements — and should be used only when exclusivity is essential.
- Independent claims define the broadest scope; dependent claims narrow the scope by adding additional limitations.
- A robust claim strategy includes multiple independent and dependent claims to provide layered protection.

## Best For

Inventors and first-time patent applicants who want to understand the structure, terminology, and strategy behind drafting effective patent claims before working with a patent attorney or attempting to draft their own non-provisional application.
