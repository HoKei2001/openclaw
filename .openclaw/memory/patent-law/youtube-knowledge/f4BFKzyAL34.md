# Patent Search in Three Simple Steps

**Channel**: John Ferrell, Esq.
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f4BFKzyAL34
**Published**: 2023-07-20

---

## Overview

Patent attorney John Ferrell walks inventors through a quick, accessible three-step method for doing a preliminary patent search before deciding whether to invest in professional services. The approach is designed to take less than three minutes and requires no specialized tools beyond a web browser. The goal is to give inventors a reasonable first-pass assessment of whether their invention already exists in some form.

Step one is a basic internet search using any search engine, including an image search, to see what products and ideas already exist in the marketplace. Ferrell cautions inventors to evaluate results carefully — many results describe aspirational or hypothetical products rather than real inventions. Step two involves searching Google Patents, which provides access to issued patents and published applications in natural language, making it easy for non-specialists to use. He emphasizes being careful when reading patents, as they can be difficult to interpret without expertise. Step three is using ChatGPT as a supplemental search tool, while warning about privacy risks (avoid disclosing invention details) and the "hallucination" problem — the model may fabricate product names or references that do not exist.

The video closes by recommending a professional patent search for high-stakes situations such as startup fundraising or pre-manufacturing, as a professional search will be far more thorough and include expert analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- Step 1: Use a regular search engine with relevant keywords, plus an image search, to identify existing products and ideas.
- Step 2: Search Google Patents using natural language to find issued patents and published applications related to your invention.
- Step 3: Use ChatGPT as a supplemental tool, but verify any product names or references it provides and avoid disclosing invention details.
- Patent search results require careful interpretation — expired patents still constitute prior art, and patent language can be difficult to parse.
- ChatGPT's training data has a knowledge cutoff, so it may miss recent patents; applications are not published until 18 months after filing.
- The USPTO website has a more powerful but harder-to-use search tool compared to Google Patents.
- For high-stakes decisions (e.g., fundraising, manufacturing), a professional patent search with attorney analysis is strongly recommended.
- Do not be discouraged if similar inventions appear — differences in how something is performed may still allow for patentability.

## Best For

Individual inventors or early-stage entrepreneurs who want to do a fast, informal check on whether their invention idea is new before spending money on professional patent services.
