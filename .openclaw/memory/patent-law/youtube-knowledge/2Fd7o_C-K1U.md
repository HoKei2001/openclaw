# Final Rejection Playbook: Amend, Interview, or Appeal

## Overview

This short video from PowerPatent explains the three strategic options available to a patent applicant after receiving a Final Office Action (FOA) from the USPTO. Receiving a final rejection is not the end of the patent prosecution process — it is a decision point that requires careful strategic thinking about which path forward best serves the applicant's goals and budget.

The first option is to amend the claims to overcome the examiner's rejections. This may involve filing a Request for Continued Examination (RCE), which reopens prosecution and allows further claim amendments and arguments. In some cases, minor amendments that clearly overcome the rejection may be considered by the examiner without an RCE. The second option is to request an examiner interview, which provides a direct conversation — in person or virtually — to discuss the rejections, explore potential claim language, and find common ground. Good preparation with proposed claim language is essential to making an interview productive. The third option is to appeal to the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB), which provides an independent review of the examiner's decision and requires a comprehensive appeal brief.

The video emphasizes that the choice among these three paths is strategic and depends on the specific merits of the case, the cost of each option, and the probability of a successful outcome. Consulting with a patent attorney to weigh these factors is strongly recommended.

## Key Takeaways

- A Final Office Action (FOA) is not the end of patent prosecution — three formal response paths remain available.
- Option 1: Amend claims, either through minor amendments or by filing a Request for Continued Examination (RCE).
- Option 2: Request an examiner interview to discuss rejections directly and explore acceptable claim language.
- Option 3: Appeal to the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) for independent review of the examiner's decision.
- An appeal requires a thorough appeal brief detailing why the examiner's rejections are incorrect.
- The choice between amend, interview, and appeal should weigh cost against probability of success.
- Consulting a patent attorney after a final rejection is strongly recommended to choose the right path.

## Best For

This video is most useful for inventors and applicants currently in patent prosecution who have received a final rejection and need to understand their strategic options for moving forward.
