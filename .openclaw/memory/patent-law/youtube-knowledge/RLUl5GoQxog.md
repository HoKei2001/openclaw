## Overview

This video by Abhay Porwal addresses the patent drafting question format in the Indian Patent Agent Examination (P2C3 — the 30-mark drafting question). The presenter provides a structured framework for decoding invention disclosures and converting them into complete patent applications within exam time constraints, working through two full example questions from the 2024 exam: a rocket-assisted aircraft system for short runway operations, and a titanium wire/carbon fiber composite material for vehicle construction.

The core methodology is to approach the structured invention disclosure (problem statement, existing solutions, challenges, objective, implementation details, and technical advantages) as a source document, extracting information directly into each section of the patent application. The presenter recommends starting with a rough draft of the claims first — even before writing the title or background — because the claims provide the structural backbone from which summary, abstract, detailed description, and diagrams can all be derived. One independent system or apparatus claim plus one method claim (as a dependent or second independent claim) covers both angles efficiently.

The video also addresses Format 2 (the official Indian patent application form), the required patent agent signature placement, the use of reference numerals in claims and abstracts, and the principle that exam answers should be directly grounded in information provided in the disclosure rather than assumed or invented.

## Key Takeaways

- In the P2C3 exam format, the invention disclosure is structured into problem, solutions, challenges, objective, implementation, and advantages — map each part to the appropriate specification section
- Always draft the claims first (even in rough), because they provide the structure for summary, abstract, and detailed description
- An independent system claim plus a method claim covers both product and process perspectives efficiently
- The summary is a plain-English paraphrase of the system claim; the abstract is a plain-English translation of claim one
- Diagrams (block diagram for system claim, flowchart for method claim) can be derived directly from the claim structure
- The background section ends with a "therefore there is a need for" statement that sets up the invention
- Use reference numerals (e.g., 100, 102, 104) consistently across diagrams, detailed description, claims, and abstract
- Do not assume or invent information beyond what the disclosure provides; treat it as an open-book exam
