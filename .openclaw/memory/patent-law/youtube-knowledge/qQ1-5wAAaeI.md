# How to Conduct Prior Art Search for Patent?

**Channel**: Hardware Massive
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qQ1-5wAAaeI
**Published**: 2015-10-25

---

## Overview

This video, presented in an interview-style format, features a patent attorney explaining both the practical and strategic value of prior art search before filing a patent application. The presenter makes the case that a prior art search is not just a formality: without one, inventors risk paying attorneys' fees and filing fees only to receive a rejection from the USPTO based on art the examiner immediately finds. Even experts in their own field cannot possibly know all relevant developments worldwide, making an objective search essential.

Beyond avoiding unnecessary costs, the presenter explains that prior art findings actively improve the quality of the patent application. Knowing what exists allows inventors to focus their specification on what is genuinely new, draft claims that start from a realistic position close to what the examiner will ultimately allow, and pre-emptively distinguish the invention from close prior art in the specification text — text that cannot be added after filing. The presenter also notes that prior art can inspire broader or defensive claim strategies by revealing how competitors might design around the invention.

The video recommends starting with a self-conducted search using Google Patents and similar free tools, but acknowledges that a skilled professional searcher will typically find relevant art more efficiently. For important filings, a professional search shortly before the final filing decision is advised.

## Key Takeaways

- Skipping a prior art search risks wasting all drafting and filing costs when the examiner finds obvious prior art immediately
- Even field experts cannot know all relevant prior art worldwide — a search provides objective perspective
- Prior art allows the specification to include pre-emptive differentiation language, which cannot be added after filing
- Finding close prior art early narrows the scope of negotiation with the examiner, saving time and attorney fees
- Prior art can also reveal opportunities to broaden claims by identifying how competitors might design around the invention
- Google Patents and similar free databases are good starting points for self-conducted searches
- A professional searcher finds relevant art faster and more reliably than a novice inventor
- A professional search shortly before the filing decision is recommended for important applications

## Best For

Inventors and startup founders considering whether to invest in a prior art search, who want a clear explanation of both the cost-saving and quality-improving reasons to search before filing.
