# The Best Advice You'll Hear From a Patent Attorney

**Channel**: inventRightTV
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoPusKY6uVk
**Published**: 2020-05-14

## Overview

Stephen Key of inventRightTV interviews Anthony, a patent attorney from Australia, using the story of inventor Connie as a concrete example of what can go wrong with bad patent representation and what good patent attorneys do differently. Connie had a well-conceived invention but her original patent attorney drafted it poorly, missed key features, and gave up after a first examination rejection — telling her the case was hopeless. Anthony's firm took over, rewrote the specification to tell the invention's "story," and ultimately got the patent through.

The interview highlights a fundamental insight from Anthony: great patent drafting is about storytelling, not just technical description. The background section of a patent is a free opportunity to demonstrate deep understanding of the problem and position the invention as the clear solution. Examiners respond to well-told stories about why the invention is better than everything that came before. This narrative-driven approach, combined with rigorous claim drafting, is what separates patent attorneys who achieve results from those who churn out poorly protected applications.

The conversation also covers the value of working with industrial designers to move from a concept to a manufacturable product before filing, the importance of thinking about competitor workarounds from day one, and why a pending application is often more valuable than a granted patent (because pending claims can still be molded to cover infringing products). Stephen and Anthony both emphasize that patent protection is only one part of the equation — commercial success requires a business plan and knowing who you are selling to.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent drafting is about storytelling: use the background section to explain the problem and position your invention as the unique solution.
- A pending patent application is often more commercially valuable than a granted patent because its claims can still be shaped to cover competitors.
- Bad patent attorneys focus on volume; good ones build long-term relationships by genuinely investing in their clients' commercial success.
- Industrial designers can help bridge the gap between a concept and a manufacturable product — they are a valuable resource for inventors.
- Think about competitor workarounds early and describe variations in your specification to prevent easy design-arounds.
- Copycat products typically appear 18–30 months after a successful product launch, which aligns with the PCT timeline.
- Know the boundaries of your own technical knowledge and get external expertise (industrial designers, manufacturing experts) where needed.
- A provisional application gives 12 months of flexibility; updating it before the non-provisional stage is valuable.

## Best For

Inventors at any stage who want to understand what separates excellent patent attorneys from mediocre ones, and how the quality of patent drafting and the "story" it tells directly impacts commercial outcomes.
