## Overview

Patent attorney Jeff Holman walks through the process of completing the USPTO Provisional Application Cover Sheet (Form SB/16) using a real provisional patent application he prepared for a "hinged storage stand for yard tools." The video is a screen-recorded walkthrough that shows exactly how to fill in each required field using Adobe Acrobat, making it a practical hands-on tutorial for inventors preparing to file their own provisional application.

The key fields covered include: listing all inventors with their first name, last name, city of residence, and state; entering the title of the invention (copied directly from the draft application); providing a correspondence address for USPTO communications; declaring whether government funding was involved; and selecting the appropriate entity status (small entity or micro entity) to qualify for reduced filing fees. The video explains that fields outlined in red boxes are required while others are optional. The electronic signature format accepted by the USPTO — placing your name between two forward slashes (e.g., /Jeff Holman/) — is also demonstrated.

The video also captures an important moment: during a final pre-submission review of the draft, Holman realized he wanted to add several new drawings of alternative hinge designs that had come to mind. This illustrates the value of a thorough final review before filing, as it can reveal additional embodiments worth including for broader patent protection.

## Key Takeaways

- The USPTO Provisional Application Cover Sheet (Form SB/16) must be completed and filed along with the provisional patent specification and drawings.
- The form must be opened and filled in using Adobe Acrobat, not a standard PDF viewer.
- Required fields (marked in red) include inventor name, city, state, and correspondence address; other fields are optional.
- If there are multiple inventors, use the "Add" button to include each one.
- Small entity and micro entity status must be actively selected to receive the applicable fee discount.
- The USPTO-accepted electronic signature format is: /YourName/ — no notarization required.
- Always conduct a thorough final review of the draft application before filing; it often reveals additional embodiments or improvements worth including.
