# Avoid Costly Patent Filing Mistakes

## Overview

This video, presented in a Q&A format by J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm alongside a paralegal colleague, addresses a common situation inventors encounter: receiving a "Notice to File Missing Parts" from the USPTO after submitting a provisional patent application. The discussion explains what the minimum requirements for a valid provisional are — a specification (written description), a cover sheet, and figures if needed — and why insufficient disclosure is the most common underlying problem when inventors file on their own.

A key insight is that provisional patent applications are not reviewed by the USPTO for quality of disclosure; the office only flags missing administrative parts (like the cover sheet or payment). This means an inventor can file a technically complete but substantively thin provisional — with an inadequately detailed description — and only discover the problem years later when the non-provisional application is examined and the examiner finds the provisional cannot support the claims. The presenters stress that a proper provisional should be fully enabling: detailed enough for someone to make and use the invention, covering multiple versions and embodiments, not just the one specific implementation the inventor has built.

The video also warns about fraudulent solicitations that follow USPTO filings: because filer contact information becomes public, inventors receive mail and emails from third parties that look like official USPTO notices requesting additional fees. The presenters advise careful verification before responding to any post-filing correspondence that appears to request payment.

## Key Takeaways

- A valid provisional requires at minimum a specification (written description), a cover sheet, and figures if needed
- The USPTO does not review provisional applications for disclosure quality — only for missing administrative parts
- A thin, inadequately detailed provisional will only be discovered as a problem during non-provisional examination, years later
- A proper provisional should be fully enabling: detailed enough for someone skilled in the field to make and use the invention
- Provisionals should cover multiple versions and embodiments, not just the specific implementation built or planned for market
- Beware of fraudulent solicitations after filing: third parties use filer contact information to send official-looking payment requests
- Legitimate USPTO notices will flag missing parts but not tell you how to fix them — third-party notices offering to fix them for a fee are suspicious
- Hiring legal counsel for provisional drafting avoids these quality and fraud risks

## Best For

Inventors who have filed or are planning to file a provisional patent application themselves, and want to understand both the minimum legal requirements and the common quality mistakes that cost rights later.
