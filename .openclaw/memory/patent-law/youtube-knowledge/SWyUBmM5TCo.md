# Patent Filing fees

**Channel**: Innovent Law
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SWyUBmM5TCo
**Published**: 2019-03-20

## Overview

This video from Innovent Law explains the USPTO's three-tier fee structure for patent filings: large entities (full undiscounted fee), small entities (50% discount), and micro entities (75% discount). The fee structure is designed to make the patent system accessible to small businesses, independent inventors, and nonprofits, recognizing that it would be unfair to charge a solo inventor the same fees as a large corporation like Procter & Gamble or Boeing.

Small entity status applies to businesses with fewer than 500 employees, individual inventors who own the rights to their invention, and nonprofit organizations. Claiming small entity status requires only checking a box during filing — no separate certification form is needed. Micro entity status offers a deeper discount (paying 25% of large entity fees) but has stricter requirements: the applicant must qualify as a small entity AND either (1) have a gross income below $184,116 in the preceding tax year (with no named inventors on more than four prior applications, and no assignment to a non-micro entity) OR (2) be employed by or assign to a qualifying university or educational institution. All joint inventors must individually meet the micro entity requirements. Micro entity status requires filing a certification form, which the attorney or agent can sign on behalf of the inventors.

The video addresses a common question about the income threshold for married inventors: the $184,116 limit applies only to the individual inventor's income, not household income — though the word "household" does appear in the statute, the practical interpretation is individual income only. Each scenario (income qualification vs. institutional qualification) has slightly different certification requirements.

## Key Takeaways

- Three USPTO fee tiers: large entity (full fee), small entity (50% discount), micro entity (75% discount — pays 25% of full fee)
- Small entity: businesses under 500 employees, individual inventors, or nonprofit organizations — just check a box, no certification needed
- Micro entity income path: gross income below ~$184,116, not named on more than 4 prior applications, not assigning to non-micro entity
- Micro entity institutional path: employer or assignee is a qualifying U.S. university or educational institution
- The $184,116 income threshold applies to each individual inventor, not household income
- Micro entity status requires a certification form signed by the inventors or their representative
- All joint inventors must individually qualify for micro entity status — one non-qualifying inventor disqualifies the entire application

## Best For

Independent inventors and small businesses preparing to file a patent application who want to understand whether they qualify for small entity or micro entity fee discounts at the USPTO.
