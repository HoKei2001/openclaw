## Overview

This clip from the Patents Demystified channel covers the four main requirements for obtaining a patent: patentable subject matter, adequate description, novelty, and non-obviousness. The presenter explains each requirement concisely, with particular attention to the non-obviousness standard, which is described as the hardest hurdle to clear. The video also addresses common mistakes inventors make when drafting their own applications, specifically providing too little description.

## Key Takeaways

- There are four requirements to get a patent: patentable subject matter, adequate description, novelty (35 USC 102), and non-obviousness.
- Patentable subject matter includes physical, utilitarian things and methods — not books, logos, or slogans (which are covered by copyright and trademark).
- Adequate description is a common failure point: inventors who file their own applications often include only a single paragraph, which is insufficient to support the claims.
- The description must enable someone of ordinary skill in the art to understand how to make and use the invention.
- Novelty means no single prior art reference describes all the elements of your claimed invention.
- Non-obviousness is typically the hardest standard — examiners will combine multiple references to argue it would be obvious to combine known elements to reach your invention.
- Examiners frequently issue rejections; this is a normal part of the process and should be expected.
