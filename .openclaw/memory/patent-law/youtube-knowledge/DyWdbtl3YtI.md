## Overview

Patent attorney April Mosby presents the USPTO's recommended seven-step prior art search strategy, with her own enhancements based on practical experience. The video distinguishes between a federal patent search (covering only U.S. patents and published applications) and a comprehensive search (which also includes foreign patents and non-patent literature such as journals, books, and websites). The discussion uses a practical example — a bicycle-mounted sensor that automatically closes a garage door when the bike enters — to walk through each step concretely.

The seven steps are: (1) brainstorm keywords and concepts related to the invention's purpose, components, materials, and function; (2) identify relevant Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC) codes using the USPTO's CPC schema; (3) review the definition of the identified CPC code to find additional, more specific keywords; (4) retrieve patents matching the CPC code from the USPTO's public patent search database; (5) conduct an in-depth review of the most relevant patents found, focusing on claims (not just abstracts, as USPTO recommends); (6) repeat the CPC-based search in the patent applications database; and (7) broaden the search using keyword searches, U.S. Patent Classification codes, foreign patent databases (espacenet.com), and non-patent literature (Google, library resources, journals).

The video also notes that some patent attorneys prefer to let the USPTO examiner conduct the search rather than duplicating effort, but a pre-filing search is valuable for confirming patentability, finding design-around opportunities, and improving the strength of the final application.

## Key Takeaways

- A comprehensive prior art search covers U.S. patents, foreign patents, AND non-patent literature; a federal search covers only U.S. patents.
- The seven-step USPTO strategy: brainstorm keywords, find CPC codes, review CPC definitions, retrieve patents by CPC, analyze relevant patents, search patent applications database, then broaden with keywords and foreign sources.
- CPC codes are essential — they provide a structured way to find patents that share technical features with your invention.
- Review patent claims closely, not just abstracts; claims reveal what is actually protected and how closely it overlaps with your idea.
- Use espacenet.com for foreign patent searches using the same CPC codes.
- Expand to non-patent literature: scientific journals, product manuals, websites, and library databases.
- A pre-filing search helps confirm patentability, find improvement opportunities, and design around existing patents.
- Some attorneys skip the pre-filing search and rely on the examiner — a valid approach when time and budget are tight.

## Best For

This video is most useful for inventors who want to perform their own comprehensive prior art search following a structured, step-by-step methodology recommended by the USPTO.
