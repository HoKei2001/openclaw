## Overview

This video from StratJuris Partners provides an overview of the PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty) route for international patent filing. Presented in a seminar format with an Indian audience context, it explains how PCT works, the advantages of using it, key procedural steps, fees, and how applicants from India and other member countries can use the system to seek patent protection in over 150 countries through a single application.

## Key Takeaways

- The PCT system is administered by WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization) in Geneva, Switzerland, and currently has 153 member countries including India, the US, China, Japan, and many others.
- Filing a single PCT application effectively designates all member countries automatically, deferring the national phase entry decision for up to 30 months from the priority date.
- The PCT process includes an international search report and may include an international preliminary examination report, both of which help applicants assess patentability before committing to national phase costs in each country.
- Filing a PCT application does not automatically grant patents in member countries — applicants must still enter the national/regional phase in each target jurisdiction within the deadline.
- Significant fee reductions (up to 90%) are available for individual inventors filing PCT applications.
- The PCT search and examination phase gives applicants valuable time to assess market potential, secure funding, and decide which countries to pursue for national phase entry.
- Language of filing is generally English; translations are required when entering national phases in non-English speaking countries (e.g., China, Japan).
- India is an active PCT user and the Indian Patent Office is a recognized international searching authority.
