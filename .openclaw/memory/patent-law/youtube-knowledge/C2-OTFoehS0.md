## Overview

This video explains how to conduct a systematic prior art search using the USPTO's official patent databases to confirm the novelty of an invention before investing in a patent application. The video emphasizes that a thorough prior art search is essential because patents are only granted for inventions that are novel, useful, and non-obvious — and finding relevant prior art early can save significant time and money that would otherwise be wasted on a doomed application.

The recommended search process follows a clear sequence: begin by brainstorming keywords that describe the invention's function, components, and intended use; then identify relevant Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC) codes, which are a hierarchical classification system used by both the USPTO and the European Patent Office to categorize patents by technical feature. With those codes in hand, search the USPTO's Patent Full Text and Image Database (PFTID) using Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) combined with field-specific searches targeting the abstract, claims, or detailed description. The video highlights the CPC system as a particularly powerful search tool, noting that classification-based searches can surface highly relevant documents that keyword-only searches would miss.

The video also stresses the importance of expanding the prior art search beyond issued patents to include non-patent literature such as scientific papers, technical journals, and product manuals, all of which can constitute prior art and be used by examiners to reject a patent application.

## Key Takeaways

- Prior art search confirms novelty before filing and avoids wasting resources on an application likely to fail.
- Prior art includes existing patents, published applications, and any public disclosure (papers, manuals, websites).
- Start with keyword brainstorming covering function, components, and use of the invention.
- Identify relevant CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) codes using the USPTO's CPC scheme tool.
- Use the USPTO's PFTID database with Boolean operators and field searches (abstract, claims, description).
- The CPC classification system is a powerful search tool that can find relevant patents missed by keyword searches alone.
- Expand the search to non-patent literature: scientific papers, technical journals, and product manuals.
- Pay close attention to the claims and drawings of similar patents found during the search.
