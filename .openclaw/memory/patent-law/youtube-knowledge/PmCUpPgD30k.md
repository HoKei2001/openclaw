## Overview

This short informational video from Law School Prep Hub outlines the types of interview questions candidates can expect when applying for a patent examiner position at the USPTO. The role requires a unique combination of legal knowledge and technical expertise, making interview preparation more complex than typical government job applications. The video serves as a concise career guide for those considering or pursuing a patent examiner career.

The video covers six major question categories: understanding the responsibilities of a patent examiner (reviewing applications, conducting prior art searches, evaluating novelty), proficiency with prior art search databases and tools, legal and technical evaluation of non-obviousness standards, communication skills for handling disagreements with applicants, workload management and ability to prioritize multiple applications under deadlines, and awareness of patent law changes and ethical standards.

Candidates are also advised to prepare for scenario-based questions that test adaptability and analytical thinking — such as how they would approach reviewing a patent application outside their area of technical expertise. Successful candidates must be able to articulate how they apply legal principles to technical inventions, and reviewing the MPEP (Manual of Patent Examining Procedure) and key patent case law is strongly recommended.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent examiner interviews test both technical knowledge and legal understanding of patent law simultaneously.
- Expect questions about the duties of the role: reviewing applications, conducting prior art searches, and assessing novelty and non-obviousness.
- Be ready to explain the tools and databases used for prior art searches (USPTO database, Google Patents, etc.).
- Communication skills are critical — expect questions about handling disagreements with patent applicants professionally.
- Demonstrate ability to prioritize multiple applications and meet deadlines under a production quota system.
- Show awareness of how to stay current with changes in patent law and USPTO regulations.
- Prepare scenario-based answers about analyzing technical documents outside your area of expertise.
- Reviewing patent statutes, case law, and the MPEP is essential interview preparation.

## Best For

Aspiring patent examiners and law or engineering graduates who are preparing for job interviews at the USPTO or other national patent offices.
