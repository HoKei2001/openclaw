## Overview

This Trademark and Patent Law Experts video explains the provisional-to-non-provisional conversion process with an emphasis on the strategic purpose of the provisional application and the continuity requirements that determine whether the conversion successfully preserves the priority date. The video opens by articulating the value of the provisional application: it establishes an early filing date, grants patent-pending status without requiring formal claims or an oath, and buys time for invention development, market research, and the decision of whether to pursue full protection — all at a lower initial cost.

The key steps of conversion are covered in sequence. The inventor must thoroughly review the provisional application and current invention state to confirm that the non-provisional application's disclosure fully supports what was described in the provisional. A complete non-provisional application must then be prepared including a detailed written description, specific patent claims that define the invention's legal boundaries, necessary drawings, and an inventor oath or declaration. The application must be filed with the USPTO and fees paid before the 12-month deadline.

A strategic insight distinguishes this video from more basic overviews: if the invention has been significantly developed beyond the initial provisional disclosure, new subject matter exists that cannot claim the provisional's priority date. In this case, the inventor may need to consider a continuation-in-part (CIP) application that acknowledges the divide between what was disclosed in the provisional and what is newly claimed, thereby preserving the priority date for the original subject matter while establishing a new date for additions.

## Key Takeaways

- Provisional applications serve as affordable placeholders that establish an early priority date without requiring formal claims, enabling time for further development and market research
- The 12-month conversion deadline is firm — missing it means the provisional expires and the priority date advantage is lost
- The non-provisional application requires: detailed written description, patent claims, drawings if needed, and an inventor oath/declaration
- Continuity of disclosure is required — the non-provisional claims must be fully supported by what was described in the provisional filing
- New subject matter in the non-provisional that was not in the provisional cannot claim the earlier priority date
- If the invention has substantially evolved beyond the provisional disclosure, a continuation-in-part (CIP) application may be the appropriate path to preserve priority for original subject matter while adding new claims
- Strategic alignment between provisional disclosure and final non-provisional claims maximizes the protection benefit of the original filing date

## Best For

Inventors who have filed a provisional patent application and want to understand both the procedural steps and the strategic considerations — particularly around continuity of disclosure and continuation-in-part options — before filing their non-provisional application.
