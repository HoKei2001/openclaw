## Overview

This Michigan Engineering video provides a structured overview of the patent attorney selection process, beginning with a foundational clarification that is often overlooked: patent law is a specialty requiring not just a state bar license but also passage of a separate patent bar examination administered by the USPTO. This additional qualification filters the pool significantly, and even within that group, attorneys specialize further — some exclusively handle patent litigation, others focus on transactional matters like licensing and M&A, and others concentrate on patent prosecution (preparing and filing applications). The video advises that inventors filing for patents generally need someone experienced specifically in prosecution, not just IP law broadly.

The video then addresses technology specialization, noting that patent attorneys typically develop deep expertise in particular technology areas — a dynamic especially pronounced in life sciences and pharma, where attorneys often hold advanced technical degrees. This means matching an attorney's technical background to the invention's domain is a practical necessity, not merely a preference. The distinction between big-company and startup clients is also explored in depth: startup founders interact directly with attorneys (rather than through in-house counsel), patent applications may need to be written with investor readability in mind, and billing flexibility — capped fees, deferred compensation, or equity arrangements — may be necessary to serve cost-sensitive early-stage companies.

Practical vetting advice rounds out the video: ask for client referrals from people in similar situations, and request examples of previously filed or issued patents in the relevant technology area. These steps allow inventors to verify both technical expertise and communication effectiveness before committing.

## Key Takeaways

- Patent attorneys must pass a separate patent bar exam in addition to the state bar — this is a baseline qualification to verify before hiring
- Even within patent prosecution, attorneys specialize by technology area; life sciences and pharma attorneys often hold advanced technical degrees
- Startup clients require a different attorney profile than large corporations: direct founder communication, investor-readable applications, and flexible billing
- Billing flexibility for startups may include capped fees, deferred payment until Series A, or equity in exchange for legal services
- Requesting client referrals from people in similar situations is a reliable way to assess fit before committing
- Reviewing examples of issued patents in your technology area confirms the attorney's actual experience and writing style
- A startup-focused attorney needs to understand the full business picture, since founders lack in-house legal support to bridge communication gaps

## Best For

Inventors and startup founders who want a structured, educator-level explanation of how patent attorney specialization works and how to verify that an attorney is genuinely qualified for their specific situation.
