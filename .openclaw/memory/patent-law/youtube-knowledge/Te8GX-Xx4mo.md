# Errors You Can't Afford to Make When Licensing Your Invention

**Channel**: John Ferrell, Esq.
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Te8GX-Xx4mo
**Published**: 2022-08-11

---

## Overview

Silicon Valley patent attorney John Ferrell presents seven critical mistakes inventors make when licensing their inventions to manufacturers or distributors. The video is organized around real-world licensing negotiation scenarios, emphasizing that these are "rookie mistakes" that experienced inventors learn to avoid — and that knowing them in advance can protect the inventor's financial and legal interests significantly.

The seven mistakes are: (1) not knowing how much to charge for the license before entering negotiations — going into a meeting without a price is the single most avoidable error; (2) permanently assigning intellectual property rights instead of licensing them — if the manufacturer goes bankrupt, the rights are lost; (3) failing to set minimum sales or purchase requirements in the royalty agreement, allowing a licensee to sit on the rights without paying anything; (4) agreeing to indemnify the manufacturer for their own manufacturing and distribution mistakes; (5) not retaining ownership of improvements made to the invention under the license — inventors should keep all rights to improvements regardless of who suggests them; (6) failing to have a lawyer review the license agreement before signing, often because inventors download generic agreements that don't fit the actual situation; and (7) doing business with an untrustworthy counterparty — the presenter advises trusting one's gut when something feels wrong.

## Key Takeaways

- Always enter licensing negotiations with a defined price — inventors who don't know their price become part of someone else's agenda
- License intellectual property rights; never permanently assign them to a manufacturer or distributor
- Include minimum sales or purchase requirements in every royalty agreement — otherwise the licensee can sit on the rights
- Do not agree to indemnify the manufacturer for their manufacturing and distribution mistakes
- Retain ownership of all improvements to the invention, regardless of who suggests them, in the license agreement
- Always have a lawyer in your state review the license agreement before signing — generic internet templates often don't fit the situation
- Trust instinct when a counterparty feels untrustworthy — if it doesn't feel right, walk away

## Best For

Inventors and small businesses who are approaching licensing negotiations for the first time and want to understand the most costly legal and financial mistakes to avoid before signing a license agreement.
