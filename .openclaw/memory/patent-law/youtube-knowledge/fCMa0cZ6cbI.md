## Overview

This short video from The Patent Professor uses a concrete real-world example — the LoJack vehicle tracking device — to explain the practical distinction between patent and trademark protection and why inventors typically need both forms of IP to fully protect their business.

## Key Takeaways

- A patent protects the idea, function, and product itself — how something works — preventing competitors from copying the underlying invention.
- A trademark protects the brand name and logo — preventing competitors from calling their product by the same name or creating confusion in the marketplace.
- Having only a patent leaves a vulnerability: a competitor could create a differently-functioning product and still sell it under your brand name, confusing and diverting your customers.
- Having only a trademark leaves a vulnerability: a competitor could copy the patented technology under a different name.
- The LoJack example illustrates this: the patent protects the vehicle-tracking technology; the trademark protects the "LoJack" brand name.
- Inventors should prioritize securing a patent filing date first to establish an early priority date, then also pursue trademark protection.
- Full brand and invention protection requires both a patent and a trademark working together.

## Best For
