## Overview

This video by Dave Korpi provides a practical walkthrough of filing a provisional patent application, using real example applications as illustrations. The presenter emphasizes that a provisional application is a serious legal document used by major companies like Google, Apple, and Facebook to protect their engineers' innovations. A key advantage of provisionals is that they allow more descriptive content than non-provisional applications — inventors can describe their invention in multiple ways, using different terminology to ensure the concept is understood by various readers, from specialists to generalists.

The video demonstrates the importance of strong drawings in a provisional application, showing examples with numbered figures that clearly illustrate an invention from multiple views. The presenter walks through an actual filed provisional application, pointing out the acknowledgment sheet (which provides the EFS ID, application number, and filing date), the specification structure, and how claim-like language (rather than formal claims) is used in provisionals. A critical point is made: improperly drafted formal claims in a provisional can do more harm than good, so claim-like language is preferred.

The presenter also shows how a single provisional can contain multiple inventions simultaneously (a cost-saving strategy), and demonstrates the filing process through the USPTO's EFS Web system. The video cautions inventors against unscrupulous "invention houses" that charge large sums for services of questionable value, and stresses the importance of learning the basics before engaging any outside service.

## Key Takeaways

- A provisional patent application is a serious legal document, not just a quick placeholder
- Major companies require engineers to file provisionals to protect innovations quickly
- Describe the invention in multiple ways and using varied terminology to reach different readers and capture broader protection
- Good drawings with numbered reference labels are essential — describe each numbered component in the specification
- Provisional applications use claim-like language rather than formal patent claims
- Improperly drafted claims in a provisional can hurt more than help
- A single provisional can protect multiple inventions simultaneously to save money
- Inventors should be cautious of expensive "invention houses" that may not deliver value

## Best For

Hands-on inventors who learn by example and want to see what a real provisional patent application looks like, including how to structure drawings, specifications, and claim-like language.
