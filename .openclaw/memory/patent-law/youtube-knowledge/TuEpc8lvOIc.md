# How to File A Design Patent Application

**Channel**: J.D. Houvener / Bold Patents Law Firm
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TuEpc8lvOIc
**Published**: 2019-03-05

---

## Overview

Patent attorney J.D. Houvener of Bold Patents Law Firm explains the structure, requirements, and strategy for filing a design patent application with the USPTO. Unlike utility patents, which protect how an invention works, design patents protect only the ornamental appearance of a product — what it looks like, not what it does. Using Apple AirPods as an example (protected under design patent D801314), Houvener illustrates how design patents protect specific shapes, contours, and proportions.

The video walks through the key elements of a design patent application: a preamble identifying the inventor and providing a simple descriptive title focused on ornamental features (not functional ones), a brief written description, one claim (which is just "the ornamental design as shown and described"), and the drawings — which are the true heart of a design patent. Design patent drawings must show all surfaces of the product from multiple views, with proper shading to represent three-dimensional depth, and must use solid lines for claimed features and dashed lines for unclaimed features.

Houvener emphasizes that before filing, inventors should conduct a novelty search for similar ornamental designs and confirm the invention is not primarily functional (functional inventions belong under utility patents, not design patents). He also strongly recommends considering whether a utility patent application should be filed alongside the design patent to protect both the appearance and function of an innovative product. The entire application must be filed electronically through the USPTO filing system.

## Key Takeaways

- Design patents protect only the ornamental appearance of a product, not its function — use utility patents for functional protection.
- The sole claim in a design patent is: "The ornamental design as shown and described."
- Drawings are the heart of a design patent — they must show all views and surfaces with proper shading for three-dimensional representation.
- Solid lines in drawings indicate claimed features; dashed lines indicate unclaimed or environmental features.
- The title should describe the shape and ornamental attributes — no functional language.
- Conduct a novelty search before filing to confirm your ornamental design is new and not an obvious variation of existing designs.
- The functionality-versus-ornamentality distinction is important: primarily functional features cannot be protected by design patents.
- Consider filing both design and utility patent applications when an invention has both novel appearance and novel function.

## Best For

Inventors and product designers who want to understand the design patent application process and how to use solid and dashed line drawings strategically to maximize their ornamental protection.
