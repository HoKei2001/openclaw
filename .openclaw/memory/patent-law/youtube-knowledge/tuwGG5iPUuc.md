## Overview

This brief clip from Brian Rayve's "Patents for Inventors" question-and-answer series answers a single, focused question: what are patent maintenance fees? The video is Chapter 5 in a primer series designed for beginning inventors who need clear answers to the most commonly asked patent questions.

The answer is concise and direct: utility patents require maintenance fee payments at three intervals — 3.5 years, 7.5 years, and 11.5 years following the date the patent was issued — in order to keep the patent in force. If these payments are not made, the patent expires before the end of its 20-year term. Design patents, by contrast, do not require any maintenance fees; once granted, a design patent remains in force for its full term without additional payments.

## Key Takeaways

- Utility patents require maintenance fee payments at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after the patent grant date
- Failure to pay maintenance fees causes the patent to expire before its 20-year term
- Design patents do not require maintenance fees — a notable cost advantage over utility patents
- The three maintenance fee payments are the only post-grant fees required to maintain a utility patent
- Missing a maintenance fee deadline can result in patent abandonment and loss of patent rights
- Patent attorneys offer services to track and remind patent holders of upcoming maintenance fee deadlines
- Maintenance fees are separate from the fees paid during the application and examination process
