## Overview

This inventRight webinar features patent searching expert Patrick Walsh of Walsh IP teaching inventors how to conduct effective patent searches using free online tools, primarily Google Patents and USPTO databases. Hosts Andrew Krauss and Stephen Key frame the session with context on why patent searching matters for licensing and prosecution strategy before handing over to Walsh, who provides a live demonstration of professional search techniques, keyword development, class code navigation, and how to read patent documents.

## Key Takeaways

- Understanding your "point of difference" — how your invention differs from existing patents — is critical both for navigating USPTO examination (so you are prepared when the examiner cites prior art) and for licensing conversations (so you can confidently explain what makes your invention unique)
- Even if you plan to hire a professional patent searcher, doing your own preliminary search first makes the professional search significantly better because you can direct the searcher toward the relevant distinctions and prior art you already know about
- Google Patents is an excellent free starting point for patent searches — it allows natural language queries, has good filtering options, and links to the full USPTO database
- Professional patent searchers use a combination of keyword strategies, CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) and USPC class codes, forward/backward citation searching, and examination of related family members to build a comprehensive prior art picture
- Patent documents should be read strategically: start with the abstract and claims, then use the description and drawings to understand what the examiner and applicant were focused on during prosecution; the image file wrapper (prosecution history) reveals what arguments were made and what prior art was considered
- When a potential licensee asks "do you have IP?", having done a patent search allows you to answer confidently: "Yes, and here is exactly how mine is different from what already exists" — turning a potential objection into a selling point
- Filing a patent application without any search could mean waiting 2–5 years for an examiner to surprise you with prior art you could have found and designed around beforehand; early searching saves money and time

## Topics Covered

- Why patent searching matters for both prosecution strategy and licensing conversations
- Benefits of doing your own search before hiring a professional searcher
- Overview of free patent search resources: Google Patents, USPTO Patent Full-Text and Image Database (PatFT), US Patent Application Full-Text Database (AppFT), and Patent Center
- How professional patent searchers approach a new search: understanding the invention, developing keyword lists, identifying CPC/USPC class codes
- Live demonstration of Google Patents search techniques including natural language queries, date filtering, and classification browsing
- How to navigate the USPTO classification system (CPC codes) to find relevant art
- Reading a patent document: abstract, claims, description, drawings, prosecution history (image file wrapper in PAIR)
- Forward citation searching (finding patents that cited a known relevant patent)
- Backward citation searching (references listed in a patent's background section)
- When to hire a professional patent searcher and what to look for
- How patent searching informs licensing pitch conversations
- The risks of skipping a prior art search before filing

## Best For

Independent inventors preparing to file a patent application or license their invention, anyone who wants to learn professional patent search techniques using free online tools, and inventors who want to understand their competitive IP landscape before approaching potential licensees.
