# How to Draft a Patent Claim

## Overview

This brief tutorial from InnovAU explains the structure of a Type 1 patent claim as used in Australia's patent system. The presenter breaks a claim into its three required components: the preamble, the post-preamble transition, and the body. The preamble identifies the subject matter of the claim in as few words as possible, potentially including a field of use. The transition word (commonly "comprising" in Australia) links the preamble to the body and signals whether the claim is open-ended. The body defines the actual inventive concept through claim integers (elements) and the interrelationships between them.

The ideal patent claim is described as a "Goldilocks" or "just right" claim — broad enough to cover meaningful variations of the invention, but not so broad as to encompass prior art or fail examination, and not so narrow as to miss important coverage. The body should be kept as minimal as possible because every additional element or limitation narrows the scope of the claim. The presenter emphasizes that the interrelationships between integers (how elements connect, interact, and contribute to the invention) are the structural glue that holds the body together.

## Key Takeaways

- A Type 1 patent claim has three parts: preamble, post-preamble transition, and body
- The preamble identifies the subject of the claim in 1–2 words, plus optional field of use
- The transition word (commonly "comprising" in Australia) links the preamble to the body
- The body defines the inventive concept through integers (elements) and their interrelationships
- The ideal claim is a "Goldilocks" claim: not too broad, not too narrow, covering just the inventive concept
- Every added element in the body narrows the claim scope, so keep the body as minimal as possible
- Interrelationships between integers explain how the elements connect and interact within the invention
- The goal is a claim that covers the inventive concept and nothing else
