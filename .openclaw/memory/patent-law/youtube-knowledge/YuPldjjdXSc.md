## Overview

This lecture by Priggya Arora is designed for students preparing for the Indian Patent Agent Examination and covers the patentability criteria under the Indian Patents Act 1970. The four key criteria are derived from the statutory definition of "invention" under Section 2(1)(m): novelty (Section 2(1)(l) — the invention must not have been disclosed in any publication or used anywhere in the world before the filing date), inventive step (Section 2(1)(ja) — a feature that represents technical advance, economic significance, or both, making the invention non-obvious to a person skilled in the art), industrial applicability (Section 2(1)(ac) — the invention must be capable of being made or used in some industry), and subject matter eligibility (the invention must not fall under Sections 3 or 4).

The lecture goes through Sections 3A through 3P and Section 4 systematically, explaining which categories of inventions are explicitly excluded from patentability in India: inventions contrary to natural laws or frivolous (3A), immoral or contrary to public order (3B), mere discovery (3C), new use of a known substance (3D, with an enhanced efficacy exception for pharmaceuticals), mere mixture without synergistic effect (3E), mere arrangement or rearrangement (3F), methods of agriculture or horticulture (3H), medical treatment processes (3I), plants and animals except microorganisms (3J), computer programs per se/algorithms/mathematical methods/business methods (3K), literary and artistic works (3L), schemes and rules (3M), presentation of information (3N), topography of integrated circuits (3O), traditional knowledge (3P), and atomic energy inventions (Section 4).

The practical guidance offered is that exam questions typically come in two forms: objective questions testing which section applies to a specific scenario, and long-form questions presenting a situation requiring the student to analyze all four patentability criteria and reach a conclusion with section references.

## Key Takeaways

- Four patentability criteria in India: novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability, and subject matter eligibility
- Novelty is global — the invention must not have been disclosed or used anywhere in the world before the filing date
- Inventive step requires technical advancement or economic significance, or both, that makes the invention non-obvious
- Section 3K bars computer programs per se, algorithms, mathematical methods, and business methods from patentability
- Industrial applicability is rarely the issue in practice — if something is of no use, no one would file for it
- Traditional knowledge (Section 3P) is protected from false patents via India's Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)
- Atomic energy inventions (Section 4) cannot be patented for national security reasons
