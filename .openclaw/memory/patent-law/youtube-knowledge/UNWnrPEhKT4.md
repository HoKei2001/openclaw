## Overview

This UpCounsel video provides a comprehensive guide to converting a provisional patent application to a full non-provisional patent, including historical context, the mechanics of provisional applications, and the strategic considerations that affect the conversion decision. The video opens with the history of provisional applications (introduced in 1995) and explains how they changed the inventor experience by replacing the requirement to build and test an invention before filing with a simpler, affordable placeholder that establishes priority while the inventor continues development.

The provisional application is defined precisely: it requires only a specification (describing how the invention works), drawings clarifying the specification, and a cover sheet with payment and contact information — no formal patent claims needed. Advantages covered include early filing date establishment, lower cost, patent-pending status, and a one-year development window. Limitations are equally emphasized: the one-year pendency period cannot be extended under any circumstances, the provisional never becomes an enforceable patent on its own, and inadequate provisional disclosure can prevent the later non-provisional from claiming the earlier priority date.

A distinctive insight in this video is the effect of conversion method on patent term: filing a new non-provisional application that claims the provisional's priority date results in a 20-year patent term measured from the non-provisional filing date — effectively giving one additional year of protection compared to directly converting the provisional (which measures from the provisional date). The video also covers international considerations: a US provisional can serve as the priority application for a PCT filing, and inventors may consider filing multiple non-provisional applications tracing to the same provisional to cover different aspects of the invention.

## Key Takeaways

- Provisional applications have existed only since 1995; they require a specification, drawings, and a cover sheet but no formal patent claims
- The one-year pendency period is absolute and cannot be extended — missing it causes the provisional to expire and public disclosures during that period may become prior art
- There is no such thing as a "provisional patent" — only a provisional patent application, which never matures into a patent without conversion
- Filing a new non-provisional application claiming the provisional's priority date (rather than directly converting) gives one additional year of patent term protection
- The non-provisional must fully support the provisional's disclosure — new subject matter introduced in the non-provisional will not benefit from the earlier priority date
- A US provisional can serve as a priority application for an international PCT filing within the same 12-month window
- Multiple non-provisional applications can be filed from a single provisional, each targeting different aspects of the invention, all claiming the same early priority date
