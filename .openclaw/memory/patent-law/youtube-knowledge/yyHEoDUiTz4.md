## Overview

This video provides a clear, accessible breakdown of the three main types of intellectual property — patents, trademarks, and copyrights — explaining what each protects, how long protection lasts, and how they differ from one another. The presenter also illustrates how a single product or business can hold all three types of IP simultaneously, using Coca-Cola and the iPhone as examples.

## Key Takeaways

- Patents protect novel inventions with industrial utility (equipment, machinery, processes) and generally last 20 years from the filing date.
- Trademarks protect brand identifiers — names, logos, slogans, colors, sounds — that distinguish a product or service from competitors; they can be renewed indefinitely as long as they remain in use.
- Copyrights protect creative expressions such as film, literature, art, photography, and music; they last the life of the author plus 70 years and are registered with the Library of Congress.
- Trademarks and patents are administered by the USPTO, though they are entirely separate divisions; copyright is handled by the Copyright Office (part of the Library of Congress).
- A single business or product can involve all three types of IP simultaneously — e.g., Coca-Cola has registered trademarks (name, logo), copyrighted advertisements, and patents on its bottling processes.
- The ® symbol denotes registered trademarks; the © symbol denotes copyright; there is no equivalent symbol for patents, though "patent pending" or a patent number may appear in fine print.
- Intellectual property is best viewed as a portfolio of protections, not a single choice.
