# Should You File A Non-provisional Patent Application For Your Invention?

## Overview

This video explains when and why an inventor should file a non-provisional patent application, contrasting it with the provisional application and clarifying what the non-provisional actually accomplishes. While a provisional application establishes a priority date and lasts 12 months, only a non-provisional application undergoes USPTO examination and can result in an issued, enforceable patent. The non-provisional requires a detailed description, clearly defined claims, drawings where needed, and a declaration of truth.

Key strategic reasons to file a non-provisional are covered: it places the application directly into the USPTO examination queue, it can serve as the basis for international applications under the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) while maintaining the original filing date, and it provides up to 20 years of exclusive rights from the filing date if granted. The video also cautions that the process involves higher fees and greater complexity than a provisional, and that mistakes in claim drafting or invention description can weaken protection or cause rejection.

Common errors to avoid include filing the non-provisional before the invention is fully developed (which can lead to narrow or rejected claims), relying only on a provisional without converting it in time, and skipping the non-provisional altogether. The video recommends consulting a patent professional to ensure the application is complete and precisely drafted.

## Key Takeaways

- Only a non-provisional patent application is examined and can result in an issued, enforceable patent
- Unlike a provisional, which only secures a filing date for 12 months, a non-provisional undergoes full USPTO examination
- The examiner checks novelty, utility, and non-obviousness before allowing a patent
- A granted non-provisional patent provides exclusive rights for up to 20 years from the filing date
- A non-provisional can serve as a base for international PCT applications, preserving the original priority date
- Higher filing fees and greater complexity distinguish the non-provisional from the provisional
- Filing before the invention is fully developed risks rejection or narrow protection
- Consulting a patent professional helps avoid incomplete disclosures, missed deadlines, and weak claims

## Best For

Inventors who have already filed a provisional application or are ready to move to formal patent protection, and who want to understand what the non-provisional application process entails and when to pursue it.
