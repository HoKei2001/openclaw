# How to Find the Right Patent Attorney for Your Invention

**Channel**: inventRightTV
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DwrrUe4w_k
**Published**: 2016-12-09

---

## Overview

Stephen Key and patent attorney Damon C discuss how inventors should approach finding and evaluating a patent attorney, covering geographic flexibility, readability of written patents, specialization requirements, and firm size considerations. The conversation is candid and practical, with both participants drawing from personal experience as both inventors and legal professionals.

A key opening point is that geographic location does not matter for patent attorneys — since patents are federal law, a USPTO-registered attorney can represent clients anywhere in the US and its territories. The hosts then address a practical vetting technique: reading patents the attorney has written. If you cannot understand them, find someone else. The attorney acknowledges he committed to writing readable patents after falling asleep reading his first one, and explains why patent language appears dense (terms of art, repetition for consistency, claim structure).

Specialization is discussed with nuance: software, electronics, and chemistry/genetics require attorneys with specific expertise, while mechanical devices and consumer products are areas many competent attorneys can handle. On firm size, the recommendation for individual inventors is clear — solo practitioners or small boutique firms are almost always better fits than large firms. Large firms are built for big clients with complex litigation needs, and the attorney notes that at large firms, the actual work may not even be done by the named attorney. The personal relationship criterion is emphasized: if you cannot understand each other on the phone and feel rapport in the first call, move on — because the relationship typically spans five to six years per patent.

## Key Takeaways

- Geographic location does not matter for USPTO-registered patent attorneys; they operate at the federal level and can represent any US-based client
- Reading patents an attorney has written is a reliable vetting technique — if you cannot understand them, the attorney is not writing clearly enough
- Software, electronics, and chemistry/genetics require specialized attorneys; mechanical and consumer product inventions are more broadly accessible
- Solo practitioners and small boutique firms are generally better fits for individual inventors than large firms, which are built for corporate clients
- Large firms are expensive and may not have the named attorney doing the actual work on your application
- Rapport and communication ability in an initial phone call is the most important selection criterion — the relationship spans years
- Patent acquisition and patent enforcement are entirely separate areas of law; inventors unlikely to face litigation don't need a large litigation-capable firm

## Best For

Inventors choosing between attorney options who want a plain-language discussion of how specialization, firm size, and personal rapport should factor into their selection decision.
